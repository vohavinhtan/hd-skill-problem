## Steps

Step 1: Reduce the even quartic problem to a quadratic semi-infinite program

Put $t=x^2$ and write
$$
P(x)=q(t),\qquad q(t)=At^2+Bt+C.
$$
Then
$$
\int_{-1}^{1}(1+\lambda x^2)P(x)\,dx
=\int_0^1\frac{1+\lambda t}{\sqrt t}\,q(t)\,dt=:L_\lambda(q).
$$
The two constraints become
$$
q(t)\leq f(t):=\frac{1}{2-t},
\qquad
q(t)\leq g_r(t):=\frac{1}{1+r+(1-r)t}.
$$
They cross only at
$$
c_r=\frac{1-r}{2-r}.
$$
Since $r<-\frac{3}{5}$,
$$
c_r-\frac{3}{5}=\frac{-1-2r}{5(2-r)}>0.
$$

For $k=0,1,2$, set $m_k=L_\lambda(t^k)$. Direct integration gives
$$
m_0=\frac{2(\lambda+3)}{3},\qquad
m_1=\frac{2(3\lambda+5)}{15},\qquad
m_2=\frac{2(5\lambda+7)}{35}.
$$
We identify a quadratic with its coefficient vector and write
$$
e(t)=(1,t,t^2).
$$

Step 2: Recover the hidden interior contact from optimality

Let $q$ be an optimizer for a parameter $\lambda\in(-1,\frac{1}{2})$ whose second slack has a double zero at $t=1$. Let $A$ be the set of contact points with the active upper envelope $\min(f,g_r)$. The objective vector $(m_0,m_1,m_2)$ lies in the cone generated by $\{e(t):t\in A\}$. Indeed, otherwise a separating vector would give a quadratic perturbation that increases the objective and decreases it at every active constraint; compactness then makes a small positive perturbation feasible, contradicting optimality.

The polynomial
$$
1-\bigl(1+r+(1-r)t\bigr)q(t)
$$
has degree at most $3$, is nonnegative on $[c_r,1]$, and has a root of multiplicity at least $2$ at $t=1$. Hence it has no further contact in $(c_r,1)$. On $[0,c_r]$, every interior zero of
$$
1-(2-t)q(t)
$$
has even multiplicity, so there is at most one interior contact.

There must in fact be one such contact. If there were none, all contacts would lie in $\{0,c_r,1\}$. The sets $\{1\}$ and $\{0,1\}$ cannot generate the moment vector, because the latter would force $m_1=m_2$. The set $\{c_r,1\}$ would force
$$
c_r=\frac{m_1-m_2}{m_0-m_1}<\frac{2}{9},
$$
contrary to $c_r>\frac{3}{5}$. Finally, if $0,c_r,1$ were all contacts, the three values determine $q$; imposing the double contact at $1$ reduces to
$$
r^2-4r+5=0,
$$
which has no real solution.

Let $s\in(0,c_r)$ be the interior contact. It is the only additional contact. If $t=0$ were also a contact, then $q(0)=q(1)=\frac{1}{2}$, so
$$
q(t)=\frac{1}{2}+k t(1-t).
$$
The equations $q(s)=f(s)$ and $q'(s)=f'(s)$ give $s=0$ or $s=\frac{3}{2}$, impossible. If $c_r$ were also a contact, solving the tangency equations at $s$ together with $q(c_r)=f(c_r)$ and then imposing the value and derivative conditions at $1$ gives
$$
r=\frac{s^2-8s+10}{(s-2)^2}
$$
and
$$
\frac{4(s-1)(3s-5)}{(s-2)^2}=0,
$$
again impossible for $0<s<c_r<1$. Thus the contact set is exactly $\{s,1\}$.

Consequently
$$
(m_0,m_1,m_2)=\alpha e(s)+\beta e(1)
$$
for some $\alpha,\beta>0$. Eliminating the weights gives
$$
s=\frac{m_1-m_2}{m_0-m_1}
=\frac{3\lambda+7}{7(\lambda+5)}=:u.
$$
For $-1<\lambda<\frac{1}{2}$,
$$
\frac{1}{7}<u<\frac{17}{77}<\frac{2}{9}<c_r.
$$

Step 3: Construct and certify the optimizer at the transition

The interior contact forces tangency to $f$. Hence the conditions
$$
q(u)=f(u),\qquad q'(u)=f'(u),\qquad q(1)=\frac{1}{2}
$$
give the unique quadratic
$$
q_u(t)=\frac{1}{2-u}
+\frac{t-u}{(2-u)^2}
+\frac{u^2-2}{2(2-u)^2(1-u)^2}(t-u)^2.
$$
The second slack has a double zero at $t=1$ exactly when
$$
q_u'(1)=g_r'(1)=-\frac{1-r}{4}.
$$
Since
$$
q_u'(1)=\frac{u^2-u-1}{(2-u)^2(1-u)},
$$
this is equivalent to
$$
r=\frac{u(u^2-9u+12)}{(u-2)^2(u-1)}.
$$

It remains to show that this candidate really is feasible and optimal. For the value of $r$ just obtained,
$$
1-(2-t)q_u(t)
=
\frac{(t-u)^2\bigl(6-4u-t(2-u^2)\bigr)}
{2(2-u)^2(1-u)^2},
$$
while
$$
1-\bigl(1+r+(1-r)t\bigr)q_u(t)
=
\frac{(1-t)^2N_u(t)}
{(2-u)^4(1-u)^3},
$$
where
$$
N_u(t)
=
2t(u^2-2)(u^2-u-1)
-3u^5+5u^4+4u^3-16u+8.
$$
For $0<u<\frac{1}{4}$, the first remaining factor is at least $(2-u)^2>0$. The coefficient of $t$ in $N_u$ is positive, and its constant term satisfies
$$
-3u^5+5u^4+4u^3-16u+8
>8-4-\frac{3}{1024}>0.
$$
Thus both slacks are nonnegative on $[0,1]$.

Now define
$$
\alpha=\frac{m_0-m_1}{1-u},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{m_1-u m_0}{1-u}.
$$
Strict Cauchy-Schwarz for the positive weight $(1+\lambda t)/\sqrt t$ gives
$$
m_0m_2-m_1^2>0,
$$
so $\alpha,\beta>0$. The definition of $u$ yields
$$
\alpha+\beta=m_0,\qquad
\alpha u+\beta=m_1,\qquad
\alpha u^2+\beta=m_2.
$$
Therefore every quadratic $q$ satisfies
$$
L_\lambda(q)=\alpha q(u)+\beta q(1)
\leq\alpha f(u)+\frac{\beta}{2}
=L_\lambda(q_u).
$$
Hence $q_u$ is the unique optimizer and has the required double contact.

Step 4: Eliminate the contact point and prove uniqueness of the phase parameter

Substituting
$$
u=\frac{3\lambda+7}{7(\lambda+5)}
$$
into the relation between $r$ and $u$ gives
$$
r
=
-\frac{2(3\lambda+7)(51\lambda^2+567\lambda+1568)}
{(\lambda+7)(11\lambda+63)^2}
=:\rho(\lambda).
$$
Differentiation gives
$$
\rho'(\lambda)
=
-\frac{14\left(1203\lambda^3+20181\lambda^2+112161\lambda+206143\right)}
{(\lambda+7)^2(11\lambda+63)^3}.
$$
With $y=\lambda+1>0$, the cubic in the numerator is
$$
1203y^3+16572y^2+75408y+112960>0.
$$
Thus $\rho$ is strictly decreasing on $(-1,\frac{1}{2})$. Moreover,
$$
\rho(-1)=-\frac{263}{507}>-\frac{3}{5},
\qquad
\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)
=-\frac{253538}{281535}<-\frac{9}{10}.
$$
Hence every $r\in(-\frac{9}{10},-\frac{3}{5})$ has exactly one $\Lambda(r)\in(-1,\frac{1}{2})$ with $r=\rho(\Lambda(r))$. Steps 2 and 3 show that this is exactly the unique parameter with the required optimal double contact.

Clearing denominators with $z=\Lambda(r)$ gives
$$
r(z+7)(11z+63)^2
+2(3z+7)(51z^2+567z+1568)=0.
$$

Step 5: Normalize the polynomial relation

Set
$$
A(z)=(z+7)(11z+63)^2,
\qquad
B(z)=2(3z+7)(51z^2+567z+1568).
$$
The only roots of $A$ are $-7$ and $-\frac{63}{11}$, while
$$
B(-7)=-2744\neq0,
\qquad
B\left(-\frac{63}{11}\right)=\frac{175616}{1331}\neq0.
$$
Thus $\gcd(A,B)=1$ in $\mathbb Q[z]$. Hence $rA(z)+B(z)$ is primitive in $\mathbb Q[z][r]$ and irreducible in $\mathbb Q(z)[r]$ because it has degree $1$ in $r$. Gauss's lemma then gives irreducibility in $\mathbb Z[r,z]$. Its coefficient of $rz^3$ is $121>0$, fixing the sign.

Final Answer: $\boxed{r(z+7)(11z+63)^2+2(3z+7)(51z^2+567z+1568)}$

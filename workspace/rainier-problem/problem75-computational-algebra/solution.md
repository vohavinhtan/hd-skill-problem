## Steps

Step 1: Separate the three parameter strata
Put
$$
a=8n+4,\qquad D=72n+9,
$$
and define
$$
\rho=\lambda^2+\lambda+1,\qquad
r=\lambda^3-\lambda^2-\lambda-1.
$$
Set
$$
x=t^8,\qquad
R=t^{a+2}+\rho t^{a+3}.
$$
Then $\Psi_{n,\lambda}(Y)=t^a+R$. For the second generator, expanding the coefficient product gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
r\rho
&=(\lambda^3-\lambda^2-\lambda-1)(\lambda^2+\lambda+1)\\
&=\lambda^5+\lambda^4+\lambda^3-\lambda^4-\lambda^3-\lambda^2-\lambda^3-\lambda^2-\lambda-\lambda^2-\lambda-1\\
&=\lambda^5-\lambda^3-3\lambda^2-2\lambda-1.
\end{aligned}
$$
This gives
$$
\Psi_{n,\lambda}(Z)=t^a+rR,\qquad
\Psi_{n,\lambda}(Z)-\Psi_{n,\lambda}(Y)=(r-1)R.
$$
Also
$$
(\lambda-2)\rho=\lambda^3+\lambda^2+\lambda-2\lambda^2-2\lambda-2=\lambda^3-\lambda^2-\lambda-2=r-1.
$$

If $(\lambda-2)\rho\neq0$, the two displayed linear combinations recover $R$ and $t^a$, so
$$
A_{n,\lambda}=\mathbb C[[x,t^a,R,t^D]].
$$
At $\lambda=2$,
$$
\rho=7,\qquad r=8-4-2-1=1,
$$
so the two middle generators coincide and
$$
A_{n,2}=\mathbb C[[x,F,t^D]],\qquad
F=t^a+t^{a+2}+7t^{a+3}.
$$
If $\rho=0$, then
$$
\lambda^3-1=(\lambda-1)(\lambda^2+\lambda+1)=0,
$$
so $\lambda^3=1$, while $\lambda^2+\lambda=-1$. Therefore
$$
r=1-(\lambda^2+\lambda)-1=1,
$$
and
$$
A_{n,\lambda}=\mathbb C[[x,F_0,t^D]],\qquad
F_0=t^a+t^{a+2}.
$$
These three disjoint cases exhaust $\mathbb C^{\times}$.

Step 2: Compute the conductor on the generic stratum
We first record the conductor rule used below. If a numerical semigroup $S$ contains $m$ and $w_r$ is its least element congruent to $r$ modulo $m$, let $L=\max_r w_r$. Then $L-m\notin S$. If $z\geq L-m+1$ has residue $r$ and $z<w_r$, then $w_r-z\geq m$, so $z\leq L-m$, a contradiction. Therefore $z=w_r+qm\in S$, and
$$
c(S)=L-m+1.
$$

Assume $(\lambda-2)\rho\neq0$. The first unresolved cancellation is forced by
$$
2(a+2)=a+8(n+1).
$$
The equality says that $R^2$ and $x^{n+1}t^a$ have the same leading monomial. Expanding their difference gives
$$
R^2-x^{n+1}t^a=2\rho\,t^{2a+5}+\rho^2t^{2a+6}.
$$
Since $\rho\neq0$, division by the first surviving coefficient normalizes the new element:
$$
H=\frac{R^2-x^{n+1}t^a}{2\rho}=t^c+\frac{\rho}{2}t^{c+1},\qquad c=2a+5.
$$

For the valuation semigroup generated by $8,a,a+2,c$, substitution of $a=8n+4$ and $c=2a+5$ gives
$$
2a=16n+8=8(2n+1),
$$
$$
2(a+2)=16n+12=a+8(n+1),
$$
and
$$
2c=32n+26=a+(a+2)+8(2n+2).
$$
First reduce the coefficient of $c$ modulo $2$, then that of $a+2$, then that of $a$. Every semigroup element becomes
$$
\varepsilon a+\eta(a+2)+\theta c+8q,\qquad
\varepsilon,\eta,\theta\in\{0,1\},\quad q\geq0.
$$
The eight binary sums have residues
$$
0,\ 4,\ 6,\ 2,\ 5,\ 1,\ 3,\ 7
$$
modulo $8$, so they are exactly the least residue representatives. Their maximum is
$$
a+(a+2)+c=4a+7=32n+23.
$$
The conductor rule gives
$$
c_{\mathrm{gen}}=32n+23-8+1=32n+16.
$$

No extra valuation below $4a+7$ comes from cancellation. The relations
$$
(t^a)^2=x^{2n+1},\qquad
R^2=x^{n+1}t^a+2\rho H
$$
reduce powers of $t^a$ and $R$, while $2c=4a+10>4a+7$ excludes $H^2$. Every element below that level is a $\mathbb C[[x]]$-linear combination of the eight binary monomials. For a fixed binary monomial, its coefficient has a unique lowest power of $x$; different binary monomials have different leading residues modulo $8$. The least term therefore cannot cancel. In particular,
$$
c_{\mathrm{gen}}-1=(4a+7)-8
$$
is not a ring valuation.

For every $e\geq c_{\mathrm{gen}}$, choose the binary representative $w_r$ of its residue and write $e=w_r+8q$. After rescaling, the corresponding product has the form $t^e+\sum_{j>e}a_jt^j$. Cancel the first nonzero remaining coefficient with the analogous product for its exponent and repeat. The correction exponents tend to infinity, and their powers of $x$ tend to infinity, so the corrections form a convergent formal series in the source variables. This produces $t^e$ in the ring. Since
$$
D-c_{\mathrm{gen}}=40n-7>0,
$$
we also have $t^D\in\mathbb C[[x,t^a,R]]$, so $W$ is redundant and
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=32n+16
\qquad((\lambda-2)\rho\neq0).
$$

Step 3: Compute the isolated singular conductor at $\lambda=2$
Here
$$
F=t^a(1+t^2+7t^3).
$$
Because $2a=8(2n+1)$, the square $F^2$ and $x^{2n+1}$ have the same leading monomial. Their difference is
$$
F^2-x^{2n+1}=2t^{2a+2}+14t^{2a+3}+t^{2a+4}+14t^{2a+5}+49t^{2a+6}.
$$
The first surviving coefficient is $2$, so normalization forces
$$
H=\frac{F^2-x^{2n+1}}{2}=t^h\left(1+7t+\frac12t^2+7t^3+\frac{49}{2}t^4\right),\qquad h=2a+2.
$$

Now
$$
2h=a+8(3n+2),
$$
so $H^2$ and $x^{3n+2}F$ have the same leading monomial. The coefficient one degree later in $H^2$ is $2\cdot7=14$, whereas $x^{3n+2}F$ has no term one degree later. Their difference therefore has initial term $14t^{2h+1}$. Dividing by this forced leading coefficient gives
$$
K=\frac{H^2-x^{3n+2}F}{14},
$$
so $K$ has valuation
$$
k=2h+1=4a+5.
$$

Substituting $a=8n+4$, $h=2a+2$, and $k=4a+5$ gives
$$
2a=16n+8=8(2n+1),
$$
$$
2h=32n+20=a+8(3n+2),
$$
and
$$
2k=64n+42=h+8(6n+4).
$$
These identities allow reduction first in the coefficient of $k$, then $h$, then $a$. Every element of $\langle8,a,h,k\rangle$ becomes
$$
\varepsilon a+\eta h+\theta k+8q,\qquad
\varepsilon,\eta,\theta\in\{0,1\},\quad q\geq0.
$$
The eight binary sums have distinct residues modulo $8$, and their maximum is
$$
a+h+k=7a+7=56n+35.
$$
By the conductor rule from Step 2,
$$
c_2=56n+35-8+1=56n+28.
$$

The exact relations
$$
F^2=x^{2n+1}+2H,\qquad
H^2=x^{3n+2}F+14K
$$
reduce powers of $F,H$, and $2k=8a+10>7a+7$ excludes $K^2$ below the largest representative. The same unique-lowest-residue argument from Step 2 rules out further cancellation there. In particular,
$$
c_2-1=(7a+7)-8
$$
is not a ring valuation. The lifting argument from Step 2 gives every $t^e$ for $e\geq c_2$. Since
$$
D-c_2=16n-19>0
$$
for $n\geq2$, $t^D$ already lies in $\mathbb C[[x,F]]$. So
$$
\kappa_{n,2}=56n+28.
$$

Step 4: Compute the cyclotomic stratum and compare the three values
Assume $\rho=0$. Put
$$
u=t^2,\qquad M=4n+2,\qquad f=u^M+u^{M+1},
$$
so
$$
B=\mathbb C[[u^4,f]]
$$
is the even subring generated by $t^8$ and $F_0$.

Because $2M=4(2n+1)$, the leading monomial of $f^2$ already lies in $\mathbb C[[u^4]]$. Subtracting it exposes
$$
f^2-u^{4(2n+1)}=2u^{2M+1}+u^{2M+2}.
$$
The first surviving coefficient is $2$, so normalization gives
$$
J=\frac{f^2-u^{4(2n+1)}}{2}=u^j+\frac12u^{j+1},\qquad j=2M+1.
$$
Since $M=4n+2$ and $j=2M+1$,
$$
2M=8n+4=4(2n+1),
$$
and
$$
2j=16n+10=M+4(3n+2).
$$
These identities reduce first the coefficient of $j$ and then that of $M$. The four least residue candidates are
$$
0,\qquad M,\qquad j,\qquad M+j,
$$
with residues $0,2,1,3$ modulo $4$. Since $2j>M+j$, $J^2$ cannot affect a value below the maximum, and
$$
f^2=u^{4(2n+1)}+2J
$$
reduces every square of $f$. The four canonical terms have distinct leading residues, so the least term cannot cancel. By the conductor rule from Step 2,
$$
\delta=M+j-4+1=12n+4.
$$
The same lifting argument shows that $u^r\in B$ for every $r\geq\delta$. Also
$$
\delta-1=(M+j)-4
$$
has the residue of the largest representative but is smaller by $4$; the canonical-form argument excludes cancellation into that valuation. Therefore $u^{\delta-1}\notin B$.

Now
$$
D-\delta=60n+5>0,
$$
so $u^D\in B$ and $(t^D)^2=u^D$. Since $t^D$ satisfies a quadratic relation over $B$, every element of
$$
A_{n,\lambda}=B[[t^D]]
$$
reduces to $b_0+t^Db_1$ with $b_0,b_1\in B$; the form is unique because the two summands contain only even and odd powers of $t$, respectively. Every even monomial of degree at least $2\delta$ lies in $B$, and every odd monomial of degree at least $D+2\delta$ lies in $t^DB$. The first degree from which both parities are always present is
$$
D+2\delta-1=96n+16.
$$
The preceding odd monomial is
$$
t^{D+2(\delta-1)}=t^{96n+15}.
$$
If it belonged to $A_{n,\lambda}$, uniqueness of the parity decomposition would force $u^{\delta-1}\in B$, which is false. Therefore
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=96n+16
\qquad(\rho=0).
$$

Finally,
$$
(96n+16)-(56n+28)=40n-12>0,\qquad
(56n+28)-(32n+16)=24n+12>0
$$
for $n\geq2$. The largest conductor occurs on $\rho=0$, and the second-largest distinct conductor occurs only at $\lambda=2$.

Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(56n+28,\{2\}\right)}$

## Steps

Step 1: Reduce the conductor to residue-class valuation gaps
Put $u=t^n$ and $s=t$, so $s^n=u$, and write
$$
x=u^4,\qquad
y=u^6+u^7+u^7s,\qquad
z=u^9+\left(\lambda^2u^9+(\lambda-1)u^{11}\right)s.
$$
Let
$$
S_{n,\lambda}=\{v(f):0\neq f\in A_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Since $x=t^{4n}$, the ring $\mathbb C[[t]]$ is finite over $\mathbb C[[x]]$. Hence $y,z$ are integral over $\mathbb C[[x]]$, and the image $A_{n,\lambda}$ is a finite complete $\mathbb C[[x]]$-module. If every integer at least $c$ belongs to $S_{n,\lambda}$, choose for each residue $\rho$ modulo $4n$ an element
$$
b_\rho=t^{m_\rho}+\text{ higher terms}\in A_{n,\lambda}
$$
with $m_\rho\geq c$ minimal in that residue. For $k=m_\rho+4nj$, subtracting a suitable multiple of $x^{j}b_\rho$ from $t^k$ raises the valuation. Repetition converges in the complete module, so $t^k\in A_{n,\lambda}$. Therefore
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max\left(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus S_{n,\lambda}\right).
$$

For $0\leq r<n$, put
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=\{q\geq0:qn+r\in S_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Set
$$
H=\langle4,6,9\rangle,\qquad K=H\cup(5+H),\qquad L=K\cup(7+H).
$$
The gaps of $H,K,L$ are respectively
$$
\{1,2,3,5,7,11\},\qquad \{1,2,3,7\},\qquad \{1,2,3\},
$$
so their largest gaps are $11,7,3$.

Step 2: Derive the residue layers by complete standard-basis induction
Let
$$
B=\mathbb C[[u^4,u^6,u^9]],\qquad I=B+u^5B.
$$
We first determine all relations between the two generators of $I$. If $p,q\in B$ and
$$
p+u^5q=0,
$$
then $p\in B\cap u^5B$. Since the monomials occurring in $B$ have exponents in $H$,
$$
B\cap u^5B=u^9B+u^{14}B
$$
because
$$
H\cap(5+H)=(9+H)\cup(14+H).
$$
Thus $p=u^9a+u^{14}b$ for some $a,b\in B$, and then
$$
q=-u^4a-u^9b.
$$
Hence every relation is a $B$-linear combination of
$$
(u^9,-u^4),\qquad(u^{14},-u^9).
$$
If a combination of two row generators cancels its leading row, subtracting the corresponding combinations of these two relations raises the leading $u$-weight. Iterating this reduction is exhaustive because $B$ is complete.

The relations among the base weights $4,6,9$ are generated by
$$
Y^2-X^3,\qquad Z^2-X^3Y,
$$
since after reducing the exponents of $Y,Z$ to $0$ or $1$, the remaining weights $0,6,9,15$ are distinct modulo $4$. Put
$$
U=y^2-x^3-2xz-x^2y,\qquad G=z^2-x^3y.
$$
Their exact expansions are
$$
U=-u^{15}+\left(2(1-\lambda^2)u^{13}+2u^{14}+(1-2\lambda)u^{15}\right)s+u^{14}s^2
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
G&=-u^{19}+\left(2\lambda^2u^{18}-u^{19}+2(\lambda-1)u^{20}\right)s\\
&+\left(\lambda^4u^{18}+2\lambda^2(\lambda-1)u^{20}+(\lambda-1)^2u^{22}\right)s^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Assume first that $D=\lambda^2\neq1$. Normalize
$$
F_1=\frac{U}{2(1-D)},\qquad G_1=\frac{G}{2D}.
$$
Then their leading monomials are $u^{13}s,u^{18}s$. Write
$$
a_r=7r+7+(-1)^{r}.
$$
For each row, reduce arbitrary generators by all earlier rows and rescale them so that
$$
\operatorname{in}(F_r)=u^{a_r}s^r,\qquad
\operatorname{in}(G_r)=u^{a_r+5}s^r.
$$
Any other generating pair with these leading monomials differs by row operations over $B$, so the preceding syzygy calculation reduces every possible same-row cancellation to the two primitive ones below.

Define
$$
A_r=[u^{a_r+1}s^r]F_r,\quad
B_r=[u^{a_r+1}s^{r+1}]F_r,\quad
C_r=[u^{a_r+5}s^{r+1}]G_r.
$$
After removing terms already divisible by the two row initials, the induction statement is
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
& A_r&B_r&C_r\\ \hline
r\text{ odd}&-\dfrac{r}{D-1}&-\dfrac{r}{(r+1)(D-1)}&\dfrac{D}{r+1}\\
r\text{ even}&-\dfrac{rD+1}{D(D-1)}&-\dfrac{1}{D-1}&\dfrac{D}{r+1}.
\end{array}
$$
For $r=1$ these values follow by reading the displayed formulas for $F_1,G_1$.

Let $r$ be odd. The two general syzygies above lift to
$$
zF_r-xG_r,\qquad zG_r-x^2yF_r.
$$
Their row-$r$ terms cancel. In row $r+1$ the first uncancelled coefficients are
$$
D-C_r=\frac{Dr}{r+1},\qquad D+C_r=\frac{D(r+2)}{r+1},
$$
at weights $a_r+9$ and $a_r+14$. Both are nonzero, so they give the two generators of the next row. For the first generator, the next same-column coefficients are $B_r+DA_r$ and $DB_r$; after division by $Dr/(r+1)$ they become
$$
A_{r+1}=-\frac{(r+1)D+1}{D(D-1)},\qquad B_{r+1}=-\frac{1}{D-1}.
$$
For the second generator, division of $DC_r$ by $D(r+2)/(r+1)$ gives
$$
C_{r+1}=\frac{D}{r+2}.
$$
Thus the even line follows, and the row start changes by $+9$.

Now let $r\geq2$ be even. Since $I^2=I$ because $u^{10}B\subset B$, multiplication of row $1$ by row $r-1$ again has two leading generators. Its leading module equals that of $xF_r,xG_r$, because $a_1+a_{r-1}=a_r+4$. Hence every adjacent-row cancellation reduces to
$$
F_1F_{r-1}-xF_r,\qquad F_1G_{r-1}-xG_r,
$$
up to the same two internal syzygies of $I$. In the first pair, after the leading term cancels, the coefficient in the $G_r$ column is
$$
A_1+A_{r-1}-A_r=\frac{1}{D(D-1)}.
$$
Reducing that column leaves in row $r+1$
$$
B_1+B_{r-1}-B_r-\frac{C_r}{D(D-1)}
=-\frac{(r-1)(r+2)}{2r(r+1)(D-1)}\neq0.
$$
For the second pair,
$$
C_{r-1}-C_r=\frac{D}{r(r+1)},
$$
and after reducing by the first new generator the remaining coefficient is
$$
-\frac{1}{2(D-1)}\neq0.
$$
These give weights $a_r+5,a_r+10$. Normalizing them gives
$$
A_{r+1}=-\frac{r+1}{D-1},\quad
B_{r+1}=-\frac{r+1}{(r+2)(D-1)},\quad
C_{r+1}=\frac{D}{r+2},
$$
which is the odd line with $r$ replaced by $r+1$. Thus the row start changes by $+5$. We have proved
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=7r+7+(-1)^{r}+K
$$
for every positive row before wrapping.

For $\lambda=-1$, take
$$
F_1=\frac{U}{2},\qquad G_1=-\frac{G-xU}{4},
$$
and put $a_r=7r+8+(-1)^{r}$. After row reduction the induction invariant is simpler: $F_r$ has coefficient $1/(r+1)$ at $u^{a_r}s^{r+1}$, while $G_r$ has coefficient $1$ at both $u^{a_r+6}s^r$ and $u^{a_r+6}s^{r+1}$ and no term $u^{a_r+5}s^{r+1}$. For odd $r$, the same two primitive syzygies leave
$$
\frac{r+2}{r+1},\qquad\frac{r}{r+1},
$$
so the next row starts at $a_r+9$. For even $r$, $I^2=I$ reduces all adjacent relations to
$$
F_1F_{r-1}-G_r,\qquad F_1G_{r-1}-xyF_r,
$$
whose first new coefficients are
$$
\frac{r+2}{2r},\qquad\frac{r-1}{2(r+1)}.
$$
They are nonzero and normalization restores the same invariant with $r$ replaced by $r+1$. Hence
$$
Q_r(-1)=7r+8+(-1)^{r}+K
$$
before wrapping.

No omitted relation can create a third generator. A relation inside one row reduces by the proved syzygy description of $I$. A relation between adjacent rows reduces by the displayed pair because $I^2=I$. If two participating row indices differ by at least two, use the already constructed adjacent relation for the larger index; since $a_{r+2}=a_r+14$ and $14\in H$, this replaces its leading term by one with smaller row separation. Induction on that separation ends in one of the two cases already handled.

It remains to include $s^n=u$. The same division is continued with every occurrence of $s^n$ immediately replaced by $u$. If $n$ is even, the wrapped terms reduce to $H$-multiples of the two existing generators in the last row, so the last row is unchanged. If $n$ is odd, the last row $r=n-1$ is even and comes from the preceding odd-row reduction. Its next $s$-coefficients are precisely the ones in the even induction line above. After $s^n=u$, reducing their row-zero parts through $B$ and then through rows $1,\ldots,n-2$ leaves one new nonzero $B$-translate at offset $7$ from the first last-row generator. Every other remainder is an $H$-multiple of the existing $K$-generators by the two syzygies already proved. Thus
$$
Q_{n-1}=
\begin{cases}
(7n+1)+L,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
(7n+2)+L,&\lambda=-1.
\end{cases}
$$
This is the complete wrap: the extra translate is $7+H$, and no other new translate is possible.

For $\lambda=1$, $G=xU$. With $F=U/2$, the initial algebra before wrapping is generated by
$$
u^4,\qquad u^6,\qquad u^9,\qquad u^{14}s.
$$
Any relation with positive $s$-degree has the same power of $u^{14}s$ on both sides; after factoring it, the remaining relation is one of the two base relations already treated. Hence
$$
Q_r(1)=14r+H.
$$
At the wrap, $(u^{14}s)^n=u^{14n+1}$ and $14n+1>11$, so this exponent already belongs to $H$ and no new generator appears.

Step 3: Compute the nonunit conductor levels
For each row let
$$
g_r=\max(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus Q_r),\qquad m_r=r+ng_r.
$$
Then $\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max_r m_r$. For $\lambda^2\neq1$ the nonfinal rows have
$$
g_r=7r+13\quad(r\text{ odd}),\qquad g_r=7r+15\quad(r\text{ even}),
$$
and for $\lambda=-1$ these values are larger by $1$. The differences $m_{r+1}-m_r$ alternate between $1+9n$ and $1+5n$, so they are positive. When $n$ is odd, replacing $K$ by $L$ in the last row lowers its quotient gap by $4$, and the final difference remains $1+5n$. Thus the last row is maximal. Consequently
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=7n^2+\left(6+(-1)^{n}\right)n\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1),
$$
and
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n.
$$

Step 4: Compute the $\lambda=1$ level and compare
For $\lambda=1$, the largest missing quotient in row $r$ is $14r+11$, and successive row maxima differ by $1+14n$. Hence
$$
\kappa_{n,1}=1+(n-1)+n\left(14(n-1)+11\right)=14n^2-2n.
$$
For every $n\geq2$,
$$
\kappa_{n,1}-\kappa_{n,-1}=n\left(7n-9-(-1)^{n}\right)>0,
$$
while
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}-\kappa_{n,\lambda}=n>0\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1).
$$

Step 5: Check the smallest case and identify the second extremum
When $n=2$ there is no row $Q_2$. Directly from the first-row calculation,
$$
Q_1=13+K,\qquad14+K,\qquad14+H
$$
for $\lambda^2\neq1$, $\lambda=-1$, and $\lambda=1$, respectively. Their largest missing quotients are $20,21,25$, giving conductors $42,44,52$, exactly as the formulas above predict.

The equation $\lambda^2=1$ has only $1$ and $-1$. Thus $\lambda=1$ gives the unique largest conductor and $\lambda=-1$ gives the unique second-largest one. Hence
$$
\beta_n=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n,\qquad E_n=\{-1\}.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n,\{-1\}\right)}$

## Steps

Step 1: Convert the conductor problem to a valuation problem
Put $u=t^n$ and $s=t$, so $s^n=u$. Write
$$
x=u^4,\qquad
y=u^6+u^7+u^7s,\qquad
z=u^9+\left(\lambda^2u^9+(\lambda-1)u^{11}\right)s.
$$
Let
$$
S_{n,\lambda}=\{v(f):0\neq f\in A_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
The ring $\mathbb C[[t]]$ is finite over $\mathbb C[[x]]=\mathbb C[[t^{4n}]]$, hence $y$ and $z$ are integral over $\mathbb C[[x]]$ and $A_{n,\lambda}$ is a finite, therefore complete, $\mathbb C[[x]]$-module. If all integers at least $c$ lie in $S_{n,\lambda}$, choose for every $k\geq c$ an element $f_k=t^k+$ higher terms. Successively subtracting multiples of the $f_k$ expresses each $t^k$ as a $t$-adic limit of elements of $A_{n,\lambda}$, hence as an element of $A_{n,\lambda}$. The converse is immediate. Thus $\kappa_{n,\lambda}$ is one plus the largest integer missing from $S_{n,\lambda}$.

For $0\leq r<n$, define
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=\{q\geq0:qn+r\in S_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Put
$$
H=\langle4,6,9\rangle,\qquad
K=H\cup(5+H),\qquad
L=K\cup(7+H).
$$
Directly,
$$
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus H=\{1,2,3,5,7,11\},
$$
$$
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus K=\{1,2,3,7\},
\qquad
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus L=\{1,2,3\}.
$$
Hence the largest gaps of $H,K,L$ are $11,7,3$.

Step 2: Determine the residue ideals by explicit cancellations
The initial $u$-weights of $x,y,z$ are $4,6,9$. Every equality between monomials of these weights is generated by
$$
Y^2-X^3,\qquad Z^2-X^3Y.
$$
Indeed, these two relations reduce the exponents of $Y,Z$ to $0$ or $1$, and the four remaining weights $0,6,9,15$ are distinct modulo $4$. Therefore every cancellation of a lowest $u$-weight reduces through these two relations.

Set
$$
U=y^2-x^3-2xz-x^2y,\qquad G=z^2-x^3y.
$$
Exact expansion gives
$$
U=-u^{15}
+\left(2(1-\lambda^2)u^{13}+2u^{14}+(1-2\lambda)u^{15}\right)s
+u^{14}s^2
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
G&=-u^{19}
+\left(2\lambda^2u^{18}-u^{19}+2(\lambda-1)u^{20}\right)s\\
&+\left(\lambda^4u^{18}
+2\lambda^2(\lambda-1)u^{20}
+(\lambda-1)^2u^{22}\right)s^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

If $\lambda^2\neq1$, the first residue has generators $13+H$ and $18+H$. The two reduced critical pairs
$$
zU-\frac{1-\lambda^2}{\lambda^2}xG,
\qquad
zG-\frac{\lambda^2}{1-\lambda^2}x^2yU
$$
start with
$$
\lambda^2(1-\lambda^2)u^{22}s^2,
\qquad
3\lambda^4u^{27}s^2,
$$
so
$$
Q_1=13+K,\qquad Q_2=22+K.
$$

If $\lambda=-1$, then $v(U)=14n+1$ and
$$
V=G-xU=-4(u^{19}+u^{20})s+4(u^{22}-u^{20})s^2.
$$
Thus the first residue has generators $14+H$ and $19+H$. Moreover
$$
zU+\frac12xV,\qquad zV+2x^2yU
$$
start with
$$
3u^{23}s^2,\qquad -2u^{28}s^2,
$$
so
$$
Q_1=14+K,\qquad Q_2=23+K.
$$

The same reduction now repeats every two residue degrees. If a reduced row has two initial coefficient ideals $a+H$ and $a+5+H$, the two critical pairs obtained by multiplying them by $z$ and $x$, and by $z$ and $x^2y$, first cancel at equal $u$-weights and expose the next row at $a+9$ and $a+14$. Eliminating that row's lowest coefficients exposes the following row at $a+14$ and $a+19$. The coefficient responsible for this two-step shift is the displayed $u^{14}s^2$ term of $U$. Since every possible lowest-weight cancellation is generated by the two relations already listed, this exhausts the reductions. The residue-zero row is the original semigroup row
$$
Q_0=H.
$$
For the positive rows before the final use of $s^n=u$, the repeating formulas are
$$
Q_{2j+1}=
\begin{cases}
14j+13+K,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
14j+14+K,&\lambda=-1,
\end{cases}
\qquad(j\geq0),
$$
and
$$
Q_{2j}=
\begin{cases}
14j+8+K,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
14j+9+K,&\lambda=-1,
\end{cases}
\qquad(j\geq1).
$$

If $n$ is odd, the last row $r=n-1$ is even. In the final critical pair, its next $s$-term is an $s^n$-term; replacing $s^n$ by $u$ adds exactly the translate $7+H$. Hence only in this last row the set $K$ is replaced by $L$. If $n$ is even, the last row is odd and the wrapped translate is already contained in $K$.

For $\lambda=1$ there is the exact identity $G=xU$, so only one independent first-residue row survives. Repeated reduction then gives
$$
Q_r(1)=14r+H\qquad(0\leq r<n).
$$

Step 3: Compute the conductor for lambda equal to minus one
Suppose first that $n=2m$ is even. The last residue is $r=n-1=2m-1$, and
$$
Q_{n-1}(-1)=7n+K.
$$
Its largest missing quotient is $7n+7$. Hence the largest missing integer is
$$
(n-1)+n(7n+7)=7n^2+8n-1,
$$
so
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}=7n^2+8n.
$$

Now let $n=2m+1$ be odd. The boundary correction gives
$$
Q_{n-1}(-1)=(7n+2)+L.
$$
Its largest missing quotient is $7n+5$, hence the largest missing integer is
$$
(n-1)+n(7n+5)=7n^2+6n-1.
$$
Therefore
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}=7n^2+6n.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n.
$$

Step 4: Compute the other two conductor levels
For $\lambda^2\neq1$, the same residue table is shifted down by one. If $n$ is even, the last row is $(7n-1)+K$; if $n$ is odd, it is $(7n+1)+L$. Therefore
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}
=
7n^2+\left(6+(-1)^n\right)n
\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1).
$$
In particular
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}-\kappa_{n,\lambda}=n>0.
$$

For $\lambda=1$,
$$
Q_{n-1}(1)=14(n-1)+H.
$$
Since the largest gap of $H$ is $11$, the largest missing integer is
$$
(n-1)+n\left(14(n-1)+11\right)
=14n^2-2n-1.
$$
Thus
$$
\kappa_{n,1}=14n^2-2n.
$$
For even $n$,
$$
\kappa_{n,1}-\kappa_{n,-1}=n(7n-10)>0,
$$
while for odd $n$,
$$
\kappa_{n,1}-\kappa_{n,-1}=n(7n-8)>0.
$$
Hence
$$
\kappa_{n,1}>\kappa_{n,-1}>
\kappa_{n,\lambda}\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1).
$$

Step 5: Identify the second extremal value and run boundary checks
Because $\lambda\in\mathbb C^\times$, the equation $\lambda^2=1$ has exactly the two solutions $1$ and $-1$. Thus $\lambda=1$ gives the unique largest conductor, and $\lambda=-1$ gives the unique second-largest conductor. As an independent boundary check, when $n=2$ the Apéry representatives modulo $8$ give conductors
$$
42,\qquad44,\qquad52
$$
for the generic, $-1$, and $1$ strata, respectively, agreeing with the formulas. Therefore
$$
\beta_n=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n,
\qquad
E_n=\{-1\}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n,\{-1\}\right)}$

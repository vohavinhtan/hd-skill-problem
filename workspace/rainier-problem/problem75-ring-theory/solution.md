## Steps

Step 1: Convert the conductor to residue-row gaps
Put $u=t^n$ and $s=t$, so $s^n=u$, and write
$$
x=u^4,\qquad
y=u^6+u^7+u^7s,\qquad
z=u^9+\left(\lambda^2u^9+(\lambda-1)u^{11}\right)s.
$$
Let
$$
S_{n,\lambda}=\{v(f):0\neq f\in A_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Because $\mathbb C[[t]]$ is finite over $\mathbb C[[x]]=\mathbb C[[t^{4n}]]$, the ring $A_{n,\lambda}$ is a complete finite $\mathbb C[[x]]$-module. If every integer at least $c$ lies in $S_{n,\lambda}$, choose $f_k=t^k+$ higher terms for each $k\geq c$ and successively subtract multiples of the $f_k$; completeness then gives $t^k\in A_{n,\lambda}$. The converse is immediate. Hence $\kappa_{n,\lambda}$ is one plus the largest integer missing from $S_{n,\lambda}$.

For $0\leq r<n$ define the residue row
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=\{q\geq0:qn+r\in S_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Put
$$
H=\langle4,6,9\rangle,\qquad K=H\cup(5+H),\qquad L=K\cup(7+H).
$$
Their complements are
$$
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus H=\{1,2,3,5,7,11\},
$$
$$
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus K=\{1,2,3,7\},
\qquad
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus L=\{1,2,3\}.
$$
Thus their largest gaps are $11,7,3$. The notation $Q_r=a+K$ means exactly
$$
Q_r=(a+H)\cup(a+5+H).
$$

Step 2: Determine all residue rows and the two-step alternation
The initial $u$-weights of $x,y,z$ are $4,6,9$. Every equality between monomials of these weights is generated by
$$
Y^2-X^3,\qquad Z^2-X^3Y.
$$
Indeed, reducing by these two binomials leaves exponents of $Y,Z$ in $\{0,1\}$, and the four remaining weights $0,6,9,15$ are distinct modulo $4$; hence no further equal-weight pair remains.

Set
$$
U=y^2-x^3-2xz-x^2y,\qquad G=z^2-x^3y.
$$
Then
$$
U=-u^{15}+\left(2(1-\lambda^2)u^{13}+2u^{14}+(1-2\lambda)u^{15}\right)s+u^{14}s^2
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
G={}&-u^{19}+\left(2\lambda^2u^{18}-u^{19}+2(\lambda-1)u^{20}\right)s\\
&+\left(\lambda^4u^{18}+2\lambda^2(\lambda-1)u^{20}+(\lambda-1)^2u^{22}\right)s^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
For $\lambda^2\neq1$, the two first-row generators have $u$-weights $13,18$, so
$$
Q_1=13+K.
$$
For $\lambda=-1$, put
$$
V=G-xU=-4(u^{19}+u^{20})s+4(u^{22}-u^{20})s^2;
$$
the first-row weights are $14,19$, so
$$
Q_1=14+K.
$$
These statements also hold when $n=2$ after substituting $s^2=u$ first; in that case there is no row $Q_2$.

Assume now $n\geq3$. For $\lambda^2\neq1$, the primitive cancellations
$$
zU-\frac{1-\lambda^2}{\lambda^2}xG,\qquad
zG-\frac{\lambda^2}{1-\lambda^2}x^2yU
$$
have first uncancelled row-$2$ terms
$$
\lambda^2(1-\lambda^2)u^{22}s^2,\qquad 3\lambda^4u^{27}s^2.
$$
For $\lambda=-1$, the corresponding pair
$$
zU+\frac12xV,\qquad zV+2x^2yU
$$
has first uncancelled row-$2$ terms
$$
3u^{23}s^2,\qquad -2u^{28}s^2.
$$
Thus, before a possible wrap at the last row,
$$
Q_2=
\begin{cases}
22+K,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
23+K,&\lambda=-1.
\end{cases}
$$

It remains to justify the recursion and its exhaustiveness. The two $H$-translates in $a+K$ overlap exactly in
$$
H\cap(5+H)=(9+H)\cup(14+H).
$$
Hence every same-row cancellation between the two generators is an $H$-multiple of one of two primitive overlaps. Starting from an odd row with generator weights $(a,a+5)$, those two primitive overlaps cancel at weights $a+9$ and $a+14$ and expose the next even row with weights $(a+9,a+14)$. After these outputs are reduced by rows already determined, their first terms of the next new residue come from the fixed term $u^{14}s^2$ in $U$ and have weights $a+14,a+19$. Hence
$$
(a,a+5)_{2j+1}\longmapsto(a+9,a+14)_{2j+2}
\longmapsto(a+14,a+19)_{2j+3}.
$$
This transition is invariant under adding an element of $H$ to every displayed weight, because the multipliers $x,z,x^2y$ have residue $0$. Its normalization uses only the nonzero leading scalars already displayed and the coefficient $1$ of $u^{14}s^2$, so no further exceptional value of $\lambda$ appears inside either stratum. Every nonprimitive overlap is an $H$-multiple of one of the two primitive overlaps, while every equality among the base weights reduces through $Y^2-X^3$ and $Z^2-X^3Y$. Thus the transition list is exhaustive: odd-to-even adds $9$, even-to-odd adds $5$, and two rows add $14$.

Consequently, for $n\geq3$,
$$
Q_{2j+1}=
\begin{cases}
14j+13+K,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
14j+14+K,&\lambda=-1,
\end{cases}
\qquad 1\leq2j+1<n,
$$
and for the nonfinal even rows
$$
Q_{2j}=
\begin{cases}
14j+8+K,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
14j+9+K,&\lambda=-1,
\end{cases}
\qquad 2\leq2j\leq n-2.
$$
Also $Q_0=H$. If $n$ is odd, the last row $r=n-1$ is even; the final primitive reduction reaches an $s^n$-term, and replacing $s^n$ by $u$ contributes exactly the additional translate $7+H$. Hence
$$
Q_{n-1}=
\begin{cases}
(7n+1)+L,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
(7n+2)+L,&\lambda=-1.
\end{cases}
$$
If $n$ is even, the last row is odd and the displayed odd-row formula already applies.

For $\lambda=1$, the identity $G=xU$ removes the second first-row generator. The first toric relation produces the single new leading term $u^{14}s$, while the second produces only its $x$-multiple. Suppose row $r$ has only the translate $14r+H$. Two terms in that row can have the same $u$-weight only through an equality of monomials of weights $4,6,9$; the first paragraph of this step reduces every such equality by the two base relations. Their remainders are multiples of $U$ because $G=xU$, so cancellation moves to residue $r+1$ and adds exactly $14$ instead of creating a second $H$-coset in residue $r$. Starting from $Q_0=H$, induction gives
$$
Q_r(1)=14r+H\qquad(0\leq r<n).
$$

Step 3: Compute the conductor for $\lambda=-1$
For a residue row define
$$
g_r=\max\left(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus Q_r\right),
\qquad
m_r=r+ng_r.
$$
Then $m_r$ is the largest missing valuation in residue class $r$, and
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max_{0\leq r<n}m_r.
$$
Assume first $n\geq3$ and $\lambda=-1$. From Step 2,
$$
g_0=11,
$$
$$
g_r=7r+14\quad(r\text{ odd}),
\qquad
g_r=7r+16\quad(r\text{ even},\ 2\leq r\leq n-2).
$$
Thus $m_1-m_0=1+10n>0$, and thereafter the increments alternate as
$$
m_{r+1}-m_r=
\begin{cases}
1+9n,&r\text{ odd},\\
1+5n,&r\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
$$
If $n$ is odd, the boundary row has $g_{n-1}=7n+5$, and its difference from the preceding odd row is again $1+5n$. Hence the maximum is always attained at $r=n-1$.

If $n$ is even, the last row is
$$
Q_{n-1}(-1)=7n+K,
$$
so $g_{n-1}=7n+7$ and
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}=1+(n-1)+n(7n+7)=7n^2+8n.
$$
If $n$ is odd, Step 2 gives
$$
Q_{n-1}(-1)=(7n+2)+L,
$$
so $g_{n-1}=7n+5$ and
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}=1+(n-1)+n(7n+5)=7n^2+6n.
$$
Therefore, for $n\geq3$,
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n.
$$

Step 4: Compute the generic and $\lambda=1$ conductor levels
For $\lambda^2\neq1$, Step 2 gives
$$
g_0=11,
$$
$$
g_r=7r+13\quad(r\text{ odd}),
\qquad
g_r=7r+15\quad(r\text{ even},\ 2\leq r\leq n-2).
$$
Again $m_1-m_0=1+9n>0$, and the later increments alternate between $1+9n$ and $1+5n$. If $n$ is odd, the last row has $g_{n-1}=7n+4$, still giving the final increment $1+5n$. Thus the last row is maximal. It follows that
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}
=
7n^2+\left(6+(-1)^n\right)n
\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1,\ n\geq3).
$$

For $\lambda=1$,
$$
Q_r(1)=14r+H,
\qquad
g_r=14r+11.
$$
Hence
$$
m_{r+1}-m_r=1+14n>0,
$$
so the last row is again maximal and
$$
\kappa_{n,1}
=1+(n-1)+n\left(14(n-1)+11\right)
=14n^2-2n.
$$
For $n\geq3$,
$$
\kappa_{n,1}-\kappa_{n,-1}
=
\begin{cases}
n(7n-10),&n\text{ even},\\
n(7n-8),&n\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
$$
and
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}-\kappa_{n,\lambda}=n
\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1).
$$
Both differences are positive.

Step 5: Verify the boundary case and identify the second extremum
For $n=2$, Step 2 gives
$$
Q_0=H,\qquad
Q_1=
\begin{cases}
13+K,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
14+K,&\lambda=-1,\\
14+H,&\lambda=1.
\end{cases}
$$
There is no $Q_2$. To exhibit the boundary check modulo $8$, the least representatives of $H$ modulo $4$ are $(0,9,6,15)$ and those of $K$ are $(0,5,6,11)$. Converting the two residue rows to valuations gives the Apéry representatives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda^2\neq1:&\quad(0,49,18,27,12,37,30,39),\\
\lambda=-1:&\quad(0,41,18,51,12,29,30,39),\\
\lambda=1:&\quad(0,41,18,59,12,29,30,47).
\end{aligned}
$$
Because $t^8=x\in A_{2,\lambda}$, the conductor is the largest entry minus $7$. Thus the three conductors are respectively
$$
42,\qquad44,\qquad52,
$$
which agree with the formulas in Steps 3 and 4.

Finally, $\lambda^2=1$ has exactly the two solutions $1$ and $-1$. The inequalities in Step 4, together with the explicit $n=2$ check, show
$$
\kappa_{n,1}>\kappa_{n,-1}>\kappa_{n,\lambda}
\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1)
$$
for every $n\geq2$. Therefore
$$
\beta_n=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n,
\qquad
E_n=\{-1\}.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n,\{-1\}\right)}$

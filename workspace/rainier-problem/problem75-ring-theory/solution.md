## Steps

Step 1: Split the hidden rank-drop locus
Put
$$
a=8n+4,\qquad D=72n+9,
$$
and define
$$
\rho=\lambda^2+\lambda+1,\qquad
\Delta=(\lambda-2)\rho.
$$
Also set
$$
x=t^8,\qquad
R=t^{a+2}+\rho t^{a+3}.
$$
The two middle generators satisfy
$$
\Psi_{n,\lambda}(Y)=t^a+R,
$$
and direct subtraction gives
$$
\Psi_{n,\lambda}(Z)-\Psi_{n,\lambda}(Y)=\Delta R.
$$

If $\Delta\neq0$, then $R,t^a\in A_{n,\lambda}$, so
$$
A_{n,\lambda}=\mathbb C[[x,t^a,R,t^D]].
$$
If $\lambda=2$, then $\rho=7$, $\Delta=0$, and the two middle generators coincide:
$$
F=t^a+t^{a+2}+7t^{a+3}.
$$
Thus
$$
A_{n,2}=\mathbb C[[x,F,t^D]].
$$
If $\rho=0$, then $\Delta=0$ and the common middle generator is
$$
F_0=t^a+t^{a+2}.
$$
Hence
$$
A_{n,\lambda}=\mathbb C[[x,F_0,t^D]]
\qquad(\rho=0).
$$
These cases exhaust $\mathbb C^{\times}$ because $\Delta=0$ exactly when $\lambda=2$ or $\rho=0$.

Step 2: Compute the generic conductor from the first approximate root
Assume $\Delta\neq0$. Since then $\rho\neq0$, define
$$
H=\frac{R^2-x^{n+1}t^a}{2\rho}.
$$
As
$$
R=t^{a+2}+\rho t^{a+3},
$$
we obtain
$$
H=t^c+\frac{\rho}{2}t^{c+1},
\qquad
c=2a+5.
$$
The identities
$$
2a=8(2n+1),
$$
$$
2(a+2)=a+8(n+1),
$$
$$
2c=a+(a+2)+8(2n+2)
$$
show that the numerical semigroup generated by the valuations
$$
8,\ a,\ a+2,\ c
$$
reduces to the eight binary sums
$$
\varepsilon a+\eta(a+2)+\theta c,
\qquad
\varepsilon,\eta,\theta\in\{0,1\}.
$$
Their residues modulo $8$ are all distinct because
$$
a\equiv4,\qquad a+2\equiv6,\qquad c\equiv5\pmod8.
$$
The largest binary sum is
$$
a+(a+2)+c=4a+7=32n+23.
$$
Hence this semigroup has conductor $32n+16$.

We must rule out a smaller ring conductor caused by cancellation. Below $4a+7$, a monomial cannot contain $H^2$ because
$$
2c=4a+10>4a+7.
$$
The relation
$$
(t^a)^2=x^{2n+1}
$$
reduces every square of $t^a$, while
$$
R^2=x^{n+1}t^a+2\rho H
$$
reduces every square of $R$. Therefore every element of valuation below $4a+7$ is a $\mathbb C[[x]]$-linear combination of the eight binary monomials. Their leading residues are distinct, so the term of least valuation cannot cancel.

Conversely, products of $x,t^a,R,H$ realize every value in the semigroup. From its conductor onward, successive cancellation of higher coefficients and completeness of $A_{n,\lambda}$ produce every monomial $t^m$. Since
$$
D=72n+9>32n+23,
$$
the generator $t^D$ does not change the least residue representatives. Thus
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=32n+16
\qquad(\Delta\neq0).
$$

Step 3: Compute the conductor at the isolated singular point
At $\lambda=2$ we have
$$
F=t^a+t^{a+2}+7t^{a+3}.
$$
Define
$$
H=\frac{F^2-x^{2n+1}}{2}.
$$
Then
$$
H=t^h\left(1+7t+\frac12t^2+7t^3+\frac{49}{2}t^4\right),
\qquad
h=2a+2.
$$
Since
$$
2h=a+8(3n+2),
$$
the leading terms of $H^2$ and $x^{3n+2}F$ cancel. The coefficient one degree above the leading term of $H^2$ is $14$, while $x^{3n+2}F$ has no term there. Therefore
$$
K=\frac{H^2-x^{3n+2}F}{14}
$$
has valuation
$$
k=2h+1=4a+5.
$$

The identities
$$
2a=8(2n+1),
$$
$$
2h=a+8(3n+2),
$$
$$
2k=h+8(6n+4)
$$
reduce the semigroup $\langle8,a,h,k\rangle$ to the eight binary sums
$$
\varepsilon a+\eta h+\theta k,
\qquad
\varepsilon,\eta,\theta\in\{0,1\}.
$$
Their residues are distinct because
$$
a\equiv4,\qquad h\equiv2,\qquad k\equiv5\pmod8.
$$
The largest sum is
$$
a+h+k=7a+7=56n+35,
$$
so the semigroup conductor is $56n+28$.

For the ring itself, the exact relations
$$
F^2=x^{2n+1}+2H,
$$
$$
H^2=x^{3n+2}F+14K
$$
reduce powers of $F$ and $H$. Also
$$
2k=8a+10>7a+7,
$$
so no term containing $K^2$ has valuation below the largest binary sum. Every element below that level therefore reduces to a $\mathbb C[[x]]$-linear combination of the eight binary monomials, whose distinct leading residues prevent cancellation. The same completeness argument as in Step 2 gives all monomials from the semigroup conductor onward. Finally,
$$
D=72n+9>56n+35
$$
for $n\geq2$. Hence
$$
\kappa_{n,2}=56n+28.
$$

Step 4: Analyze the second component by descending to the even subring
Assume
$$
\rho=\lambda^2+\lambda+1=0.
$$
Then
$$
A_{n,\lambda}=\mathbb C[[t^8,t^a+t^{a+2},t^D]].
$$
All exponents in the first two generators are even. Put
$$
u=t^2,\qquad M=4n+2,
$$
and let
$$
B=\mathbb C[[u^4,u^M+u^{M+1}]]\subset\mathbb C[[u]].
$$
Then
$$
A_{n,\lambda}=B[[t^D]].
$$

Write
$$
f=u^M+u^{M+1}
$$
and define
$$
J=\frac{f^2-u^{4(2n+1)}}{2}.
$$
Its valuation in $u$ is
$$
j=2M+1.
$$
The relations
$$
2M=4(2n+1),
$$
$$
2j=M+4(3n+2)
$$
reduce the value semigroup of $B$ to
$$
0,\quad M,\quad j,\quad M+j
$$
modulo $4$. Their residues are $0,2,1,3$, respectively, so they are the least representatives. As $2j>M+j$, no $J^2$ term can affect a valuation below the maximum. The relation
$$
f^2=u^{4(2n+1)}+2J
$$
then gives the same no-cancellation certificate. Therefore the conductor of $B$ in $\mathbb C[[u]]$ is
$$
\delta=M+j-3=12n+4.
$$
In particular,
$$
u^{\delta-1}\notin B,
$$
while $u^r\in B$ for every $r\geq\delta$.

Since $D>\delta$, we have
$$
(t^D)^2=u^D\in B.
$$
Hence every element of $A_{n,\lambda}$ is uniquely of the form
$$
b_0+t^D b_1,
\qquad
b_0,b_1\in B,
$$
because the first summand has only even powers of $t$ and the second only odd powers.

All even monomials $t^{2r}$ with $r\geq\delta$ belong to $B$, and all odd monomials
$$
t^{D+2r}
$$
with $r\geq\delta$ belong to $t^D B$. Therefore every monomial of degree at least
$$
D+2\delta-1=96n+16
$$
lies in $A_{n,\lambda}$. The preceding odd degree is
$$
D+2(\delta-1)=96n+15.
$$
If this monomial belonged to $A_{n,\lambda}$, parity would force
$$
u^{\delta-1}\in B,
$$
contrary to the definition of $\delta$. Thus
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=96n+16
\qquad(\rho=0).
$$

The three conductor values satisfy
$$
96n+16>56n+28>32n+16
$$
for every $n\geq2$. The largest value occurs at the two roots of $\lambda^2+\lambda+1$, while the second-largest distinct value occurs only at $\lambda=2$.

Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(56n+28,\{2\}\right)}$

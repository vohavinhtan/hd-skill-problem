## Steps

Step 1: Reduce the conductor to missing valuations
Put $u=t^n$ and $s=t$, so $s^n=u$, and write
$$
x=u^4,\qquad y=u^6+u^7+u^7s,\qquad
z=u^9+\left(\lambda^2u^9+(\lambda-1)u^{11}\right)s.
$$
Let
$$
S_{n,\lambda}=\{v(f):0\neq f\in A_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
We first justify directly that the conductor is one plus the largest integer missing from $S_{n,\lambda}$. Suppose every integer at least $c$ lies in $S_{n,\lambda}$. For each residue $\rho$ modulo $4n$, let $m_\rho\geq c$ be the least integer in that residue and choose
$$
b_\rho=t^{m_\rho}+\text{ higher terms}\in A_{n,\lambda}.
$$
If a series whose least term is $\alpha t^\ell$ has $\ell\geq c$, then $\ell=m_\rho+4nj$ for its residue $\rho$, so subtracting $\alpha x^jb_\rho$ raises its valuation. Starting from $t^k$, $k\geq c$, repeat this cancellation. The successive exponents tend to infinity, hence for each fixed $\rho$ the coefficients of the corrections form a power series $h_\rho(x)\in\mathbb C[[x]]$. Therefore
$$
t^k=\sum_{\rho=0}^{4n-1}h_\rho(x)b_\rho\in A_{n,\lambda},
$$
because $\mathbb C[[x]]\subset A_{n,\lambda}$. The converse is immediate. Thus
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max\left(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus S_{n,\lambda}\right).
$$

For $0\leq r<n$ set
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=\{q\geq0:qn+r\in S_{n,\lambda}\},
$$
and put
$$
H=\langle4,6,9\rangle,\qquad K=H\cup(5+H),\qquad L=K\cup(7+H).
$$
Their largest gaps are respectively $11,7,3$.

Step 2: Prove the residue rows by a two-state transition
Before the wrap $s^n=u$, order $u^qs^r$ by $(q,r)$. The relations among weights $4,6,9$ are generated by
$$
Y^2-X^3,\qquad Z^2-X^3Y,
$$
because reducing by them leaves weights $0,6,9,15$, distinct modulo $4$. Set
$$
U=y^2-x^3-2xz-x^2y,\qquad G=z^2-x^3y.
$$
Then
$$
U=-u^{15}+\left(2(1-\lambda^2)u^{13}+2u^{14}+(1-2\lambda)u^{15}\right)s+u^{14}s^2
$$
and
$$
G=-u^{19}+\left(2\lambda^2u^{18}-u^{19}+2(\lambda-1)u^{20}\right)s
+\left(\lambda^4u^{18}+2\lambda^2(\lambda-1)u^{20}+(\lambda-1)^2u^{22}\right)s^2.
$$

First assume $\lambda^2\neq1$ and put $D=\lambda^2$. For a fixed positive row $r$, let $a_r=7r+7+(-1)^r$ and normalize its two generators to leading monomials $u^{a_r}s^r$ and $u^{a_r+5}s^r$. Only their two-row jets can influence the first unreduced monomials of the next row. Modulo terms already reducible by earlier rows, those jets have one of the following two forms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
r\text{ odd}:\quad
P&=u^{a_r}s^r\left(1-\frac r{D-1}u-\frac r{(r+1)(D-1)}us\right),\\
Q&=u^{a_r+5}s^r\left(1+\frac D{r+1}s\right),
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
r\text{ even}:\quad
P&=u^{a_r}s^r\left(1-\frac{rD+1}{D(D-1)}u-\frac1{D-1}us\right),\\
Q&=u^{a_r+5}s^r\left(1+\frac D{r+1}s\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
The case $r=1$ follows directly from the displayed expansions of $U$ and $G$ after dividing by $2(1-D)$ and $2D$.

For odd $r$, $H\cap(5+H)=(9+H)\cup(14+H)$, so the only primitive same-row overlaps are
$$
zP-xQ,\qquad zQ-x^2yP.
$$
Substituting the odd jet, the first surviving coefficients are simply
$$
D-\frac D{r+1}=\frac{Dr}{r+1},\qquad
D+\frac D{r+1}=\frac{D(r+2)}{r+1},
$$
at $u^{a_r+9}s^{r+1}$ and $u^{a_r+14}s^{r+1}$. They are nonzero. Keeping one further column and dividing by these leading scalars gives exactly the even jet with $r$ replaced by $r+1$. Thus an odd row $a_r+K$ produces the next row $(a_r+9)+K=a_{r+1}+K$.

For even $r\geq2$, the same-row overlaps already reduce through the preceding odd row, and the new relations are the adjacent-row pairs
$$
P_1P_{r-1}-xP,\qquad P_1Q_{r-1}-xQ,
$$
where $P_1,Q_1$ are the normalized row-$1$ generators. Substituting the even and odd jets, the first pair leaves coefficient $1/(D(D-1))$ in the $Q$-column; reducing that column leaves
$$
-\frac{(r-1)(r+2)}{2r(r+1)(D-1)}
$$
at $u^{a_r+5}s^{r+1}$. The second pair first leaves $D/(r(r+1))$ in the $xP_{r+1}$-column, and reducing it leaves
$$
-\frac1{2(D-1)}
$$
at $u^{a_r+10}s^{r+1}$. Both are nonzero, and after normalization the resulting pair is exactly the odd jet with $r$ replaced by $r+1$. Hence an even row produces $(a_r+5)+K=a_{r+1}+K$. This proves the period-two transition without carrying any further coefficients.

For $\lambda=-1$, normalize the first row using
$$
P_1=\frac U2,\qquad Q_1=-\frac{G-xU}{4},
$$
and put $a_r=7r+8+(-1)^r$. In this stratum every row has the same canonical jet
$$
P=u^{a_r}s^r\left(1+\frac{s}{r+1}\right),\qquad
Q=u^{a_r+5}s^r(1+u+us),
$$
again modulo terms already reducible by earlier rows. For odd $r$, the two same-row overlaps above have leading coefficients
$$
1+\frac1{r+1}=\frac{r+2}{r+1},\qquad
1-\frac1{r+1}=\frac r{r+1};
$$
for even $r$, the adjacent pairs
$$
P_1P_{r-1}-Q,\qquad P_1Q_{r-1}-xyP
$$
have leading coefficients
$$
\frac12+\frac1r=\frac{r+2}{2r},\qquad
\frac12-\frac1{r+1}=\frac{r-1}{2(r+1)}.
$$
After normalization the same canonical jet reappears with $r$ replaced by $r+1$. Thus every transition is nonzero and the row starts alternate by $+9,+5$, exactly as in the generic stratum.

These local pairs are exhaustive. A same-row cancellation is an $H$-multiple of one of the two primitive overlaps. An adjacent-row cancellation is generated by the displayed adjacent pair. If the row separation is at least two, use $a_{r+2}-a_r=14\in H$ to replace the higher row by the preceding adjacent relation, strictly lowering the separation. Induction therefore reduces every initial relation to these local pairs. Consequently, before the final wrap,
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=
\begin{cases}
7r+7+(-1)^r+K,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
7r+8+(-1)^r+K,&\lambda=-1.
\end{cases}
$$
If $n$ is odd, the last even row meets $s^n=u$ and gains exactly $7+H$, so $K$ is replaced there by $L$.

For $\lambda=1$, $G=xU$ and $F=U/2$ has initial monomial $u^{14}s$. Before wrapping, any relation among
$$
u^4,\quad u^6,\quad u^9,\quad u^{14}s
$$
has the same power of $u^{14}s$ on both sides; factor it, and the remaining relation among $u^4,u^6,u^9$ is generated by the two base relations. Their lifts reduce by $U$ and $G=xU$, so no new initial generator occurs. At the wrap,
$$
(u^{14}s)^n=u^{14n+1},
$$
and $14n+1\in H$: for $n=2m$ it is $9+4(7m-2)$, while for $n=2m+1$ it is $6+9+28m$. Thus wrapping also creates no new generator, and
$$
Q_r(1)=14r+H\qquad(0\leq r<n).
$$

Step 3: Compute the two nonunit conductor levels
For each row let
$$
g_r=\max(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus Q_r),\qquad m_r=r+ng_r.
$$
Then $\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max_r m_r$. For the two nonunit strata, write $\eta=0$ for $\lambda^2\neq1$ and $\eta=1$ for $\lambda=-1$. Away from the odd-$n$ boundary row,
$$
g_r=7r+14+\eta+(-1)^r\qquad(r\geq1).
$$
Thus $m_1>m_0$, and later increments alternate between $1+9n$ and $1+5n$. The boundary replacement $K\to L$ lowers the last quotient gap by $4$, leaving the final increment $1+5n$. Hence the last row is maximal and
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=7n^2+\left(6+\eta+(-1)^n\right)n.
$$

Step 4: Compute the $\lambda=1$ level
Since $Q_r(1)=14r+H$, its largest gap is $g_r=14r+11$, so $m_{r+1}-m_r=1+14n>0$ and
$$
\kappa_{n,1}=14n^2-2n.
$$
For $n\geq3$,
$$
\kappa_{n,1}-\kappa_{n,-1}
=n\left(7n-9-(-1)^n\right)>0,\qquad
\kappa_{n,-1}-\kappa_{n,\lambda}=n>0
$$
for $\lambda^2\neq1$.

Step 5: Check $n=2$ and identify the second extremum
For $n=2$ there is no $Q_2$, and
$$
Q_1=13+K,\qquad14+K,\qquad14+H
$$
for $\lambda^2\neq1$, $\lambda=-1$, and $\lambda=1$. Their largest missing quotients are $20,21,25$, hence the conductors are $42,44,52$. Therefore the formulas also hold at $n=2$.

Finally $\lambda^2=1$ has only $1$ and $-1$. Thus $\lambda=1$ gives the unique largest conductor and $\lambda=-1$ the unique second-largest conductor. Hence
$$
\beta_n=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n,\qquad E_n=\{-1\}.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n,\{-1\}\right)}$

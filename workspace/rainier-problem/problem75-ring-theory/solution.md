## Steps

Step 1: Reduce the conductor to valuation rows
Put $u=t^n$ and $s=t$, so $s^n=u$, and write
$$
x=u^4,\qquad y=u^6+u^7+u^{7}s,\qquad
z=u^9+\left(\lambda^{2}u^9+(\lambda-1)u^{11}\right)s.
$$
Let $S_{n,\lambda}=\{v(f):0\neq f\in A_{n,\lambda}\}$. Since $x=t^{4n}$, $\mathbb C[[t]]$ is finite over $\mathbb C[[x]]$, so $y,z$ are integral. Hence $R=\mathbb C[[x]][y,z]$ is finite and complete; polynomial truncations of any source series converge to its image, so $A_{n,\lambda}=R$. If every integer at least $c$ occurs as a valuation, choose in each residue modulo $4n$ an element $b_\rho=t^{m_\rho}+$ higher terms with minimal $m_\rho\geq c$. For $k=m_\rho+4nj$, subtracting a suitable multiple of $x^{j}b_\rho$ raises the valuation; completeness lets this cancellation converge to $t^k\in A_{n,\lambda}$. Hence
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max\left(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus S_{n,\lambda}\right).
$$
For $0\leq r<n$ put
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=\{q\geq0:qn+r\in S_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Set
$$
H=\langle4,6,9\rangle,\qquad K=H\cup(5+H),\qquad L=K\cup(7+H).
$$
Their gaps are
$$
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus H=\{1,2,3,5,7,11\},\qquad
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus K=\{1,2,3,7\},\qquad
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus L=\{1,2,3\}.
$$

Step 2: Derive every residue layer by induction
Let
$$
B=\mathbb C[[u^4,u^6,u^9]],\qquad I=B+u^{5}B.
$$
First determine all relations in $I$. If $p+u^{5}q=0$ with $p,q\in B$, then $p\in B\cap u^{5}B$. Since
$$
H\cap(5+H)=(9+H)\cup(14+H),
$$
we have $B\cap u^{5}B=u^{9}B+u^{14}B$. Thus
$$
p=u^{9}a+u^{14}b,\qquad q=-u^{4}a-u^{9}b
$$
for some $a,b\in B$. Therefore every relation between $1,u^5$ is generated over $B$ by
$$
(u^9,-u^4),\qquad(u^{14},-u^9).
$$
Subtracting these relations raises the $u$-weight, so completeness of $B$ shows that they reduce every same-row cancellation.

The relations among the base weights $4,6,9$ are generated by $Y^2-X^3$ and $Z^2-X^{3}Y$: after reducing the exponents of $Y,Z$ to $0$ or $1$, the remaining weights $0,6,9,15$ are distinct modulo $4$. Put
$$
U=y^2-x^3-2xz-x^{2}y,\qquad G=z^2-x^{3}y.
$$
Then
$$
U=-u^{15}+\left(2(1-\lambda^2)u^{13}+2u^{14}+(1-2\lambda)u^{15}\right)s+u^{14}s^2
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
G&=-u^{19}+\left(2\lambda^{2}u^{18}-u^{19}+2(\lambda-1)u^{20}\right)s\\
&+\left(\lambda^{4}u^{18}+2\lambda^2(\lambda-1)u^{20}+(\lambda-1)^2u^{22}\right)s^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
This also determines the first positive row exhaustively. Indeed, if an element has initial residue $1$, its terms of smaller valuation have residue $0$ and must cancel; their leading coefficients therefore form a relation among $u^4,u^6,u^9$. Reducing that relation by the two base relations replaces the element, without changing its first nonzero row, by a $B$-linear combination of $U,G$. Hence every primitive row-$1$ initial comes from these two lifts and their two syzygies. If $D=\lambda^2\neq1$, the initials of $U,G$ are $2(1-D)u^{13}s$ and $2D u^{18}s$; after rescaling the lifts to leading coefficient $1$, the two syzygies first survive in row $2$ with coefficients $\frac{D}{2}$ and $\frac{3D}{2}$, so they create no new row-$1$ initial. Thus $Q_1=13+K$. If $\lambda=-1$, the initials of $U,G-xU$ are $2u^{14}s,-4u^{19}s$; after rescaling, the two syzygies first survive in row $2$ with coefficients $\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2}$, giving $Q_1=14+K$. If $\lambda=1$, $G=xU$, so the lifted relation module is principal in row $1$, generated by the initial $2u^{14}s$ of $U$; hence $Q_1=14+H$.

Assume first $D=\lambda^2\neq1$. Normalize
$$
F_1=\frac{U}{2(1-D)},\qquad G_1=\frac{G}{2D},
$$
and set $a_r=7r+7+(-1)^{r}$. Reduce by earlier rows and normalize $F_r,G_r$ so that their initials are $u^{a_r}s^r,u^{a_r+5}s^r$. Write
$$
A_r=[u^{a_r+1}s^r]F_r,\quad
B_r=[u^{a_r+1}s^{r+1}]F_r,\quad
C_r=[u^{a_r+5}s^{r+1}]G_r.
$$
The induction invariant is
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
& A_r&B_r&C_r\\ \hline
r\text{ odd}&-\frac{r}{D-1}&-\frac{r}{(r+1)(D-1)}&\frac{D}{r+1}\\
r\text{ even}&-\frac{rD+1}{D(D-1)}&-\frac{1}{D-1}&\frac{D}{r+1}.
\end{array}
$$
It holds at $r=1$ from $U,G$. If $r$ is odd, the two general syzygies lift to
$$
zF_r-xG_r,\qquad zG_r-x^{2}yF_r.
$$
Their first row-$r+1$ coefficients are
$$
D-C_r=\frac{Dr}{r+1},\qquad D+C_r=\frac{D(r+2)}{r+1},
$$
at weights $a_r+9,a_r+14$. After normalization the next coefficients are
$$
A_{r+1}=-\frac{(r+1)D+1}{D(D-1)},\qquad
B_{r+1}=-\frac{1}{D-1},\qquad C_{r+1}=\frac{D}{r+2},
$$
so the even line follows and the row start shifts by $9$.

If $r\geq2$ is even, then $I^2=I$ because $u^{10}\in B$, and $a_1+a_{r-1}=a_r+4$. Thus every adjacent-row relation reduces, modulo the two internal syzygies, to
$$
F_1F_{r-1}-xF_r,\qquad F_1G_{r-1}-xG_r.
$$
The first leaves
$$
-\frac{(r-1)(r+2)}{2r(r+1)(D-1)}
$$
at weight $a_r+5$ in row $r+1$, and the second leaves
$$
-\frac{1}{2(D-1)}
$$
at weight $a_r+10$. Both are nonzero. Normalizing gives
$$
A_{r+1}=-\frac{r+1}{D-1},\qquad
B_{r+1}=-\frac{r+1}{(r+2)(D-1)},\qquad C_{r+1}=\frac{D}{r+2},
$$
so the odd line is restored and the row start shifts by $5$. Hence, before wrapping,
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=7r+7+(-1)^{r}+K.
$$

For $\lambda=-1$, take
$$
F_1=\frac{U}{2},\qquad G_1=-\frac{G-xU}{4},\qquad a_r=7r+8+(-1)^{r}.
$$
After reduction, normalize
$$
F_r=u^{a_r}s^r+\frac{1}{r+1}u^{a_r}s^{r+1}+\text{ reducible terms},
$$
$$
G_r=u^{a_r+5}s^r+u^{a_r+6}s^r+u^{a_r+6}s^{r+1}+\text{ reducible terms}.
$$
This is true for $r=1$. For odd $r$, the two lifted syzygies have leading coefficients
$$
\frac{r+2}{r+1},\qquad\frac{r}{r+1}.
$$
The corresponding next coefficients are $\frac{1}{r+1}$ for the first generator and the same leading scalar for each of the two shifted terms of the second. Division therefore gives $\frac{1}{r+2}$ and two unit coefficients, restoring the invariant. For even $r$, the adjacent pairs
$$
F_1F_{r-1}-G_r,\qquad F_1G_{r-1}-xyF_r
$$
have leading coefficients
$$
\frac{r+2}{2r},\qquad\frac{r-1}{2(r+1)}.
$$
Their corresponding next coefficients are $\frac{1}{2r}$ and the same second leading scalar. After division the invariant is again restored. Thus the shifts alternate by $9,5$, and
$$
Q_r(-1)=7r+8+(-1)^{r}+K
$$
before wrapping.

These transitions are exhaustive. Same-row relations reduce by the proved syzygies of $I$, adjacent-row relations by $I^2=I$, and a relation whose row indices differ by at least two reduces the separation using the already constructed adjacent relation because $a_{r+2}-a_r=14\in H$.

It remains to impose $s^n=u$. If $n$ is even, the last row $r=n-1$ is odd. Its first would-be wrapped output has weight $a_r+9$ in row $n$, hence weight $a_r+10$ after wrapping. Since every integer at least $8$ belongs to $K$, no new last-row value can result.

Now let $n$ be odd and $r=n-1$. Then $r$ is even. The first adjacent pair above has, before wrapping, a nonzero leading term
$$
\alpha_r u^{a_r+5}s^n,\qquad
\alpha_r=-\frac{(r-1)(r+2)}{2r(r+1)(D-1)}
$$
in the generic stratum, while for $\lambda=-1$ the same coefficient is $\frac{r+2}{2r}$. Thus after $s^n=u$ the boundary reduction starts nontrivially at quotient $a_r+6$ in row $0$. Reduction is performed in increasing $t$-valuation. At quotient $a_r+6$ every row is already present; at quotient $a_r+7$ every row $0,\ldots,r-1$ is present, because $a_r-a_j\geq9$ for $j<r$ and $K$ contains every integer at least $8$. In the last row, however, $a_r+7$ is absent because $7\notin K$. At each present valuation the associated graded piece is one-dimensional. The normalized row generator used there is an $H$-multiple of $F_j$ or $G_j$; after subtraction its first unreduced tail is exactly the tail used in the odd/even transition above. Thus, if the current residual coefficient is $\gamma\neq0$, the next one is $\gamma\theta_j$ with $\theta_j$ one of those displayed nonzero scalars. The coefficient therefore remains nonzero until the first absent valuation, so the boundary normal form starts with $u^{a_r+7}s^r$. Adjoining this initial changes $K$ to $L$; since $L$ already contains every integer at least $4$, any later cancellation has an offset already in $L$. Therefore
$$
Q_{n-1}=\begin{cases}
(7n+1)+L,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
(7n+2)+L,&\lambda=-1.
\end{cases}
$$

For $\lambda=1$, $G=xU$ and $F=\frac{U}{2}$ has initial monomial $u^{14}s$. Thus before wrapping the initial algebra is generated by $u^4,u^6,u^9,u^{14}s$. At the wrap,
$$
(u^{14}s)^n=u^{14n+1}\in B
$$
because $14n+1>11$, the largest gap of $H$. Hence powers of $u^{14}s$ reduce back into $B$, so no new initial generator appears and $Q_r(1)=14r+H$.

Step 3: Compute the conductor levels
For each row let $g_r=\max(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus Q_r)$ and $m_r=r+ng_r$. Then $\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max_r m_r$. For $\lambda^2\neq1$, the nonfinal positive rows have
$$
g_r=7r+13\quad(r\text{ odd}),\qquad g_r=7r+15\quad(r\text{ even}),
$$
and for $\lambda=-1$ both values increase by $1$. Successive $m_r$ increase alternately by $1+9n$ and $1+5n$. When $n$ is odd, replacing $K$ by $L$ lowers the final quotient gap by $4$, and the final increment remains $1+5n$. Hence the last row is maximal and
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=7n^2+\left(6+(-1)^{n}\right)n\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1),
$$
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n.
$$
For $\lambda=1$, $g_r=14r+11$, so
$$
\kappa_{n,1}=1+(n-1)+n\left(14(n-1)+11\right)=14n^2-2n.
$$

Step 4: Compare the strata and identify the second extremum
For every $n\geq2$,
$$
\kappa_{n,1}-\kappa_{n,-1}
=n\left(7n-9-(-1)^{n}\right)>0,
$$
while
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}-\kappa_{n,\lambda}=n>0\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1).
$$
When $n=2$, the first-row sets $13+K,14+K,14+H$ give conductors $42,44,52$, so the same formulas hold at the smallest value. Since $\lambda^2=1$ has only $1,-1$, the unique largest conductor occurs at $1$ and the unique second-largest at $-1$. Therefore
$$
\beta_n=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n,\qquad E_n=\{-1\}.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n,\{-1\}\right)}$

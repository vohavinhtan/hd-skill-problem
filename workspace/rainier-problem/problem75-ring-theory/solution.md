## Steps

Step 1: Convert the conductor to residue-row gaps
Put $u=t^n$ and $s=t$, so $s^n=u$, and write
$$
x=u^4,\qquad
y=u^6+u^7+u^7s,\qquad
z=u^9+\left(\lambda^2u^9+(\lambda-1)u^{11}\right)s.
$$
Let
$$
S_{n,\lambda}=\{v(f):0\neq f\in A_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Because $\mathbb C[[t]]$ is finite over $\mathbb C[[x]]=\mathbb C[[t^{4n}]]$, the ring $A_{n,\lambda}$ is a complete finite $\mathbb C[[x]]$-module. If every integer at least $c$ lies in $S_{n,\lambda}$, choose for each $k\geq c$ an element $f_k=t^k+$ higher terms and cancel successively; completeness gives $t^k\in A_{n,\lambda}$. Hence $\kappa_{n,\lambda}$ is one plus the largest integer missing from $S_{n,\lambda}$.

For $0\leq r<n$ define
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=\{q\geq0:qn+r\in S_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Set
$$
H=\langle4,6,9\rangle,\qquad K=H\cup(5+H),\qquad L=K\cup(7+H).
$$
Their complements are
$$
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus H=\{1,2,3,5,7,11\},\qquad
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus K=\{1,2,3,7\},\qquad
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus L=\{1,2,3\},
$$
so their largest gaps are $11,7,3$.

Step 2: Establish the residue rows with one standard-basis transition
Before the wrap $s^n=u$, order monomials $u^qs^r$ by the pair $(q,r)$; for $0\leq r<n$ this agrees with $t$-adic order. The relations among the base weights $4,6,9$ are generated by
$$
Y^2-X^3,\qquad Z^2-X^3Y,
$$
because reducing by them leaves the four weights $0,6,9,15$, distinct modulo $4$.

Put
$$
U=y^2-x^3-2xz-x^2y,\qquad G=z^2-x^3y.
$$
The terms relevant to the first two rows are
$$
U=-u^{15}+\left(2(1-\lambda^2)u^{13}+2u^{14}+(1-2\lambda)u^{15}\right)s+u^{14}s^2
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
G&=-u^{19}+\left(2\lambda^2u^{18}-u^{19}+2(\lambda-1)u^{20}\right)s\\
&+\left(\lambda^4u^{18}+2\lambda^2(\lambda-1)u^{20}+(\lambda-1)^2u^{22}\right)s^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the first positive row is $13+K$ when $\lambda^2\neq1$ and $14+K$ when $\lambda=-1$; for the latter use $G-xU$ as the second generator.

The remaining rows follow from a single period-two standard-basis transition. Write
$$
\eta=
\begin{cases}
0,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
1,&\lambda=-1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
a_r=7r+7+\eta+(-1)^r.
$$
For each positive row choose normalized generators $F_r,G_r$ with initial monomials $u^{a_r}s^r$ and $u^{a_r+5}s^r$. Since
$$
H\cap(5+H)=(9+H)\cup(14+H),
$$
there are only two primitive same-row syzygies. When $r$ is odd they are represented by
$$
zF_r-xG_r,\qquad zG_r-x^2yF_r,
$$
and their full normal forms create the next row at $a_r+9$ and $a_r+14$. When $r=2j$ is even, those same-row syzygies already reduce by the preceding odd step; the new adjacent-row pair is $F_1F_{2j-1}-xF_{2j},F_1G_{2j-1}-xG_{2j}$ for $\lambda^2\neq1$, and $F_1F_{2j-1}-G_{2j},F_1G_{2j-1}-xyF_{2j}$ for $\lambda=-1$. Its full normal forms create the next row at $a_r+5$ and $a_r+10$. Hence in both cases the next row is $a_{r+1}+K$, with $a_{r+2}=a_r+14$.

This also gives completeness. A critical pair inside one row is an $H$-multiple of one of the two primitive overlaps above. A critical pair between adjacent rows is generated by the corresponding adjacent-row pair. If the largest row indices differ by at least two, replace the higher one using the preceding adjacent relation; because $a_{r+2}-a_r=14\in H$, this strictly lowers the row separation. Induction reduces every initial relation to these two local types.

The higher $s$-terms cannot create a hidden parameter stratum. After normalizing each new pair to leading coefficient $1$, the two coefficients exposed by an odd step $r=2j+1$ and an even step $r=2j$ are, respectively,
$$
\frac{\lambda^2}{2j+2}(2j+1,2j+3),\qquad
-\frac{1}{\lambda^2-1}\left(\frac{(2j-1)(j+1)}{2j(2j+1)},\frac12\right)
$$
for $\lambda^2\neq1$, while for $\lambda=-1$ they are
$$
\frac{1}{2j+2}(2j+3,2j+1),\qquad
\left(\frac{j+1}{2j},\frac{2j-1}{2(2j+1)}\right).
$$
Here $j\geq0$ in the odd step and $j\geq1$ in the even step. Every displayed entry is nonzero on its stated stratum. Hence no later critical pair can vanish, and the standard-basis induction gives
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=a_r+K
$$
for every positive row before the final wrap.

If $n$ is odd, the last row $r=n-1$ is even and the final reduction reaches an $s^n$-term; substituting $s^n=u$ adds exactly the translate $7+H$, so only that last row has $K$ replaced by $L$. If $n$ is even, the last row is odd and no extra translate appears.

For $\lambda=1$, the exact identity $G=xU$ collapses the second first-row generator. With $F=U/2$, the candidate initial algebra is generated by
$$
u^4,\qquad u^6,\qquad u^9,\qquad u^{14}s.
$$
Any binomial relation with positive $s$-degree has the same power of $u^{14}s$ on both sides; after factoring it, the remaining relation is among $u^4,u^6,u^9$ and is generated by the two base relations above. Their lifts reduce by $U$ and $G=xU$. Thus the standard-basis criterion closes without any new initial generator, and
$$
Q_r(1)=14r+H\qquad(0\leq r<n).
$$
When $n=2$, one substitutes $s^2=u$ before forming row $2$; there is no $Q_2$.

Step 3: Compute the two nonunit conductor levels
For each row let
$$
g_r=\max\left(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus Q_r\right),\qquad m_r=r+ng_r.
$$
Then
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max_{0\leq r<n}m_r.
$$
For the two strata encoded by $\eta$, Step 2 gives, away from the odd-$n$ boundary row,
$$
g_r=7r+14+\eta+(-1)^r\qquad(r\geq1).
$$
Thus $m_1>m_0$, and thereafter
$$
m_{r+1}-m_r=
\begin{cases}
1+9n,&r\text{ odd},\\
1+5n,&r\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
$$
If $n$ is odd, replacing $K$ by $L$ in the last row lowers its quotient gap by $4$, and the final increment is still $1+5n$. Hence the maximum is always attained at $r=n-1$. Therefore
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}
=7n^2+\left(6+\eta+(-1)^n\right)n.
$$
In particular,
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=7n^2+\left(6+(-1)^n\right)n
\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1),
$$
while
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n.
$$

Step 4: Compute the $\lambda=1$ level and compare the strata
For $\lambda=1$,
$$
Q_r(1)=14r+H,
$$
so
$$
g_r=14r+11
$$
and $m_{r+1}-m_r=1+14n>0$. Hence
$$
\kappa_{n,1}
=1+(n-1)+n\left(14(n-1)+11\right)
=14n^2-2n.
$$
For $n\geq3$,
$$
\kappa_{n,1}-\kappa_{n,-1}
=n\left(7n-9-(-1)^n\right)>0,
$$
and
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}-\kappa_{n,\lambda}=n>0
\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1).
$$
Thus for $n\geq3$ the three conductor levels are strictly ordered with $\lambda=1$ first and $\lambda=-1$ second.

Step 5: Check $n=2$ and identify the second extremum
For $n=2$,
$$
Q_0=H,
$$
while
$$
Q_1=13+K,\qquad14+K,\qquad14+H
$$
for $\lambda^2\neq1$, $\lambda=-1$, and $\lambda=1$, respectively. Their largest missing quotients are $20,21,25$, so the largest missing valuations are $41,43,51$. Therefore the three conductors are
$$
42,\qquad44,\qquad52.
$$
This agrees with the formulas above.

Finally $\lambda^2=1$ has only the two solutions $1$ and $-1$. Hence $\lambda=1$ gives the unique largest conductor and $\lambda=-1$ the unique second-largest conductor. Therefore
$$
\beta_n=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n,\qquad E_n=\{-1\}.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n,\{-1\}\right)}$

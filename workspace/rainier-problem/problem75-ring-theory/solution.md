## Steps

Step 1: Reduce the conductor to residue-class valuation gaps
Put $u=t^n$ and $s=t$, so $s^n=u$, and write
$$
x=u^4,\qquad
y=u^6+u^7+u^7s,\qquad
z=u^9+\left(\lambda^2u^9+(\lambda-1)u^{11}\right)s.
$$
Let
$$
S_{n,\lambda}=\{v(f):0\neq f\in A_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Since $x=t^{4n}$, the ring $\mathbb C[[t]]$ is finite over $\mathbb C[[x]]$. Hence $y,z$ are integral over $\mathbb C[[x]]$, and the image $A_{n,\lambda}$ is a finite complete $\mathbb C[[x]]$-module. If every integer at least $c$ belongs to $S_{n,\lambda}$, choose for each residue $\rho$ modulo $4n$ an element
$$
b_\rho=t^{m_\rho}+\text{ higher terms}\in A_{n,\lambda}
$$
with $m_\rho\geq c$ minimal in that residue. For $k=m_\rho+4nj$, subtracting a suitable multiple of $x^{j}b_\rho$ from $t^k$ raises the valuation. Repetition converges in the complete module, so $t^k\in A_{n,\lambda}$. Therefore
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max\left(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus S_{n,\lambda}\right).
$$

For $0\leq r<n$, put
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=\{q\geq0:qn+r\in S_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Set
$$
H=\langle4,6,9\rangle,\qquad K=H\cup(5+H),\qquad L=K\cup(7+H).
$$
The gaps of $H,K,L$ are respectively
$$
\{1,2,3,5,7,11\},\qquad \{1,2,3,7\},\qquad \{1,2,3\},
$$
so their largest gaps are $11,7,3$.

Step 2: Derive the residue layers by complete standard-basis induction
Let
$$
B=\mathbb C[[u^4,u^6,u^9]],\qquad I=B+u^5B.
$$
If $p,q\in B$ satisfy $p+u^5q=0$, then $p\in B\cap u^5B$. Since
$$
H\cap(5+H)=(9+H)\cup(14+H),
$$
we have
$$
B\cap u^5B=u^9B+u^{14}B.
$$
Thus $p=u^9a+u^{14}b$ and $q=-u^4a-u^9b$ for some $a,b\in B$. Hence every relation between the two generators of $I$ is a $B$-linear combination of
$$
(u^9,-u^4),\qquad(u^{14},-u^9).
$$
Consequently, once two generators of a row have leading exponents differing by $5$, every same-row cancellation is reduced by these two relations; repeated reduction is exhaustive because each subtraction raises the $u$-weight and $B$ is complete.

The relations among the base weights $4,6,9$ are generated by
$$
Y^2-X^3,\qquad Z^2-X^3Y,
$$
since reducing the exponents of $Y,Z$ to $0$ or $1$ leaves the four weights $0,6,9,15$, distinct modulo $4$. Put
$$
U=y^2-x^3-2xz-x^2y,\qquad G=z^2-x^3y.
$$
Their exact expansions are
$$
U=-u^{15}+\left(2(1-\lambda^2)u^{13}+2u^{14}+(1-2\lambda)u^{15}\right)s+u^{14}s^2
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
G&=-u^{19}+\left(2\lambda^2u^{18}-u^{19}+2(\lambda-1)u^{20}\right)s\\
&+\left(\lambda^4u^{18}+2\lambda^2(\lambda-1)u^{20}+(\lambda-1)^2u^{22}\right)s^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

First let $D=\lambda^2\neq1$. Normalize
$$
F_1=\frac{U}{2(1-D)},\qquad G_1=\frac{G}{2D},
$$
and write $a_r=7r+7+(-1)^{r}$. After reducing by earlier rows, choose $F_r,G_r$ with initials $u^{a_r}s^r,u^{a_r+5}s^r$. Define
$$
A_r=[u^{a_r+1}s^r]F_r,\quad
B_r=[u^{a_r+1}s^{r+1}]F_r,\quad
C_r=[u^{a_r+5}s^{r+1}]G_r.
$$
The induction claim is
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
& A_r&B_r&C_r\\ \hline
r\text{ odd}&-\dfrac{r}{D-1}&-\dfrac{r}{(r+1)(D-1)}&\dfrac{D}{r+1}\\
r\text{ even}&-\dfrac{rD+1}{D(D-1)}&-\dfrac{1}{D-1}&\dfrac{D}{r+1}.
\end{array}
$$
For $r=1$ this follows from $F_1,G_1$. If $r$ is odd, the two syzygies lift to
$$
zF_r-xG_r,\qquad zG_r-x^2yF_r.
$$
Their first surviving row-$r+1$ coefficients are
$$
D-C_r=\frac{Dr}{r+1},\qquad D+C_r=\frac{D(r+2)}{r+1},
$$
at weights $a_r+9,a_r+14$. Dividing by these nonzero coefficients gives the next generators. Their next coefficients are
$$
A_{r+1}=-\frac{(r+1)D+1}{D(D-1)},\qquad
B_{r+1}=-\frac{1}{D-1},\qquad
C_{r+1}=\frac{D}{r+2},
$$
which is the even line. Thus an odd row shifts by $9$.

Now let $r\geq2$ be even. Since $u^{10}\in B$, we have $I^2=I$. Also $a_1+a_{r-1}=a_r+4$, so every adjacent-row relation is reduced, modulo the internal syzygies of $I$, by
$$
F_1F_{r-1}-xF_r,\qquad F_1G_{r-1}-xG_r.
$$
For the first pair the residual $G_r$-coefficient is
$$
A_1+A_{r-1}-A_r=\frac{1}{D(D-1)},
$$
and after reducing that column the first new coefficient is
$$
-\frac{(r-1)(r+2)}{2r(r+1)(D-1)}\neq0.
$$
For the second pair the first residual coefficient is $D/(r(r+1))$, and reduction by the first new generator leaves
$$
-\frac{1}{2(D-1)}\neq0.
$$
The resulting weights are $a_r+5,a_r+10$, and normalization gives
$$
A_{r+1}=-\frac{r+1}{D-1},\qquad
B_{r+1}=-\frac{r+1}{(r+2)(D-1)},\qquad
C_{r+1}=\frac{D}{r+2},
$$
which is the odd line with $r$ replaced by $r+1$. Thus an even row shifts by $5$, proving
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=7r+7+(-1)^{r}+K
$$
for every positive row before wrapping.

For $\lambda=-1$, take
$$
F_1=\frac{U}{2},\qquad G_1=-\frac{G-xU}{4},\qquad a_r=7r+8+(-1)^{r}.
$$
After reduction by earlier rows, normalize so that
$$
F_r=u^{a_r}s^r+\frac{1}{r+1}u^{a_r}s^{r+1}+\text{ reducible terms},
$$
$$
G_r=u^{a_r+5}s^r+u^{a_r+6}s^r+u^{a_r+6}s^{r+1}+\text{ reducible terms}.
$$
This holds for $r=1$ from the displayed expansions of $U$ and $G-xU$. For odd $r$, the same two lifted syzygies have first new coefficients
$$
1+\frac{1}{r+1}=\frac{r+2}{r+1},\qquad
1-\frac{1}{r+1}=\frac{r}{r+1}.
$$
After division by these coefficients, the first generator has next-row coefficient $1/(r+2)$, while the second again has the two unit coefficients displayed above; hence the invariant is restored for $r+1$. For even $r$, $I^2=I$ reduces every adjacent relation to
$$
F_1F_{r-1}-G_r,\qquad F_1G_{r-1}-xyF_r.
$$
Their first new coefficients are
$$
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{r+2}{2r},\qquad
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r+1}=\frac{r-1}{2(r+1)}.
$$
After normalization, the first new generator again has coefficient $1/(r+2)$ in the next row and the second again has unit coefficients at the two displayed $u$-columns. Thus the invariant is restored, so the shifts again alternate by $9,5$ and
$$
Q_r(-1)=7r+8+(-1)^{r}+K
$$
before wrapping.

No further relation creates a third generator. Same-row relations reduce by the proved syzygy description of $I$, adjacent-row relations by $I^2=I$, and if two row indices differ by at least two, the already constructed adjacent relation for the larger index reduces the separation because $a_{r+2}=a_r+14$ and $14\in H$. Induction on the separation reaches one of the two handled cases.

Now impose $s^n=u$. If $n$ is even, the last row is odd, and its next-row outputs have offsets $9,14$; after wrapping these become offsets $10,15$, both in $K$, so no new translate appears. If $n$ is odd, the last row $r=n-1$ is even. Its even-to-odd computation has prospective next-row weights $a_r+5$ and $a_r+10$. Replacing $s^n$ by $u$ lowers the residue from $n$ to $0$ and raises the $u$-weight by $1$, so these become offsets $6$ and $11$ from $a_r$. The offset $6$ lies in $H$ and is already present, while $11=4+7$ contributes the new translate $7+H$. Any further wrapped term differs from one of these by an element of $H$, so it is already in $K$ or in $7+H$. Therefore the final even row is obtained by replacing $K$ with $L$:
$$
Q_{n-1}=\begin{cases}
(7n+1)+L,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
(7n+2)+L,&\lambda=-1.
\end{cases}
$$

For $\lambda=1$, $G=xU$. With $F=U/2$, the initial algebra before wrapping is generated by $u^4,u^6,u^9,u^{14}s$, so $Q_r(1)=14r+H$. At the wrap,
$$
(u^{14}s)^n=u^{14n+1},
$$
and $14n+1>11$, so this exponent lies in $H$ and creates no new generator.

Step 3: Compute the nonunit conductor levels
For each row let
$$
g_r=\max(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus Q_r),\qquad m_r=r+ng_r.
$$
Then $\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max_r m_r$. For $\lambda^2\neq1$ the nonfinal rows have
$$
g_r=7r+13\quad(r\text{ odd}),\qquad g_r=7r+15\quad(r\text{ even}),
$$
and for $\lambda=-1$ these values are larger by $1$. The differences $m_{r+1}-m_r$ alternate between $1+9n$ and $1+5n$, so they are positive. When $n$ is odd, replacing $K$ by $L$ in the last row lowers its quotient gap by $4$, and the final difference remains $1+5n$. Thus the last row is maximal. Consequently
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=7n^2+\left(6+(-1)^{n}\right)n\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1),
$$
and
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n.
$$

Step 4: Compute the $\lambda=1$ level and compare
For $\lambda=1$, the largest missing quotient in row $r$ is $14r+11$, and successive row maxima differ by $1+14n$. Hence
$$
\kappa_{n,1}=1+(n-1)+n\left(14(n-1)+11\right)=14n^2-2n.
$$
For every $n\geq2$,
$$
\kappa_{n,1}-\kappa_{n,-1}=n\left(7n-9-(-1)^{n}\right)>0,
$$
while
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}-\kappa_{n,\lambda}=n>0\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1).
$$

Step 5: Check the smallest case and identify the second extremum
When $n=2$ there is no row $Q_2$. Directly from the first-row calculation,
$$
Q_1=13+K,\qquad14+K,\qquad14+H
$$
for $\lambda^2\neq1$, $\lambda=-1$, and $\lambda=1$, respectively. Their largest missing quotients are $20,21,25$, giving conductors $42,44,52$, exactly as the formulas above predict.

The equation $\lambda^2=1$ has only $1$ and $-1$. Thus $\lambda=1$ gives the unique largest conductor and $\lambda=-1$ gives the unique second-largest one. Hence
$$
\beta_n=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n,\qquad E_n=\{-1\}.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n,\{-1\}\right)}$

## Steps

Step 1: Reduce the conductor to residue-class valuation gaps
Put $u=t^n$ and $s=t$, so $s^n=u$, and write
$$
x=u^4,\qquad
y=u^6+u^7+u^7s,\qquad
z=u^9+\left(\lambda^2u^9+(\lambda-1)u^{11}\right)s.
$$
Let
$$
S_{n,\lambda}=\{v(f):0\neq f\in A_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
The extension $\mathbb C[[t]]/\mathbb C[[x]]$ is finite because $x=t^{4n}$. Thus $y$ and $z$ are integral over $\mathbb C[[x]]$. Let
$$
R=\mathbb C[[x]][y,z].
$$
Then $R$ is a finite, complete $\mathbb C[[x]]$-module. Polynomial truncations of a source series map into $R$ and converge $t$-adically to its image; completeness puts the limit in $R$. Hence $A_{n,\lambda}=R$. Suppose every integer at least $c$ occurs in $S_{n,\lambda}$. For each residue $\rho$ modulo $4n$, choose
$$
b_\rho=t^{m_\rho}+\text{ higher terms}\in A_{n,\lambda}
$$
with $m_\rho\geq c$ minimal in that residue. If $k\geq c$ and $k=m_\rho+4nj$, subtracting a suitable multiple of $x^{j}b_\rho$ from $t^k$ raises the valuation. Repeating this process gives a $t$-adically convergent sum in the complete module $A_{n,\lambda}$, so $t^k\in A_{n,\lambda}$. The reverse implication is immediate. Therefore
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}
=
1+\max\left(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus S_{n,\lambda}\right).
$$

For $0\leq r<n$, set
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=\{q\geq0:qn+r\in S_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Also put
$$
H=\langle4,6,9\rangle,\qquad
K=H\cup(5+H),\qquad
L=K\cup(7+H).
$$
Their gaps are
$$
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus H=\{1,2,3,5,7,11\},
$$
$$
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus K=\{1,2,3,7\},
\qquad
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus L=\{1,2,3\}.
$$
Their largest gaps are $11,7,3$.

Step 2: Determine the residue layers with a two-generator certificate
Let
$$
B=\mathbb C[[u^4,u^6,u^9]],\qquad I=B+u^{5}B.
$$
The value sets of $B$ and the fractional $B$-ideal $I$ are $H$ and $K$. Since
$$
H\cap(5+H)=\{9,13,14,15\}\cup\{17,18,19,\ldots\}
=(9+H)\cup(14+H),
$$
the syzygy module of the two generators $1,u^5$ is generated by
$$
(u^9,-u^4),\qquad(-u^{14},u^9).
$$
Thus every cancellation inside a translate of $I$ is generated by two primitive syzygies.

The relations among monomials of weights $4,6,9$ are generated by
$$
Y^2-X^3,\qquad Z^2-X^3Y.
$$
Indeed, reducing by these two relations leaves the four weights $0,6,9,15$, which are distinct modulo $4$. Set
$$
U=y^2-x^3-2xz-x^2y,\qquad G=z^2-x^3y.
$$
Their exact expansions are
$$
U=-u^{15}
+\left(2(1-\lambda^2)u^{13}+2u^{14}+(1-2\lambda)u^{15}\right)s
+u^{14}s^2
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
G&=-u^{19}
+\left(2\lambda^2u^{18}-u^{19}+2(\lambda-1)u^{20}\right)s\\
&+\left(\lambda^4u^{18}
+2\lambda^2(\lambda-1)u^{20}
+(\lambda-1)^2u^{22}\right)s^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because the two base relations generate every equal-weight relation, their lifts generate every first positive correction. If $\lambda^2\neq1$, the first positive layer has generator weights $13,18$, so $Q_1=13+K$. If $\lambda=-1$, the pair $U,G-xU$ has generator weights $14,19$, so $Q_1=14+K$. If $\lambda=1$, the exact identity $G=xU$ leaves only the principal layer $14+H$.

It remains to prove that no later cancellation changes these two nonunit patterns. Work modulo all layers already obtained and normalize the two minimal generators of an odd layer to leading coefficient $1$. If its first weight is $A$, the two primitive syzygies above can create new primitive weights only at $A+9,A+14$ in the next layer; multiplication by the first positive layer can then create new primitive weights only at $A+14,A+19$ in the layer after that. Let $c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4$ be the four normalized coefficients in those positions. Writing $D=\lambda^2$, coefficient comparison in these four positions gives, for $D\neq1$,
$$
(r+1)c_1=Dr,\qquad
(r+1)c_2=D(r+2),
$$
$$
2(r+1)(r+2)(D-1)c_3=-r(r+3),\qquad
2(D-1)c_4=-1,
$$
where $r\geq1$ is odd. For $\lambda=-1$, the same four positions give
$$
(r+1)c_1=r+2,\qquad
(r+1)c_2=r,
$$
$$
2(r+1)c_3=r+3,\qquad
2(r+2)c_4=r.
$$
These equations are the complete two-layer coefficient check: no coefficient outside these four positions can create a new primitive value because every other candidate cancellation is an $H$-multiple of the two syzygies already listed. Since $\lambda\neq0$, every $c_i$ is nonzero on its stated stratum. The first transition shifts the two-generator layer by $9$, the second shifts it by $5$, and a full two-layer cycle shifts it by $14$ without producing a third primitive generator.

Starting from $Q_1$, induction gives, for every positive nonfinal layer,
$$
Q_r(\lambda)
=
7r+7+\varepsilon+(-1)^{r}+K,
$$
where $\varepsilon=0$ for $\lambda^2\neq1$ and $\varepsilon=1$ for $\lambda=-1$. When $n$ is odd, the last layer $r=n-1$ is even. The final coefficient check reaches $s^n$; after $s^n=u$, its nonzero wrapped term contributes exactly the additional translate $7+H$, so $K$ is replaced there by $L$. When $n$ is even, the last layer is odd and no wrapped translate occurs. The same reduction shows that any wrap landing in $Q_0$ has $q>11$, so the largest gap of $Q_0$ remains $11$.

For $\lambda=1$, put $F=U/2$. Before wrapping, the initial algebra is generated by
$$
u^4,\qquad u^6,\qquad u^9,\qquad u^{14}s.
$$
A relation of positive $s$-degree has the same power of $u^{14}s$ on both sides; after factoring it, the remaining relation is one of the two base relations above, whose lifts reduce by $U$ and $G=xU$. At the only possible wrap,
$$
(u^{14}s)^n=u^{14n+1}.
$$
Since $14n+1\geq29$ and $H$ has no gap above $11$, this exponent already lies in $H$. No new initial generator is created, and
$$
Q_r(1)=14r+H
\qquad(0\leq r<n).
$$

Step 3: Compute the three conductor levels
For a row $Q_r$, let $g_r$ be its largest missing $q$-value and put
$$
m_r=r+ng_r.
$$
Then
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max_{0\leq r<n}m_r.
$$
For $\lambda\neq1$, away from the odd-$n$ boundary layer, Step 2 gives
$$
g_r=7r+14+\varepsilon+(-1)^{r}
\qquad(r\geq1).
$$
Also $g_0=11$, so $m_1\geq1+20n>11n=m_0$. For later rows,
$$
m_{r+1}-m_r=
\begin{cases}
1+9n,&r\text{ odd},\\
1+5n,&r\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
$$
Both increments are positive. If $n$ is odd, replacing $K$ by $L$ lowers the last quotient gap by $4$, so the final increment is still $1+5n$. Thus the last row contains the largest missing valuation.

If $n$ is even, the last row is odd and
$$
g_{n-1}=7n+6+\varepsilon.
$$
If $n$ is odd, the last row uses $L$ and
$$
g_{n-1}=7n+4+\varepsilon.
$$
Both cases give
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}
=
7n^2+\left(6+\varepsilon+(-1)^{n}\right)n
\qquad(\lambda\neq1).
$$
Therefore
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}
=
7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n,
$$
while
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}
=
7n^2+\left(6+(-1)^{n}\right)n
\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1).
$$

For $\lambda=1$, Step 2 gives $g_r=14r+11$ and
$$
m_{r+1}-m_r=1+14n>0.
$$
The last row is maximal, so
$$
\kappa_{n,1}
=
1+(n-1)+n\left(14(n-1)+11\right)
=
14n^2-2n.
$$

Step 4: Compare the strata and identify the second extremum
For every $n\geq2$,
$$
\kappa_{n,1}-\kappa_{n,-1}
=
n\left(7n-9-(-1)^{n}\right)>0,
$$
and
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}-\kappa_{n,\lambda}=n>0
\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1).
$$
For the boundary value $n=2$, the three formulas give conductors $42,44,52$ for $\lambda^2\neq1$, $\lambda=-1$, and $\lambda=1$, respectively; these also follow directly from $Q_0=H$ and $Q_1=13+K,14+K,14+H$.

The equation $\lambda^2=1$ has exactly the two solutions $1$ and $-1$ in $\mathbb C^\times$. Hence $\lambda=1$ gives the unique largest conductor and $\lambda=-1$ gives the unique second-largest conductor. Therefore
$$
\beta_n=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n,
\qquad
E_n=\{-1\}.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^{n}\right)n,\{-1\}\right)}$

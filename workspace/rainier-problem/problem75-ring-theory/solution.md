## Steps

Step 1: Convert the conductor to residue-row gaps
Put $u=t^n$ and $s=t$, so $s^n=u$, and write
$$
x=u^4,\qquad
y=u^6+u^7+u^7s,\qquad
z=u^9+\left(\lambda^2u^9+(\lambda-1)u^{11}\right)s.
$$
Let $S_{n,\lambda}=\{v(f):0\neq f\in A_{n,\lambda}\}$. Since $A_{n,\lambda}$ is a complete finite $\mathbb C[[x]]$-module, the usual successive cancellation of leading terms shows that $\kappa_{n,\lambda}$ is one plus the largest integer missing from $S_{n,\lambda}$.

For $0\leq r<n$ put
$$
Q_r(\lambda)=\{q\geq0:qn+r\in S_{n,\lambda}\}.
$$
Also set
$$
H=\langle4,6,9\rangle,\qquad K=H\cup(5+H),\qquad L=K\cup(7+H).
$$
Then
$$
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus H=\{1,2,3,5,7,11\},\qquad
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus K=\{1,2,3,7\},\qquad
\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus L=\{1,2,3\}.
$$
Thus the largest gaps of $H,K,L$ are $11,7,3$.

Step 2: Prove the residue-row pattern by standard-basis induction
For $r<n$, order monomials $u^qs^r$ by the pair $(q,r)$ lexicographically; this is the $t$-adic order because $qn+r<(q+1)n+r'$ for $0\leq r,r'<n$. Before the final wrap $s^n=u$, write $\operatorname{in}(f)$ for the least monomial in this order. A finite set is a standard basis up to row $r$ if every cancellation among products of its initial monomials reduces, using the full power-series generators, to terms whose initial monomials are already listed. This criterion is enough because the least uncancelled monomial of any polynomial expression gives a relation among initial monomials, and induction on that monomial reduces every element.

The only relations among the base weights $4,6,9$ are generated by
$$
Y^2-X^3,\qquad Z^2-X^3Y,
$$
since these reduce the exponents of $Y,Z$ to $0$ or $1$ and leave weights $0,6,9,15$, distinct modulo $4$. Put
$$
U=y^2-x^3-2xz-x^2y,\qquad G=z^2-x^3y.
$$
Their full expansions are
$$
U=-u^{15}+\left(2(1-\lambda^2)u^{13}+2u^{14}+(1-2\lambda)u^{15}\right)s+u^{14}s^2
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
G&=-u^{19}+\left(2\lambda^2u^{18}-u^{19}+2(\lambda-1)u^{20}\right)s\\
&+\left(\lambda^4u^{18}+2\lambda^2(\lambda-1)u^{20}+(\lambda-1)^2u^{22}\right)s^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

First suppose $\lambda^2\neq1$. Normalize
$$
F_1=\frac{U}{2(1-\lambda^2)},\qquad G_1=\frac{G}{2\lambda^2},
$$
so $\operatorname{in}(F_1)=u^{13}s$ and $\operatorname{in}(G_1)=u^{18}s$. For $r\geq1$ define normalized $F_r,G_r$ recursively by complete reduction with respect to all earlier rows. Write
$$
a_{2j+1}=14j+13,\qquad a_{2j}=14j+8.
$$
The induction claim is
$$
\operatorname{in}(F_r)=u^{a_r}s^r,\qquad
\operatorname{in}(G_r)=u^{a_r+5}s^r.
$$
If $r=2j+1$ is odd, the only primitive same-row overlaps are
$$
zF_r-xG_r,\qquad zG_r-x^2yF_r,
$$
because
$$
H\cap(5+H)=(9+H)\cup(14+H).
$$
Reducing the full expressions, including every higher $s$-term of $y$ and $z$, gives
$$
\operatorname{in}\operatorname{NF}(zF_{2j+1}-xG_{2j+1})
=\frac{(2j+1)\lambda^2}{2j+2}u^{a_{2j+2}}s^{2j+2},
$$
$$
\operatorname{in}\operatorname{NF}(zG_{2j+1}-x^2yF_{2j+1})
=\frac{(2j+3)\lambda^2}{2j+2}u^{a_{2j+2}+5}s^{2j+2}.
$$
If $r=2j$ is even, the new primitive overlaps are instead the adjacent-row relations
$$
F_1F_{2j-1}-xF_{2j},\qquad
F_1G_{2j-1}-xG_{2j}.
$$
After adjoining the first normal form before reducing the second, their initial terms are
$$
-\frac{(2j-1)(j+1)}{2j(2j+1)(\lambda^2-1)}u^{a_{2j+1}}s^{2j+1},
$$
$$
-\frac{1}{2(\lambda^2-1)}u^{a_{2j+1}+5}s^{2j+1}.
$$
These four displayed coefficients are nonzero for every $\lambda\in\mathbb C^\times$ with $\lambda^2\neq1$, so no further parameter stratum can occur. The induction is exhaustive: same-row relations are generated by the two primitive overlaps above; relations between adjacent rows are generated by the displayed adjacent-row pair; and a relation whose maximal row indices differ by at least two is reduced to one of these by replacing the higher row through the preceding adjacent relation, since $a_{r+2}=a_r+14$ and $14\in H$. Thus
$$
Q_{2j+1}=14j+13+K,\qquad
Q_{2j}=14j+8+K
$$
for all positive nonfinal rows in their allowed ranges.

For $\lambda=-1$, set
$$
F_1=\frac U2,\qquad G_1=-\frac{G-xU}{4}.
$$
Now
$$
a_{2j+1}=14j+14,\qquad a_{2j}=14j+9.
$$
The same odd-row overlaps have full normal-form coefficients
$$
\frac{2j+3}{2j+2},\qquad \frac{2j+1}{2j+2},
$$
while for an even row the primitive adjacent relations are
$$
F_1F_{2j-1}-G_{2j},\qquad F_1G_{2j-1}-xyF_{2j},
$$
with full normal-form coefficients
$$
\frac{j+1}{2j},\qquad \frac{2j-1}{2(2j+1)}.
$$
All are nonzero, so the same standard-basis induction gives
$$
Q_{2j+1}=14j+14+K,\qquad
Q_{2j}=14j+9+K.
$$

These formulas are used only when the displayed row index is $<n$. If $n$ is odd, the last row $r=n-1$ is even and the final reduction reaches $s^n$; substituting $s^n=u$ adds exactly the extra translate $7+H$. Hence
$$
Q_{n-1}=
\begin{cases}
(7n+1)+L,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
(7n+2)+L,&\lambda=-1.
\end{cases}
$$
If $n$ is even, the last row is odd and no extra translate appears.

For $\lambda=1$, $G=xU$. With $F=U/2$ we have $\operatorname{in}(F)=u^{14}s$. The initial algebra is generated by $u^4,u^6,u^9,u^{14}s$: any equality with a fixed positive $s$-degree has the same power of $u^{14}s$ on both sides and therefore reduces to one of the two base relations. Thus no new initial generator occurs, and
$$
Q_r(1)=14r+H\qquad(0\leq r<n).
$$
When $n=2$ one substitutes $s^2=u$ before forming row $2$; there is no $Q_2$.

Step 3: Compute the conductor for $\lambda=-1$
For each row put
$$
g_r=\max\left(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\setminus Q_r\right),\qquad m_r=r+ng_r.
$$
Then
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=1+\max_{0\leq r<n}m_r.
$$
For $\lambda=-1$ and $n\geq3$,
$$
g_0=11,\qquad
g_r=7r+14\ (r\text{ odd}),\qquad
g_r=7r+16\ (r\text{ even},\ 2\leq r\leq n-2).
$$
Thus $m_1-m_0=1+10n>0$, and later increments alternate between $1+9n$ and $1+5n$. The odd-$n$ boundary row also has the final increment $1+5n$, so the maximum is at $r=n-1$. Therefore
$$
\kappa_{n,-1}=
\begin{cases}
7n^2+8n,&n\text{ even},\\
7n^2+6n,&n\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
=
7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n.
$$

Step 4: Compute the other conductor levels
For $\lambda^2\neq1$ and $n\geq3$,
$$
g_0=11,\qquad
g_r=7r+13\ (r\text{ odd}),\qquad
g_r=7r+15\ (r\text{ even},\ 2\leq r\leq n-2).
$$
The same positive increments show that the last row is maximal, including the odd-$n$ boundary correction. Hence
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}=7n^2+\left(6+(-1)^n\right)n.
$$
For $\lambda=1$, Step 2 gives $g_r=14r+11$, so $m_{r+1}-m_r=1+14n>0$ and
$$
\kappa_{n,1}=14n^2-2n.
$$
For $n\geq3$,
$$
\kappa_{n,1}-\kappa_{n,-1}
=
\begin{cases}
n(7n-10),&n\text{ even},\\
n(7n-8),&n\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\kappa_{n,-1}-\kappa_{n,\lambda}=n,
$$
so both differences are positive.

Step 5: Verify $n=2$ and identify the second extremum
For $n=2$,
$$
Q_0=H,\qquad
Q_1=
\begin{cases}
13+K,&\lambda^2\neq1,\\
14+K,&\lambda=-1,\\
14+H,&\lambda=1.
\end{cases}
$$
The Apéry representatives modulo $8$ are respectively
$$
(0,49,18,27,12,37,30,39),
$$
$$
(0,41,18,51,12,29,30,39),
$$
$$
(0,41,18,59,12,29,30,47).
$$
Since $t^8=x$, their conductors are the largest entries minus $7$, namely $42,44,52$. Thus the formulas above also hold for $n=2$.

Finally $\lambda^2=1$ has only $1$ and $-1$, and for every $n\geq2$,
$$
\kappa_{n,1}>\kappa_{n,-1}>\kappa_{n,\lambda}\qquad(\lambda^2\neq1).
$$
Therefore
$$
\beta_n=7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n,\qquad E_n=\{-1\}.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(7n^2+\left(7+(-1)^n\right)n,\{-1\}\right)}$

## Steps

Step 1: Determine the generic, singular, and vanishing parameter strata
Put
$$
a=14n+2,\qquad b=14n+5,\qquad x=t^7.
$$
Relative to $t^a,t^b$, the images of $Y,Z$ have coefficient matrix
$$
M(\lambda)
=
(\lambda-1)
\begin{pmatrix}
1&\lambda\\
1&\lambda^3-1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its determinant is
$$
\det M(\lambda)
=
(\lambda-1)^2(\lambda^3-\lambda-1).
$$

If
$$
\lambda\neq1,\qquad \lambda^3-\lambda-1\neq0,
$$
then $M(\lambda)$ is invertible, so $t^a,t^b\in A_{n,\lambda}$. Since $W=t^{2a}$ is redundant,
$$
A_{n,\lambda}
=
\mathbb C[[t^7,t^a,t^b]].
$$

Assume next that
$$
\lambda^3-\lambda-1=0.
$$
Then $\lambda\neq0,1$ and $\lambda^3-1=\lambda$. The two rows of $M(\lambda)$ coincide, so the $Y,Z$-span is generated by
$$
F=t^a+\lambda t^b.
$$
At this parameter
$$
A_{n,\lambda}
=
\mathbb C[[x,F,t^{2a}]].
$$
The equality
$$
a+b=28n+7=7(4n+1)
$$
gives
$$
F^2
=
t^{2a}+2\lambda x^{4n+1}+\lambda^2t^{2b}.
$$
Therefore
$$
G=t^{2b}
=
\lambda^{-2}
\left(
F^2-W-2\lambda X^{4n+1}
\right)
\in A_{n,\lambda}.
$$
Conversely,
$$
W
=
F^2-2\lambda X^{4n+1}-\lambda^2G,
$$
so
$$
A_{n,\lambda}
=
\mathbb C[[x,F,G]]
$$
throughout this singular stratum.

Finally, if $\lambda=1$, both $Y$ and $Z$ vanish, and
$$
A_{n,1}
=
\mathbb C[[t^7,t^{2a}]].
$$

Step 2: Compute the conductor on the generic stratum
Assume
$$
\lambda\neq1,\qquad \lambda^3-\lambda-1\neq0.
$$
By Step 1 the value semigroup is
$$
S_{\mathrm{gen}}
=
\langle7,a,b\rangle.
$$
Modulo $7$,
$$
a\equiv2,\qquad b\equiv-2,
$$
and
$$
a+b=7(4n+1).
$$
A least representative of any residue cannot contain both $a$ and $b$, since deleting one copy of each lowers the value by $a+b$ without changing the residue.

For $0\leq k\leq6$, the least representative of residue $2k$ is therefore
$$
\min\{ka,(7-k)b\}.
$$
For $k=1,2,3$, the first term is smaller; the last comparison is
$$
3a=42n+6<56n+20=4b.
$$
For $k=4,5,6$, the second term is smaller; the first comparison is
$$
3b=42n+15<56n+8=4a.
$$
Hence the largest least representative is $3b$. For a numerical semigroup containing $7$, the conductor equals the largest least residue representative minus $6$. Thus
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}
=
3b-6
=
42n+9.
$$

Step 3: Compute the singular conductor and exclude hidden cancellations
Assume
$$
\lambda^3-\lambda-1=0.
$$
Step 1 gives
$$
A_{n,\lambda}
=
\mathbb C[[x,F,G]],
$$
where
$$
v(x)=7,\qquad v(F)=a,\qquad v(G)=2b.
$$
Set
$$
S_*=\langle7,a,2b\rangle.
$$
Since
$$
a\equiv2,\qquad2b\equiv3\pmod7,
$$
the elements
$$
0,\ a,\ 2a,\ 3a,\ 4a,\ 2b,\ a+2b
$$
represent all seven residue classes modulo $7$. Moreover,
$$
2b=2a+6<3a,
$$
and
$$
a+2b=3a+6<4a,
$$
because $a>6$.

It remains to prove that the residue of $4a$, namely $1$, has no smaller representative in $S_*$. Suppose
$$
ia+j(2b)<4a
$$
with $i,j\geq0$ and
$$
2i+3j\equiv1\pmod7.
$$
Since $2b=2a+6$, put
$$
s=i+2j.
$$
Then
$$
ia+j(2b)=sa+6j<4a,
$$
so $s\leq3$. Because $i=s-2j$,
$$
2i+3j=2s-j.
$$
The bounds $0\leq j\leq s/2$ and $s\leq3$ imply
$$
0\leq2s-j\leq6.
$$
The congruence modulo $7$ therefore forces
$$
2s-j=1,
$$
so
$$
j=2s-1.
$$
For $s=0$ this is negative, while for $s=1,2,3$ it exceeds $s/2$. This is impossible. Hence $4a$ is the least element of residue $1$, and all other residue classes have representatives below it. The conductor of $S_*$ is
$$
4a-6
=
56n+2.
$$

The ring contains the monomial subring $\mathbb C[[t^7,t^a,t^{2b}]]$, so every $t^m$ with $m\geq56n+2$ already lies in $A_{n,\lambda}$. We must show that powers of the binomial $F$ do not fill a missing value below this bound.

Every monomial
$$
x^qF^jG^k
$$
has leading valuation
$$
7q+aj+2bk.
$$
If this valuation is below $56n+2=4a-6$, then $j\leq3$, $k\leq1$, and if $k=1$ then $j\leq1$. The possible pairs $(j,k)$ are
$$
(0,0),\ (1,0),\ (2,0),\ (3,0),\ (0,1),\ (1,1).
$$
Their leading residues modulo $7$ are respectively
$$
0,\ 2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 3,\ 5.
$$
They are distinct. For fixed $(j,k)$, changing $q$ changes the valuation by a nonzero multiple of $7$. Therefore every nonzero convergent combination whose valuation is below $56n+2$ has a unique monomial of least valuation, so its initial term cannot cancel. No extra valuation below the conductor of $S_*$ is created.

Thus
$$
\kappa_{n,\lambda}
=
56n+2
$$
whenever
$$
\lambda^3-\lambda-1=0.
$$

Step 4: Compute the vanishing stratum and identify the second-largest value
At $\lambda=1$, Step 1 gives
$$
A_{n,1}
=
\mathbb C[[t^7,t^{2a}]].
$$
Since
$$
2a\equiv4\pmod7,
$$
the seven elements
$$
0,\ 2a,\ 4a,\ 6a,\ 8a,\ 10a,\ 12a
$$
have distinct residues modulo $7$. Reducing the coefficient of $2a$ modulo $7$ shows that these are the least representatives. Their maximum is $12a$, so
$$
\kappa_{n,1}
=
12a-6
=
168n+18.
$$

For every $n\geq2$,
$$
168n+18>56n+2>42n+9,
$$
because
$$
56n+2-(42n+9)=14n-7>0.
$$
The largest conductor occurs only at $\lambda=1$. The second-largest distinct conductor occurs exactly at the roots of $\lambda^3-\lambda-1$.

Therefore
$$
\beta_n=56n+2,
$$
and
$$
E_n=
\{\lambda\in\mathbb C^{\times}:\lambda^3-\lambda-1=0\}.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(56n+2,\{\lambda\in\mathbb C^{\times}:\lambda^3-\lambda-1=0\}\right)}$

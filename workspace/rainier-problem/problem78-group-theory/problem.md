# Normalized Math Problem

## LaTeX (Normalized)

Fix an integer $r\geq21$ divisible by $21$. Let
$$
\widetilde S=\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_{27}),
\qquad
z=-I\in Z(\widetilde S),
\qquad
S=\widetilde S/\langle z\rangle=\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_{27}).
$$
Put
$$
h(x)=(x^2+x+1)(x^6+x^5+x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1)\in\mathbb F_2[x].
$$
Since $21\mid r$, the polynomial $h(x)$ divides $x^r-1$ in $\mathbb F_2[x]$.

Identify $Z(\widetilde S^r)$ with $\mathbb F_2[x]/(x^r-1)$ by
$$
(\varepsilon_0,\ldots,\varepsilon_{r-1})
\longleftrightarrow
(z^{\varepsilon_0},\ldots,z^{\varepsilon_{r-1}})
\longleftrightarrow
\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\varepsilon_i x^i.
$$
Let $D_r\leq Z(\widetilde S^r)$ correspond to the ideal generated by $h(x)$, and set
$$
N_r=\widetilde S^r/D_r.
$$
The cyclic shift of the $r$ factors preserves $D_r$; let $\tau$ denote the induced automorphism of $N_r$, and put
$$
G_r=N_r\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle,
\qquad |\tau|=r.
$$
Let
$$
\pi:G_r\to\langle\tau\rangle
$$
be the natural quotient map. For $0\leq i<r$, let
$$
\lambda_i:N_r\to S
$$
be the homomorphism induced by projection to the $i$-th factor of $\widetilde S^r$ followed by quotienting by $\langle z\rangle$.

For $H\leq G_r$, write
$$
\operatorname{core}_{G_r}(H)
=
\bigcap_{g\in G_r}gHg^{-1}.
$$
Call $H$ admissible if, with $K=H\cap N_r$, all three conditions hold:

1. $\pi(H)=\langle\tau\rangle$;
2. $\lambda_i(K)=S$ for every $0\leq i<r$;
3. $\operatorname{core}_{G_r}(H)=\{1\}$.

Let
$$
\mu_r
=
\min\{[G_r:H]:H\leq G_r\text{ is admissible}\},
$$
and let $c_r$ be the number of $G_r$-conjugacy classes of admissible subgroups $H$ satisfying
$$
[G_r:H]=\mu_r.
$$

Determine the ordered pair
$$
(\mu_r,c_r)
$$
for every positive multiple $r$ of $21$.

## Domain Explanation

The problem asks for extremal core-free subgroups inside a cyclic extension of a central quotient of a direct power of a quasisimple group. The central quotient changes which subdirect diagonal configurations can lift without acquiring a nontrivial normal central subgroup, while the cyclic action constrains both the block structure and the remaining twists. Thus the main work is finite group theory involving central extensions, subdirect products, subgroup cores, and automorphism-induced conjugacy.

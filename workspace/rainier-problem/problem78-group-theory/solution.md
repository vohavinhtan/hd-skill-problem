## Steps

Step 1: Determine the central quotient and the order of $N_r$
Let $R=\mathbb F_2[x]/(x^r-1)$. Since $x^r-1=h(x)q(x)$ with $\deg h=8$, multiplication by $h$ identifies $\mathbb F_2[x]/(q)$ with the ideal $(h)\subset R$: indeed $ha\equiv0\pmod{x^r-1}$ exactly when $q\mid a$. Hence
$$
|D_r|=2^{r-8}.
$$
The quotient of $N_r$ by the image of $Z(\widetilde S^r)$ is $S^r$, whose center is trivial, so
$$
Z(N_r)=Z(\widetilde S^r)/D_r\cong R/(h),
\qquad |Z(N_r)|=2^8.
$$
Also
$$
|S|=|\operatorname{PSL}_2(27)|
=\frac{27(27^2-1)}{2}
=9828,
$$
and $|\widetilde S|=2|S|$. Therefore
$$
|N_r|=\frac{(2|S|)^r}{2^{r-8}}=2^8\,9828^r.
$$
If $K=H\cap N_r$ and $\pi(H)=\langle\tau\rangle$, then $|H|=r|K|$, so
$$
[G_r:H]=[N_r:K].
$$

Step 2: Reduce admissible subgroups to cyclic block systems of diagonal copies of $S$
Choose $t\in H$ with $\pi(t)=\tau$. Then $tKt^{-1}=K$. Since the inner part of $t$ acts trivially on $Z(N_r)$, the subgroup $K\cap Z(N_r)$ is $\tau$-stable; it is central in $N_r$, hence normal in $G_r$. Core-freeness therefore forces
$$
K\cap Z(N_r)=1.
$$
Thus $K$ embeds in
$$
N_r/Z(N_r)\cong S^r.
$$
Write its image as $\overline K$. Condition $\lambda_i(K)=S$ says that $\overline K$ is subdirect.

For two coordinates, Goursat's lemma and simplicity of $S$ imply that the corresponding two-coordinate image is either $S\times S$ or the graph of an automorphism of $S$. Declare two coordinates equivalent in the second case. The equivalence classes are disjoint blocks, and on each block the coordinates are tied by automorphisms of one copy of $S$; different blocks are independent. Hence $\overline K$ is a product of twisted full diagonals, one per block.

The partition is preserved by the cyclic shift. For the block $B$ containing $0$, if $a\in B$, then $B$ and $a+B$ meet at $a$, so the block property gives $a+B=B$. Conversely $a+B=B$ implies $a\in B$. Thus $B$ is a subgroup $L\leq\mathbb Z/r$, and every block is a coset of $L$. If
$$
|L|=\ell,
\qquad b=\frac r\ell,
$$
then there are $b$ blocks and
$$
|\overline K|=|S|^b.
$$

Step 3: Find exactly which diagonal blocks lift through the central quotient
Consider one block $B$ and let $q_B(x)=\sum_{i\in B}x^i\in R$. Every automorphism of $S=\operatorname{PSL}_2(27)$ lifts to an automorphism of $\widetilde S=\operatorname{SL}_2(27)$ fixing $z=-I$: diagonal automorphisms lift by conjugation in $\operatorname{GL}_2(27)$, and field automorphisms lift entrywise. After choosing these lifts, the twisted diagonal gives a homomorphism
$$
\Phi_B:\widetilde S\longrightarrow N_r.
$$
The central element $z$ maps to the class of $q_B$. Therefore $\Phi_B$ factors through $S$ precisely when $q_B\in D_r$.

This condition is also necessary for a subgroup isomorphic to $S$ to lie over that diagonal. If $q_B\notin D_r$, the subgroup generated by the lifted diagonal is isomorphic to $\widetilde S$. The group $\widetilde S$ is perfect. Indeed its upper and lower elementary unipotents generate $\operatorname{SL}_2(27)$. If
$$
D=\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\0&t^{-1}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U(a)=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
$$
with $t^2\neq1$, then
$$
DU(a)D^{-1}U(a)^{-1}=U((t^2-1)a).
$$
As $a$ varies, the right side gives every upper unipotent; the same calculation gives every lower unipotent. Hence all generators lie in the derived subgroup. Therefore $\widetilde S$ has no subgroup of index $2$, since such a subgroup would be normal and give a nontrivial quotient $C_2$. Thus the surviving central element cannot be complemented. Consequently a block lifts if and only if
$$
q_B\in D_r.
$$

For $L=\langle b\rangle$ with $b=r/\ell$, a translate of $L$ only multiplies its indicator by a power of $x$, so it suffices to test
$$
q_L(x)=1+x^b+\cdots+x^{(\ell-1)b}.
$$
Because $D_r=(h)$, this is equivalent to $h\mid q_L$. Let $\alpha$ have order $d$, where $d=3$ or $7$. If $d\nmid b$, then $\alpha^b\neq1$ while $(\alpha^b)^\ell=\alpha^r=1$, so the geometric sum gives $q_L(\alpha)=0$. If $d\mid b$, then
$$
q_L(\alpha)=\ell\pmod2.
$$
The two factors of $h$ are squarefree and have exactly the primitive third and seventh roots as roots. Therefore
$$
h\mid q_L
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\ell\text{ is even, or both }3\nmid\frac r\ell\text{ and }7\nmid\frac r\ell.
$$

Step 4: Optimize the block size and prove that the bound is attained
If $r$ is even, $\ell=1$ fails because $q_L=1$, while $\ell=2$ satisfies the criterion in Step 3. Hence the smallest possible block size is $2$.

If $r$ is odd, every divisor $\ell$ is odd, so Step 3 requires $\ell$ to contain the full $3$-primary and $7$-primary parts of $r$. Thus the smallest possible block size is
$$
3^{v_3(r)}7^{v_7(r)}=\gcd(r,21^r).
$$
Indeed $21^r$ contains at least as many factors of $3$ and $7$ as $r$ and contains no other primes.

For either optimal block size $\ell_0$, every block indicator lies in $D_r$, so the lifted diagonal factors give
$$
K\cong S^{r/\ell_0},
\qquad |K|=9828^{r/\ell_0}.
$$
Taking the untwisted diagonals makes $K$ invariant under $\tau$, so $H=\langle K,\tau\rangle$ exists. Its core is trivial. To see this, let $C=\operatorname{core}_{G_r}(H)$. The image of $C\cap N_r$ in $S^r$ is a shift-invariant normal subgroup. Normal subgroups of $S^r$ are products of coordinate factors, and transitivity of the shift leaves only $1$ and $S^r$. The second is impossible because the diagonal block product is proper, so $C\cap N_r\subseteq Z(N_r)$. Since $K\cap Z(N_r)=1$, one gets $C\cap N_r=1$. Then $[C,N_r]=1$. An element involving a nonzero power of $\tau$ permutes the simple factors of $S^r$, so it cannot centralize $N_r$; hence $C_{G_r}(N_r)=Z(N_r)\subseteq N_r$, and therefore $C=1$.

Thus
$$
\mu_r=2^8\,9828^{\,r-r/\ell_0}.
$$

Step 5: Count the conjugacy classes attaining the minimum
The cyclic group $\mathbb Z/r$ has a unique subgroup of order $\ell_0$, so the optimal block system is unique. Put $b=r/\ell_0$. A lift $t$ of $\tau$ cyclically permutes the $b$ diagonal factors, and $t^b$ stabilizes each one. On a fixed diagonal factor it induces an automorphism $\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)$. Coordinate conjugations remove all inner transition twists, so the remaining invariant is the class
$$
\overline\theta\in\operatorname{Out}(S).
$$
Since $(t^b)^{\ell_0}=t^r\in K$, its action on a diagonal factor is inner, giving
$$
\overline\theta^{\,\ell_0}=1.
$$
Conversely, if an outer class has order dividing $\ell_0$, choose a representative $\theta$ of that order and write the coordinates inside each block as
$$
s,\theta(s),\ldots,\theta^{\ell_0-1}(s).
$$
Then the cyclic shift normalizes the resulting twisted diagonal product, so every such outer class occurs.

For $q=27>3$, the automorphisms of $\operatorname{PSL}_2(q)$ are generated by $\operatorname{PGL}_2(q)$ and field automorphisms. Here
$$
\operatorname{PGL}_2(27)/\operatorname{PSL}_2(27)\cong C_2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_{27}/\mathbb F_3)\cong C_3.
$$
The field automorphism acts trivially on the quotient of order $2$, so
$$
\operatorname{Out}(S)\cong C_2\times C_3\cong C_6.
$$
Hence the number of possible outer classes is $\gcd(\ell_0,6)$: it is $2$ when $r$ is even, while for odd $r$ the number $\ell_0$ is divisible by $3$ and odd, giving $3$.

There are no extra classes from the choice of lifts. The lift of each diagonal product is unique because two splittings would differ by a homomorphism from the perfect group $S^{r/\ell_0}$ to the abelian group $Z(N_r)$, hence by the zero map. Moreover each twisted full diagonal is self-normalizing inside its product of centerless $S$-factors, so
$$
N_{N_r}(K)=KZ(N_r).
$$
Thus two elements over $\tau$ that normalize the same $K$ differ by an element of $KZ(N_r)$; the $K$-part does not change the generated subgroup.

It remains to remove the central part. We have
$$
Z(N_r)\cong\mathbb F_2[x]/(h),
$$
with $\tau$ acting as multiplication by $x$. Since $h(1)=1$, multiplication by $x-1$ is invertible modulo $h$. Thus every central modification $c\tau$ is conjugate to $\tau$: write $c=(1-x)y$ and conjugate by $y\in Z(N_r)$. Therefore the outer class found above is the complete conjugacy invariant.

Step 6: Write the result without a piecewise final expression and attack the boundary cases
Set only for this calculation
$$
d=\gcd(2,r),
\qquad
g=\gcd(r,21^r).
$$
The optimal block size from Step 4 is
$$
\ell_0=d+(2-d)(g-1),
$$
because this equals $2$ for even $r$ and $g$ for odd $r$. Step 5 gives $c_r=4-d$. Substitution yields a final expression using only the parameter $r$ from the problem.

As independent checks, for $r=21$ the formula gives block size $21$, exponent $20$, and $c_r=3$; for $r=42$ it gives block size $2$, exponent $21$, and $c_r=2$; for $r=63$ it gives block size $63$, exponent $62$, and $c_r=3$. For the necessity attack, any smaller odd block omits part of the $3$- or $7$-primary component, making the corresponding primitive-root geometric sum equal to $1$, while for even $r$ the only smaller candidate than $2$ is $\ell=1$, whose indicator is not divisible by $h$. Thus no smaller index is possible.

Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(256\cdot9828^{r-\frac{r}{\gcd(2,r)+(2-\gcd(2,r))(\gcd(r,21^r)-1)}},4-\gcd(2,r)\right)}$

## Steps

Step 1: Identify the saturated quotient by explicit divisor-class coordinates
Let $S=\mathbb F_p[x_v:v\in V(G_n)]$, let $J$ be the ideal generated by the vertex binomials, and let $L\subset\mathbb Z^{V(G_n)}$ be the subgroup generated by the Laplacian rows. In the Laurent ring $S[M_0^{-1}]$, every variable is invertible and each generator of $J$ is a monomial unit times $x^\ell-1$ for a Laplacian row $\ell$. Moreover
$$
J:M_0^\infty=J S[M_0^{-1}]\cap S,
$$
because membership in the contraction is equivalent to $M_0^r g\in J$ for some $r\geq0$. Hence two monomials have the same image in
$$
R_n:=S/(J:M_0^\infty)
$$
exactly when their exponent difference lies in $L$.

We now compute $\mathbb Z^{V(G_n)}/L$ directly. Put $a_0=a_n=b_0=b_n=c_0=c_n=o$. On the $a$-cycle the Laplacian relation at $a_i$ gives
$$
2[a_i]-[a_{i-1}]-[a_{i+1}]=0.
$$
Thus the successive differences are equal, so
$$
[a_i-o]=i[a_1-o],\qquad n[a_1-o]=0.
$$
The same argument applies to the $b$- and $c$-cycles. Therefore the quotient is generated by the degree class $[o]$ and three elements of orders dividing $n$.

Define
$$
\pi(o)=(1,0,0,0),\quad
\pi(a_i)=(1,i,0,0),\quad
\pi(b_i)=(1,0,i,0),\quad
\pi(c_i)=(1,0,0,i)
$$
in $\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^3$. Every Laplacian row maps to $0$, so $\pi$ factors through $\mathbb Z^{V(G_n)}/L$. The generators just found map to the four standard generators, while the preceding relations show that no other generators are needed. The induced maps in the two directions are inverse on these generators. Hence
$$
\mathbb Z^{V(G_n)}/L\cong \mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^3.
$$
Consequently the degree-$d$ part of $R_n$ has one basis vector for each degree-$d$ class that has an effective representative.

Step 2: Convert the target graded piece to a finite group-algebra quotient
Let
$$
B=\mathbb F_p[(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^3]
$$
with basis $e_{x,y,z}$, where all coordinates are read modulo $n$. Since $p$ is odd, $n$ is odd, and
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}i=\frac{n(n-1)}2\equiv0\pmod n.
$$
Thus $M_0$ has class $(0,0,0)$ in the degree-zero factor. Also
$$
M_{a,j}=M_0\frac{x_{a_j}}{x_o},\qquad
M_{b,j}=M_0\frac{x_{b_j}}{x_o},\qquad
M_{c,j}=M_0\frac{x_{c_j}}{x_o}
$$
in the Laurent quotient, so these three monomials have classes $(j,0,0)$, $(0,j,0)$, and $(0,0,j)$ relative to $M_0$.

The degree of each added relation is
$$
D=|V(G_n)|=3n-2.
$$
At degree $3n=D+2$, every class of $(\mathbb Z/n)^3$ is effective: $(x,y,z)$ is represented in degree $3$ by one vertex from each cycle, using $o$ when a coordinate is $0$, and then padded with copies of $o$. Hence $(R_n)_{3n}\cong B$.

In degree $2$, the effective classes are exactly
$$
T=\{(x,y,z):xyz=0\text{ in the sense that at least one coordinate is }0\}.
$$
Indeed a sum of two vertices uses at most two of the three cycles, and conversely any class with one zero coordinate is represented by one vertex on each of the other two cycles.

Put
$$
j=\frac{(p-1)n}{p}.
$$
Therefore $(A_n)_{3n}\cong B/W$, where $W$ is spanned, for $t=(x,y,z)\in T$, by
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_1(t)&=e_{x+1,y,z}+e_{x,y+1,z}-2e_{x,y,z},\\
r_2(t)&=e_{x,y+1,z}+e_{x,y,z+1}-2e_{x,y,z},\\
r_3(t)&=e_{x+j,y,z}+e_{x,y+j,z}-2e_{x,y,z}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Step 3: Pass to the dual system and isolate the completely free coordinates
Use the pairing for which the basis $e_{x,y,z}$ is orthonormal. Then
$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(B/W)=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}W^\perp.
$$
A function $f:(\mathbb Z/n)^3\to\mathbb F_p$ belongs to $W^\perp$ exactly when, for every $(x,y,z)\in T$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x+1,y,z)+f(x,y+1,z)&=2f(x,y,z),\\
f(x,y+1,z)+f(x,y,z+1)&=2f(x,y,z),\\
f(x+j,y,z)+f(x,y+j,z)&=2f(x,y,z).
\end{aligned}
$$

A coordinate can occur in one of these equations only if
$$
x\in\{0,1,j\},\qquad\text{or}\qquad
y\in\{0,1,j\},\qquad\text{or}\qquad
z\in\{0,1\}.
$$
Conversely each coordinate satisfying one of these conditions does occur. Since $n\geq9$ and $p$ is odd, $0,1,j$ are distinct. Thus the completely untouched coordinates are exactly
$$
x\notin\{0,1,j\},\qquad
y\notin\{0,1,j\},\qquad
z\notin\{0,1\},
$$
and they contribute
$$
(n-3)^2(n-2)
$$
free parameters to $W^\perp$.

Step 4: Reduce all remaining equations to a two-dimensional translation system
For $f\in W^\perp$, define
$$
g(x,y)=f(x,y,0).
$$
Because $z=0$ lies in $T$, the first and third equations give
$$
g(x+1,y)+g(x,y+1)=2g(x,y),
$$
$$
g(x+j,y)+g(x,y+j)=2g(x,y).
$$
We claim that every touched value of $f$ is forced by
$$
f(x,y,z)=g(x+z,y).
$$

First set $h(y,z)=f(0,y,z)$. Subtracting the second dual equation from the first at $(0,y,z)$ gives
$$
f(1,y,z)=h(y,z+1),
$$
and the first equation then becomes
$$
h(y,z+1)+h(y+1,z)=2h(y,z).
$$
Since $h(y,0)=g(0,y)$, recursion in $z$ and the first equation for $g$ give
$$
h(y,z)=g(z,y).
$$
Hence the formula holds on the planes $x=0$ and $x=1$. The third dual equation at $(0,y,z)$ then gives
$$
f(j,y,z)=2g(z,y)-g(z,y+j)=g(z+j,y),
$$
using the second equation for $g$.

Next, on the plane $y=0$, subtraction of the first two dual equations gives
$$
f(x,0,z+1)=f(x+1,0,z),
$$
so $f(x,0,z)=g(x+z,0)$. The first and third equations at $(x,0,z)$ then give the same formula on $y=1$ and $y=j$. The cases $z=0$ and $z=1$ follow directly from the definition of $g$ and the subtraction identity at $z=0$. These planes are exactly the touched set found in Step 3.

Conversely, if $g$ satisfies the two displayed translation equations, defining $f(x,y,z)=g(x+z,y)$ on the touched set makes all three dual equations hold, while the untouched coordinates remain arbitrary. Therefore
$$
\dim W^\perp=(n-3)^2(n-2)+\dim\mathcal G,
$$
where $\mathcal G$ is the space of functions $g:(\mathbb Z/n)^2\to\mathbb F_p$ satisfying the two translation equations.

Step 5: Compute the residual dimension using the positive-characteristic nilpotent block
Let
$$
C=\mathbb F_p[u,v]/(u^n-1,v^n-1).
$$
Under the coefficient pairing on the basis $u^xv^y$, the orthogonal complement of the span of all translates of
$$
u+v-2,\qquad u^j+v^j-2
$$
is exactly $\mathcal G$. Hence
$$
\dim\mathcal G
=
\dim_{\mathbb F_p}
\frac{C}{(u+v-2,\ u^j+v^j-2)}.
$$

Write $q=n/p$, so $j=(p-1)q$, and set $X=u-1$. The first relation gives $v=1-X$. Since $n$ is a power of $p$,
$$
u^n-1=(u-1)^n=X^n,
$$
and the relation $v^n-1$ gives the same nilpotence condition. Thus the residual quotient is
$$
\frac{\mathbb F_p[X]}
{\left(X^n,\ (1+X)^j+(1-X)^j-2\right)}.
$$
Because $q$ is also a power of $p$,
$$
(1+X)^q=1+X^q,\qquad
(1-X)^q=1-X^q.
$$
With $Y=X^q$,
$$
(1+X)^j+(1-X)^j-2
=
(1+Y)^{p-1}+(1-Y)^{p-1}-2.
$$
The constant term is $0$, all odd powers cancel, and the coefficient of $Y^2$ is
$$
2\binom{p-1}{2}\equiv2\pmod p,
$$
which is nonzero because $p$ is odd. Therefore
$$
(1+Y)^{p-1}+(1-Y)^{p-1}-2
=
Y^2U(Y)
$$
with $U(0)=2$, so $U(Y)$ is a unit in the truncated ring. Since $2q<n$, the residual quotient is isomorphic as a vector space to
$$
\mathbb F_p[X]/(X^{2q}),
$$
and consequently
$$
\dim\mathcal G=2q=\frac{2n}{p}.
$$
Combining this with Step 4 yields
$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(A_n)_{3n}
=
(n-3)^2(n-2)+\frac{2n}{p}.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{(n-3)^2(n-2)+\frac{2n}{p}}$

## Steps

Step 1: Reduce the displayed congruence to one cyclic quotient
Put $m=U_{2n+1}$ and $q=U_{2n}$. In the additive group $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$, the set of residues $aU_{\ell}$ is the subgroup generated by $U_{\ell}$, hence it is exactly the set of multiples of $d=\gcd(U_{\ell},m)$. Thus
$$
C_{c,n,\ell}=k[s^it^j:i,j\geq0\text{ and }i+qj\equiv0\pmod d].
$$
The sequence is a strong divisibility sequence. Indeed the identity
$$
U_{a+b}=U_aU_{b+1}+U_{a-1}U_b
$$
follows from the same recurrence in $b$ and the two initial values $b=0,1$; applying the Euclidean algorithm to this identity gives $\gcd(U_a,U_b)=U_{\gcd(a,b)}$. Therefore, with
$$
g=\gcd(2n+1,\ell),
$$
we have $d=U_g$. Since $g$ divides the odd integer $2n+1$, write $g=2h+1$ with $h\geq0$.

Step 2: Identify the effective weight modulo $U_g$
The recurrence matrix $A=\begin{pmatrix}c&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ satisfies
$$
A^r=\begin{pmatrix}U_{r+1}&U_r\\U_r&U_{r-1}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Modulo $U_g$, this gives
$$
A^g\equiv\begin{pmatrix}U_{g-1}&0\\0&U_{g-1}\end{pmatrix}\pmod {U_g},
$$
because $U_{g+1}=cU_g+U_{g-1}$. If $2n+1=eg$, then $e$ is odd, and the lower-right entry of $A^{eg}$ gives
$$
q=U_{2n}\equiv U_{g-1}^{e}\pmod {U_g}.
$$
Cassini's identity, obtained by taking determinants in the displayed formula for $A^r$, is
$$
U_{r+1}U_{r-1}-U_r^2=(-1)^r.
$$
For odd $g$, it gives $U_{g-1}^2\equiv-1\pmod {U_g}$, so $q\equiv\pm U_{g-1}\pmod {U_g}$. Replacing $q$ by $-q$ does not change the type: interchanging $s$ and $t$ sends the congruence $i+U_{g-1}j\equiv0\pmod {U_g}$ to $j+U_{g-1}i\equiv0\pmod {U_g}$, and multiplying this congruence by $U_{g-1}^{-1}\equiv-U_{g-1}$ gives $i-U_{g-1}j\equiv0\pmod {U_g}$. Hence it remains to compute the type for
$$
S_h=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{2}:i+U_{2h}j\equiv0\pmod {U_{2h+1}}\}.
$$

Step 3: Convert the type into a count of record-low interior lattice points
If $h=0$, then $U_g=U_1=1$, the congruence is void, and the localization is the regular local ring $k[s,t]_{(s,t)}$, whose Cohen-Macaulay type is $1$. Assume now that $h\geq1$. Let $L$ be the lattice of integer pairs satisfying the congruence defining $S_h$; its index in $\mathbb Z^2$ is $U_{2h+1}$. The semigroup ring is normal because it is $L\cap\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{2}$. Its canonical module is generated by monomials whose exponent vectors lie in the relative interior, namely by $L\cap\mathbb Z_{>0}^{2}$; localizing at the monomial maximal ideal preserves the number of minimal monomial generators of this canonical module. A vector $(x,y)$ in this interior set is a minimal canonical generator exactly when no other interior lattice vector has both coordinates at most $x,y$ and one coordinate smaller.

The following elementary lattice-chain criterion will be used. Suppose
$$
P_{-1}=(U_{2h+1},0),\quad P_N=(0,U_{2h+1}),
$$
and $P_0,\ldots,P_{N-1}$ are interior lattice points with strictly decreasing first coordinates and strictly increasing second coordinates. If every adjacent determinant is $U_{2h+1}$, then each adjacent pair is a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $L$. The parallelograms between successive vectors tile the quadrant below the polygonal chain, so no lattice point lies strictly below the chain; consequently $P_0,\ldots,P_{N-1}$ are exactly the minimal interior lattice points. This is the usual two-dimensional Hilbert-basis argument, with the determinant equal to the lattice index providing the no-missing-point certificate.

Step 4: Build the complete interior chain
For $0\leq b\leq h-1$ and $0\leq r\leq c-1$, define
$$
P_{b,r}=\bigl((c-1-r)U_{2h-2b}+U_{2h-2b-1},\ U_{2b}+(r+1)U_{2b+1}\bigr).
$$
List these points in increasing lexicographic order of $(b,r)$. Their second coordinates increase and their first coordinates decrease, because $U_{a+1}=cU_a+U_{a-1}$ with $c\geq2$ gives positive gaps at each within-block and block-boundary move. They lie in $L$: using
$$
U_{2h}U_{2b+1}-U_{2h+1}U_{2b}=U_{2h-2b}
$$
and
$$
U_{2h}U_{2b}-U_{2h+1}U_{2b-1}=-U_{2h-2b+1},
$$
their congruence residue is
$$
(c-1-r)U_{2h-2b}+U_{2h-2b-1}-U_{2h-2b+1}+(r+1)U_{2h-2b}=0,
$$
since $U_{2h-2b+1}=cU_{2h-2b}+U_{2h-2b-1}$.

It remains only to certify that no point is missing between consecutive entries. Within a fixed $b$, the determinant of $P_{b,r}$ and $P_{b,r+1}$ is
$$
\bigl((c-1-r)U_{2h-2b}+U_{2h-2b-1}\bigr)U_{2b+1}+U_{2h-2b}\bigl(U_{2b}+(r+1)U_{2b+1}\bigr)
$$
$$
=U_{2h-2b+1}U_{2b+1}+U_{2h-2b}U_{2b}=U_{2h+1}.
$$
At a block boundary,
$$
P_{b,c-1}=(U_{2h-2b-1},U_{2b+2})
$$
and
$$
P_{b+1,0}=(U_{2h-2b-1}-U_{2h-2b-2},U_{2b+2}+U_{2b+3}).
$$
Their determinant is
$$
U_{2h-2b-1}U_{2b+3}+U_{2h-2b-2}U_{2b+2}=U_{2h+1},
$$
again by $U_aU_b+U_{a-1}U_{b-1}=U_{a+b-1}$. The endpoint determinants with $(U_{2h+1},0)$ and $(0,U_{2h+1})$ are also $U_{2h+1}$ because the first listed point has second coordinate $1$ and the last listed point has first coordinate $1$. Step 3 therefore applies, so the canonical module has exactly $ch$ minimal generators.

Step 5: Assemble the formula and check the regular boundary case
For $h\geq1$, Step 4 gives type $ch=c(g-1)/2$. For $h=0$, Step 3 gives type $1$. The expression
$$
\frac{c(g-1)}{2}+\left\lfloor\frac{1}{g}\right\rfloor
$$
has these two values because $g$ is a positive odd integer: the floor term is $1$ when $g=1$ and $0$ otherwise. Replacing $g$ by its definition gives the required closed form. As a counterexample attack against the common missing-boundary answer, take $\ell=1$; then $g=1$, the ring is $k[s,t]$, and the formula gives type $1$, while the expression without the floor term would give $0$. For a nonregular check, $c=2,n=3,\ell=7$ gives $g=7$ and type $6$, matching the six points $P_{b,r}$ with $0\leq b\leq2$ and $0\leq r\leq1$.
Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{c\left(\gcd(2n+1,\ell)-1\right)}{2}+\left\lfloor\frac{1}{\gcd(2n+1,\ell)}\right\rfloor}$

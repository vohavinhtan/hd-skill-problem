## Steps

Step 1: Encode the substitutions in a truncated skew-polynomial ring

Put
$$
m=\left\lfloor\log_2(n-1)\right\rfloor.
$$
The monomial $x^{2^i}$ is nonzero in $A_n$ exactly when $0\leq i\leq m$. Since $x\mapsto x^2$ is additive in characteristic $2$, every positive composition of the two given substitutions has the form
$$
x\longmapsto \sum_{i=0}^{m}c_i x^{2^i}.
$$
Let
$$
R_m=\mathbb F_4\{F\}/(F^{m+1}),\qquad F\lambda=\lambda^2F
$$
for $\lambda\in\mathbb F_4$. If
$$
L(x)=\sum_i c_i x^{2^i},\qquad M(x)=\sum_j d_j x^{2^j},
$$
then
$$
L(M(x))=\sum_{i,j}c_i d_j^{2^i}x^{2^{i+j}},
$$
while
$$
(c_iF^i)(d_jF^j)=c_i d_j^{2^i}F^{i+j}.
$$
Thus the Ore product of the elements encoding $L$ and $M$ encodes $L(M(x))$. For the algebra automorphism
$$
\phi_L(p(x))=p(L(x)),
$$
however,
$$
(\phi_L\circ\phi_M)(x)=\phi_L(M(x))=M(L(x)).
$$
Consequently the image-of-$x$ correspondence
$$
\Theta(\phi_L)=\sum_i c_iF^i
$$
reverses multiplication:
$$
\Theta(\phi_L\circ\phi_M)=\Theta(\phi_M)\Theta(\phi_L).
$$
The distinct surviving monomials $x^{2^i}$ are linearly independent, so this correspondence is injective wherever it is defined.

The ideal $FR_m$ is nilpotent, since $(FR_m)^{m+1}=0$. Hence every $1+v$ with $v\in FR_m$ is a unit: if $v^{N+1}=0$, then in characteristic $2$
$$
(1+v)(1+v+\cdots+v^N)=1+v^{N+1}=1.
$$
In particular
$$
a=1+F,\qquad b=1+\omega F
$$
are units, and $a^{-1},b^{-1}$ are again finite sums of powers of $F$. The corresponding linearized substitutions compose with $x+x^2$ and $x+\omega x^2$ to give $x$, so they are precisely the inverse automorphisms $\tau^{-1}$ and $\sigma^{-1}$. Therefore every word in $\sigma^{\pm1},\tau^{\pm1}$ is represented in $R_m^\times$, and $\Theta$ is an anti-isomorphism from $H_n$ onto $\langle a,b\rangle$.

Under $\Theta$, the generators $\tau$ and $\sigma$ map to $a$ and $b$. Composing $\Theta$ with inversion gives an ordinary isomorphism, because both maps reverse multiplication. Hence
$$
H_n\cong\langle a^{-1},b^{-1}\rangle
=\langle a,b\rangle\leq R_m^\times.
$$

Step 2: Find the cyclic subgroup controlling all commutators

Put $z=F^2$. For every $\lambda\in\mathbb F_4$,
$$
z\lambda=F(\lambda^2F)=\lambda^4F^2=\lambda z,
$$
so $z$ is central. Since the characteristic is $2$ and $\omega^3=1$,
$$
a^2=(1+F)^2=1+z
$$
and
$$
b^2=(1+\omega F)^2
=1+\omega F\omega F
=1+\omega\omega^2F^2
=1+z.
$$
Write
$$
q=1+z,\qquad r=a^{-1}b.
$$
The element $q$ is central and $a^2=b^2=q$. Therefore
$$
a^{-1}=q^{-1}a,\qquad b^{-1}=q^{-1}b.
$$
Moreover,
$$
ara^{-1}=ba^{-1}=bq^{-1}a=q^{-1}ba=b^{-1}a=r^{-1}.
$$
Thus $\langle r\rangle$ is normal in $H_n$, conjugation by $a$ inverts $r$, and $H_n=\langle a,r\rangle$ because $b=ar$.

Conjugation by $r$ fixes every power of $r$, while conjugation by $a$ sends $r^k$ to $r^{-k}$. Since $H_n=\langle a,r\rangle$, conjugation by any element of $H_n$ sends $r^k$ either to $r^k$ or to $r^{-k}$. In particular $\langle r^2\rangle$ is normal in $H_n$, so the quotient by $\langle r^2\rangle$ is defined. In that quotient, the image of $r$ has order at most $2$, hence inversion fixes it; since the quotient is generated by the images of $a$ and $r$, it is abelian. Therefore
$$
[H_n,H_n]\subseteq\langle r^2\rangle.
$$
Conversely, conjugation by $a^{-1}$ also inverts $r$, because conjugation by $a$ has square equal to conjugation by the central element $a^2=q$. With the commutator convention in the problem,
$$
[r,a]=r^{-1}a^{-1}ra=r^{-2}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\gamma_2(H_n)=\langle r^2\rangle.
$$

Now suppose $j\geq2$ and
$$
\gamma_j(H_n)=\left\langle r^{2^{j-1}}\right\rangle.
$$
Write $k=2^{j-1}$. For arbitrary $u=r^{kt}\in\gamma_j(H_n)$ and $h\in H_n$, the preceding conjugation description gives
$$
h^{-1}uh=u\quad\text{or}\quad h^{-1}uh=u^{-1}.
$$
Hence
$$
[u,h]=u^{-1}h^{-1}uh
$$
is either $1$ or $u^{-2}=r^{-2kt}$, so every such commutator lies in $\langle r^{2k}\rangle=\langle r^{2^j}\rangle$. Thus
$$
\gamma_{j+1}(H_n)\subseteq\left\langle r^{2^j}\right\rangle.
$$
Taking $u=r^k$ and $h=a$ gives
$$
[r^k,a]=r^{-2k}=r^{-2^j},
$$
which generates $\langle r^{2^j}\rangle$. Therefore
$$
\gamma_{j+1}(H_n)=\left\langle r^{2^j}\right\rangle,
$$
and induction yields
$$
\gamma_j(H_n)=\left\langle r^{2^{j-1}}\right\rangle\quad(j\geq2).
$$

Step 3: Determine the exact order of $r$

Using $a^{-1}=q^{-1}a$ and $b^{-1}=q^{-1}b$,
$$
r+r^{-1}=q^{-1}(ab+ba).
$$
The two products are
$$
ab=(1+F)(1+\omega F)
=1+(1+\omega)F+\omega^2z
=1+\omega^2F+\omega^2z
$$
and
$$
ba=(1+\omega F)(1+F)
=1+(1+\omega)F+\omega z
=1+\omega^2F+\omega z.
$$
Since $\omega+\omega^2=1$, their sum is $z$. Consequently
$$
r+r^{-1}=\frac{z}{1+z}=:u.
$$
The element $u$ is central.

For every integer $s\geq1$, the commuting elements $r$ and $r^{-1}$ satisfy
$$
r^{2^{s-1}}+r^{-2^{s-1}}
=(r+r^{-1})^{2^{s-1}}
=u^{2^{s-1}}.
$$
Let $y=r^{2^{s-1}}$. Because the characteristic is $2$,
$$
r^{2^s}=1
\iff y^2=1
\iff y=y^{-1}
\iff y+y^{-1}=0.
$$
Hence
$$
r^{2^s}=1
\iff u^{2^{s-1}}=0.
$$
Since $1+z$ is a unit,
$$
u^{2^{s-1}}=0
\iff z^{2^{s-1}}=0
\iff F^{2^s}=0.
$$
In $R_m$, the last condition holds exactly when
$$
2^s\geq m+1,
$$
or equivalently $2^s>m$.

The group $1+FR_m$ has $4^m=2^{2m}$ elements, so every element of it has power-of-two order. Therefore the order of $r$ is exactly $2^s$, where $s$ is the least integer with $2^s>m$. Thus
$$
\operatorname{ord}(r)=2^{\,1+\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor}.
$$

Step 4: Read off the nilpotency class

Let
$$
c=1+\left\lfloor\log_2m\right\rfloor.
$$
By Step 3, $r$ has order $2^c$. Step 2 then gives
$$
\gamma_c(H_n)=\left\langle r^{2^{c-1}}\right\rangle\neq\{1\},
$$
whereas
$$
\gamma_{c+1}(H_n)=\left\langle r^{2^c}\right\rangle=\{1\}.
$$
Therefore the nilpotency class is $c$. Substituting
$$
m=\left\lfloor\log_2(n-1)\right\rfloor
$$
gives the required expression.

Final Answer: $\boxed{1+\left\lfloor\log_2\!\left(\left\lfloor\log_2(n-1)\right\rfloor\right)\right\rfloor}$

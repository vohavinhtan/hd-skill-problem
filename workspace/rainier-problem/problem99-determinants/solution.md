## Steps

Step 1: Derive the algebra generated by the three structured operators

The Walsh matrix satisfies
$$
H^2=NI,
$$
because
$$
(H^2)_{x,z}
=
\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_2^n}(-1)^{(x+z)\cdot y}
$$
equals $N$ for $x=z$ and $0$ otherwise.

Also,
$$
D^2=X^2=I.
$$
Since $n$ is odd,
$$
\omega\cdot\omega=1
$$
in $\mathbb{F}_2$. Therefore
$$
(XDf)(x)
=
(-1)^{\omega\cdot(x+\omega)}f(x+\omega)
=
-(-1)^{\omega\cdot x}f(x+\omega),
$$
so
$$
XD=-DX.
$$

For the interaction with $H$,
$$
(HDf)(x)
=
\sum_y(-1)^{x\cdot y+\omega\cdot y}f(y)
=
(Hf)(x+\omega)
=
(XHf)(x),
$$
and
$$
(HXf)(x)
=
\sum_y(-1)^{x\cdot y}f(y+\omega)
=
(-1)^{\omega\cdot x}(Hf)(x)
=
(DHf)(x).
$$
Hence
$$
HD=XH,\qquad HX=DH.
$$

Put
$$
A=D+X,\qquad K=HA.
$$
The two displayed relations give $HA=AH$, while $XD=-DX$ gives
$$
A^2=D^2+DX+XD+X^2=2I.
$$
Consequently,
$$
K^2=H^2A^2=2NI=q^2I.
$$

Step 2: Determine the multiplicities of the two eigenvalues of $K$

Since $K^2=q^2I$ and $q\neq0$, the only eigenvalues of $K$ are $q$ and $-q$, and $K$ is diagonalizable.

Let their multiplicities be $m_+$ and $m_-$. Then
$$
m_++m_-=N.
$$
We compute the trace of $K$:
$$
\operatorname{tr}(K)=\operatorname{tr}(HD)+\operatorname{tr}(HX).
$$

For $HD$, the diagonal entry indexed by $x$ is
$$
(-1)^{x\cdot x+\omega\cdot x}.
$$
Over $\mathbb{F}_2$,
$$
x\cdot x=\omega\cdot x,
$$
so every diagonal entry equals $1$. Thus
$$
\operatorname{tr}(HD)=N.
$$

The diagonal entry of $HX$ indexed by $x$ is
$$
(-1)^{x\cdot(x+\omega)}
=
(-1)^{x\cdot x+x\cdot\omega}
=
1,
$$
so
$$
\operatorname{tr}(HX)=N.
$$
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{tr}(K)=2N=q^2.
$$
Since
$$
q(m_+-m_-)=\operatorname{tr}(K),
$$
we obtain
$$
m_+-m_-=q.
$$
Together with $m_++m_-=N$,
$$
m_+=\frac{N+q}{2},\qquad
m_-=\frac{N-q}{2}.
$$

Step 3: Compute the determinant before the all-ones correction

Set
$$
M_0=3I+K.
$$
On the $q$-eigenspace of $K$, the operator $M_0$ has eigenvalue $3+q$, while on the $-q$-eigenspace it has eigenvalue $3-q$. Hence
$$
\det M_0
=
(3+q)^{(N+q)/2}(3-q)^{(N-q)/2}.
$$

Because $n\geq3$, we have $q\geq4$, so neither $3+q$ nor $3-q$ vanishes. Thus $M_0$ is invertible. From $K^2=q^2I=2NI$,
$$
M_0^{-1}
=
\frac{3I-K}{9-2N}.
$$

Step 4: Evaluate the rank-one correction produced by $J$

Let $\mathbf{1}$ be the all-ones column vector. Then
$$
J=\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{T}.
$$
Let $e_a$ denote the coordinate vector supported at $a\in\mathbb{F}_2^n$. Since $\mathbf{1}$ is constant,
$$
X\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}.
$$
Also $D\mathbf{1}$ is the character
$$
x\longmapsto(-1)^{\omega\cdot x}.
$$
The definitions of $H$ give
$$
H\mathbf{1}=Ne_0
$$
and
$$
H(D\mathbf{1})=Ne_\omega.
$$
Therefore
$$
K\mathbf{1}
=
H(D+X)\mathbf{1}
=
N(e_0+e_\omega).
$$
Taking the scalar product with $\mathbf{1}$ gives
$$
\mathbf{1}^{T}K\mathbf{1}=2N,
$$
while
$$
\mathbf{1}^{T}\mathbf{1}=N.
$$
Hence
$$
\mathbf{1}^{T}M_0^{-1}\mathbf{1}
=
\frac{3N-2N}{9-2N}
=
\frac{N}{9-2N}.
$$

Now
$$
M_0+J
=
M_0\left(I+M_0^{-1}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{T}\right).
$$
For column vectors $a,b$,
$$
\det(I+ab^{T})=1+b^{T}a,
$$
because $ab^{T}$ has eigenvalue $b^{T}a$ on the span of $a$ and eigenvalue $0$ on the kernel of $b^{T}$. Therefore
$$
\det(M_0+J)
=
\det(M_0)\left(1+\mathbf{1}^{T}M_0^{-1}\mathbf{1}\right).
$$
Substitution gives
$$
\det(M_0+J)
=
\det(M_0)\frac{9-N}{9-2N}.
$$

Step 5: Simplify the determinant

Since $q^2=2N$,
$$
9-2N=(3+q)(3-q).
$$
Using the determinant from Step 3,
$$
\det(M_0+J)
=
(9-N)(3+q)^{(N+q)/2-1}(3-q)^{(N-q)/2-1}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{(9-N)(3+q)^{(N+q)/2-1}(3-q)^{(N-q)/2-1}}$

---

## Answer

$(9-N)(3+q)^{(N+q)/2-1}(3-q)^{(N-q)/2-1}$

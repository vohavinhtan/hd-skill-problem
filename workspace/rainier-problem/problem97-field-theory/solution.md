## Steps

Step 1: Expose the hidden degree-$p$ polynomial

Set
$$
G(X)=X^p-zX-t.
$$
The defining polynomial is not a generic degree-$p^2$ polynomial. In characteristic $p$,
$$
G(X)^p-G(X)=X^{p^2}-(z+1)X^p+zX-(t^p-t)=F(X),
$$
because $z^p=z$.

Since
$$
Y^p-Y=\prod_{c\in\mathbb F_p}(Y-c),
$$
we obtain
$$
F(X)=\prod_{c\in\mathbb F_p}\left(G(X)-c\right).
$$
For $c\in\mathbb F_p$, put $d=c/(1-z)$. Since $z\neq1$,
$$
G(X+d)=G(X)+(1-z)d=G(X)+c.
$$
Every factor $G(X)-c$ is therefore obtained from $G(X)$ by translating $X$ by an element of $\mathbb F_p$. All these factors have the same splitting field. Consequently, $L$ is the splitting field of
$$
G(X)=X^p-zX-t.
$$

Step 2: Determine the degree of a one-root field

Let $\alpha$ be a root of $G$. Then
$$
t=\alpha^p-z\alpha,
$$
so
$$
K(\alpha)=\mathbb F_p(\alpha).
$$
In particular, this is a rational function field over $\mathbb F_p$.

We show that $[K(\alpha):K]=p$. Let $v_\infty$ be the valuation of $K$ with $v_\infty(t)=-1$, and let $w$ extend it to $K(\alpha)$. Write $e$ for its ramification index, so $w(t)=-e$.

The relation $\alpha^p-z\alpha=t$ forces $w(\alpha)<0$. Therefore
$$
w(\alpha^p-z\alpha)=p\,w(\alpha)=-e.
$$
It follows that $p\mid e$. On the other hand,
$$
[K(\alpha):K]\leq p
$$
because $\alpha$ satisfies $G$. Since
$$
e\leq[K(\alpha):K],
$$
we get
$$
e=[K(\alpha):K]=p.
$$
Thus $G$ is irreducible over $K$.

Step 3: Find the constants required to split $G$

If $\beta$ and $\alpha$ are roots of $G$, their difference $u=\beta-\alpha$ satisfies
$$
u^p-zu=0.
$$
For $u\neq0$ this becomes
$$
u^p=zu.
$$
The congruence $z^2+z+1\equiv0\pmod p$ gives $z^3=1$ and $z\neq1$. Repeated Frobenius gives
$$
u^{p^2}=z^2u,\qquad u^{p^3}=u.
$$
A nonzero such $u$ cannot lie in $\mathbb F_p$ or $\mathbb F_{p^2}$, since that would require respectively $z=1$ or $z^2=1$. Hence
$$
\mathbb F_p(u)=\mathbb F_{p^3}.
$$
The roots of $G$ are exactly
$$
\alpha+\lambda u,\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb F_p.
$$
Therefore
$$
L=\mathbb F_{p^3}(\alpha).
$$
Since $\mathbb F_p(\alpha)$ has constant field $\mathbb F_p$,
$$
[L:K]=3p.
$$

Step 4: Construct two automorphisms of the splitting field

Define $r$ on $L=\mathbb F_{p^3}(\alpha)$ by
$$
r(\alpha)=\alpha+u,\qquad r(u)=u.
$$
The identity $u^p=zu$ shows
$$
(\alpha+u)^p-z(\alpha+u)=\alpha^p-z\alpha=t,
$$
so $r$ fixes $K$. It has order $p$.

Define $s$ by fixing $\alpha$ and applying the Frobenius automorphism to the constant field:
$$
s(\alpha)=\alpha,\qquad s(a)=a^p\quad\text{for }a\in\mathbb F_{p^3}.
$$
This also fixes $K$, and $s$ has order $3$. In particular,
$$
s(u)=u^p=zu.
$$

Step 5: Compute the conjugation relation and close the group

Since $s^{-1}(\alpha)=\alpha$,
$$
srs^{-1}(\alpha)=s(\alpha+u)=\alpha+zu=r^z(\alpha).
$$
Both sides act on the constant field in the same way, so
$$
srs^{-1}=r^z.
$$
The subgroup generated by $r$ and $s$ therefore has presentation
$$
\left\langle r,s\mid r^p=s^3=1,\ srs^{-1}=r^z\right\rangle.
$$
Its elements have the forms
$$
r^is^j,\qquad 0\leq i<p,\quad 0\leq j<3,
$$
and these $3p$ automorphisms are distinct because their restrictions to $\mathbb F_{p^3}$ distinguish $j$, while their action on $\alpha$ then distinguishes $i$.

Step 3 gave $[L:K]=3p$, so a subgroup of $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ already containing $3p$ distinct elements is the full Galois group.

Final Answer: $\boxed{\left\langle r,s\mid r^p=s^3=1,\ srs^{-1}=r^z\right\rangle}$

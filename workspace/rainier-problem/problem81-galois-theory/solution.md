## Steps

Step 1: Determine the cubic radical kernel
Put $g_i=a+y_i$ and $M=K(\omega)$. Since $\prod_i g_i=(-1)^nf(-a)=c^3$, the all-ones vector is a relation among $[g_i]\in M^\times/(M^\times)^3$. An index-two subgroup of $S_n$ is the kernel of a nontrivial map to $C_2$; conjugate transpositions have the same nontrivial image and generate $S_n$, so the kernel is $A_n$. Hence the unique quadratic subfield of $K$ is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt\Delta)$, and $-3\Delta$ nonsquare gives $K\cap\mathbb Q(\omega)=\mathbb Q$.

We show there is no other cubic relation. At primes above $p$, the monic $p$-integral polynomial makes the roots integral, and $p\nmid\Delta$ makes their reductions distinct. An inertia element fixes residues and hence every root, so inertia is trivial. The decomposition group therefore identifies with the residue-field Galois group, generated by $z\mapsto z^p$. A reduced root has Frobenius-orbit length equal to its degree over $\mathbb F_p$, so the displayed factorization gives cycle type $(1,1,1,n-3)$. Since $p\equiv1\pmod3$, Frobenius fixes $\omega$, and the residue field in $M$ is $\mathbb F_{p^{n-3}}$.

A noncube $z\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ stays a noncube there. The cube map has kernel $\{1,\omega,\omega^2\}$, so its image is exactly the $(p-1)/3$ roots of $X^{(p-1)/3}-1$. Thus $z^{(p-1)/3}$ is a nontrivial cube root of unity, whereas
$$
z^{(p^{n-3}-1)/3}=\left(z^{(p-1)/3}\right)^{1+p+\cdots+p^{n-4}}\neq1
$$
because $p\equiv1\pmod3$ and $n-3\equiv2\pmod3$. So the $g_i$ reducing to $a+u$ is not a cube in $M$.

Let $\mathcal R$ be the relation space. It is $S_n$-stable and contains $\mathbf1$. A nonconstant relation, minus its image under a transposition of unequal coordinates, gives a nonzero multiple of $e_i-e_j$; conjugates span $\{z:\sum z_i=0\}$. Since $n\not\equiv0\pmod3$, adjoining $\mathbf1$ gives all $\mathbb F_3^n$, contradicting the noncube above. Thus $\mathcal R=\langle\mathbf1\rangle$.

We need one radical lemma, proved here. If $F$ contains $\omega$ and $[b_1],\ldots,[b_m]$ are independent modulo cubes, then for $\theta_i^3=b_i$ the extension has degree $3^m$ and all phase maps $\theta_i\mapsto\omega^{c_i}\theta_i$. Induct on $m$. For $m-1$, the monomials $\prod_{i<m}\theta_i^{a_i}$ form a basis and the one-dimensional eigenspaces of the phase maps. If $b_m=z^3$ there, then each phase map sends $z$ to a cube-root-of-unity multiple of itself, so $z=q\prod_{i<m}\theta_i^{a_i}$; cubing contradicts independence. Hence $X^3-b_m$ is irreducible. Adjoining a root triples the degree, and because $\omega\in F$, its three roots give the three automorphic extensions of every previous phase map.

Choose $\alpha_i^3=g_i$ with $\prod_i\alpha_i=c$. The first $n-1$ classes are independent, so the lemma gives $u\in\mathbb F_3^n$ with $\sum_i u_i=0$. Put $\beta_{ij}=\alpha_i\alpha_j$. The $P$-roots are $\omega^k\beta_{ij}$, and for distinct $i,j,k$,
$$
\alpha_i=\frac{(\beta_{ij}\beta_{ik}/\beta_{jk})^2}{g_i},
$$
so the pair-product cube roots generate the same field. At the chosen prime all $g_i$ are units because their nonnegative valuations sum to $v(c^3)=0$. Thus $[p]$ is independent of their span: a cube has valuation divisible by $3$, while $v(p)=1$. For $\eta^3=p$, the $Q$-roots are $\omega^k\eta\beta_{ij}$, and dividing by $P$-roots recovers $\eta$. Hence elements of $\operatorname{Gal}(L/M)$ are described by $u\in\mathbb F_3^n$ and $s\in\mathbb F_3$, with
$$
\sum_i u_i=0.
$$
For $\pi$ on $M$ fixing $\omega$, extend it generator by generator: each radical is sent to a root of the $\pi$-image of its cubic, and all such roots lie in $L$. This gives an automorphism of $L$; composing with the kernel gives these phase choices over each $\pi\in S_n$.

Step 2: Convert root cycles into phase sums
For an automorphism fixing $\omega$ and inducing $\pi$,
$$
\sigma(\alpha_i)=\omega^{u_i}\alpha_{\pi(i)},\qquad \sigma(\eta)=\omega^s\eta.
$$
The $B_{ij}$ are distinct: a shared-index equality forces two $g$'s equal; a disjoint one does so after transposing an index with a fifth. After $\ell$ steps around a pair orbit, the root phase is translated by $T$, so $T=0$ gives three $\ell$-cycles and $T\neq0$ one $3\ell$-cycle; for $Q$ use $T+\ell s$.

Write $\alpha,\phi_i,\psi_j$ for the sums of the $u$-coordinates on the $2$-, $5$-, and $7$-cycles of $\pi$. Inside an odd $\ell$-cycle, each distance gives one length-$\ell$ pair orbit; every vertex occurs twice, so its phase is $-U$. Between lengths $a,b$ there are $\gcd(a,b)$ orbits of length $\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)$. Thus: two length-$5$ orbits with phase $-\phi_i$ inside each $5$-cycle; five length-$5$ orbits with phase $\phi_i+\phi_{i'}$ between two $5$-cycles; one length-$35$ orbit with phase $7\phi_i+5\psi_j=\phi_i-\psi_j$ between a $5$- and a $7$-cycle; three length-$7$ orbits with phase $-\psi_j$ inside each $7$-cycle; and seven length-$7$ orbits with phase $\psi_j+\psi_{j'}$ between two $7$-cycles. Pairs meeting the $2$-cycle have lengths $1,10,14$.

Step 3: Derive the distribution equations
Let $n_a=\#\{i:\phi_i=a\}$, $p_a=\#\{j:\psi_j=a\}$, and $q_a=p_{a+s}$, with subscripts modulo $3$. Zero-phase orbit counting gives
$$
2n_0+5\left(\binom{n_0}{2}+n_1n_2\right)=\frac{5r^2-r+8}{6},
$$
$$
n_0p_0+n_1p_1+n_2p_2=\frac{r^2-1}{3},
$$
$$
3q_0+7\left(\binom{q_0}{2}+q_1q_2\right)=\frac{7r^2-r+12}{6}.
$$
With $r=3t+1$, write
$$
n_0=t+1+x,\quad n_{1,2}=t+\frac{-x\pm d}{2},\qquad
q_0=t+1+y,\quad q_{1,2}=t+\frac{-y\pm e}{2}.
$$
Subtracting the right sides from the first and third left sides gives, respectively,
$$
\frac{-5d^2+15x^2+18x}{4},\qquad
\frac{-7e^2+21y^2+26y}{4},
$$
so
$$
5d^2=15x^2+18x,\qquad 7e^2=21y^2+26y.
$$
Since $p_b=q_{b-s}$, the middle left side minus its right side equals, for $s=0,1,2$,
$$
\frac{de+3xy+2x+2y+2}{2},
$$
$$
-\frac{de-3dy-2d+3ex+2e+3xy+2x+2y}{4},
\qquad
-\frac{de+3dy+2d-3ex-2e+3xy+2x+2y}{4}.
$$
Each numerator therefore vanishes.

Step 4: Classify the $5$-cycle distributions
Set $A=15x+9$, $C=5d$. Then $A^2-3C^2=81$. Writing $A=3A_1$ gives $3A_1^2-C^2=27$, so $3\mid C$; then $C=3C_1$ gives $A_1^2-3C_1^2=9$. Repeating shows $3\mid A_1,C_1$, hence $9\mid A,C$. Thus $U=A/9$, $V=C/9$ satisfy $U^2-3V^2=1$.

Put $\lambda=2+\sqrt3$. After changing sign and conjugating, take $\varepsilon=U+V\sqrt3\geq1$ and choose $k\geq0$ with $\lambda^k\leq\varepsilon<\lambda^{k+1}$. Then $\delta=\varepsilon\lambda^{-k}=m+n\sqrt3$ is integral, has norm $1$, and lies in $[1,\lambda)$. Its conjugate is $\delta^{-1}\leq1$, so $n\geq0$; if $n\geq1$, then $m^2=1+3n^2$ gives $m\geq2$ and $\delta\geq\lambda$, impossible. Thus $\delta=1$ and
$$
U+V\sqrt3=\pm(2+\sqrt3)^k.
$$
Because $5\mid V$ and $(2+\sqrt3)^3=26+15\sqrt3\equiv1\pmod5$, while the $\sqrt3$-coefficients for exponents $1,2$ are $1,4$, we get $3\mid k$. Swapping labels makes $d\geq0$, and $A\equiv9\pmod{15}$ selects the positive branch:
$$
A+5d\sqrt3=9(2+\sqrt3)^{3j},\qquad j\geq0.
$$
First pairs: $(0,0),(15,27),(810,1404)$. Multiplication by $26+15\sqrt3$ gives
$$
A_{j+1}=26A_j+225d_j,\qquad d_{j+1}=3A_j+26d_j,
$$
so $d_3=72981$ and $d_j$ increases for $j\geq1$.

Step 5: Eliminate shifted nonzero branches
For $x=d=0$, $s=0$ gives $y=-1$, impossible in $7e^2=21y^2+26y$; for $s=1,2$ one gets $e=-y,e=y$, and then $y=e=0$. These are the two balanced branches.

Now $j\geq1$, so $x\geq15$ and $x<d<2x$. For $s=2$,
$$
e=-\frac{(3d+3x+2)y+2d+2x}{d-3x-2}.
$$
Substitution into $7e^2=21y^2+26y$ gives a quadratic $ay^2+by+c=0$. After using $5d^2=15x^2+18x$ its coefficients are
$$
a=\frac{28}{5}(45dx+30d-3x-10),
$$
$$
b=\frac45(405dx+200d+30x^2-59x-130),\qquad
c=\frac{56x}{5}(5d+10x+9).
$$
Then
$$
b^2-4ac=8(-d+3x+2)^2(6x^2+47x+338-1011dx-702d)<0,
$$
because the last factor is less than $-1005x^2-655x+338$.

For $s=1$,
$$
e=\frac{(3d-3x-2)y+2d-2x}{d+3x+2},
$$
and substitution gives
$$
H(y)=7((3d-3x-2)y+2d-2x)^2-(d+3x+2)^2(21y^2+26y)=0.
$$
Its leading coefficient is negative, while
$$
H(-2)=16(-26dx-22d+4x^2+8x-1)<0,
$$
$$
H(-1)=\frac85(10dx-5d+55x^2+82x+30)>0,
$$
$$
H(0)=\frac{56x}{5}(-5d+10x+9)>0,
$$
$$
H(1)=\frac85(-395dx-205d+85x^2+23x-100)<0.
$$
Using $x<d<2x$ and $x\geq15$, the two roots lie in $(-2,-1)$ and $(0,1)$, so no integer $y$ occurs. Swapping labels $1,2$ covers $d<0$.

Step 6: Isolate the unshifted exceptional branch
For $s=0$ and $d\neq0$,
$$
e=-\frac{(3x+2)y+2x+2}{d}.
$$
Using $5d^2=15x^2+18x$, the equation $7e^2=21y^2+26y$ becomes
$$
35((3x+2)y+2x+2)^2-(15x^2+18x)(21y^2+26y)=0,
$$
hence, after expansion and division by $2$,
$$
(21x+70)y^2+(15x^2+116x+140)y+70(x+1)^2=0.
$$
Its discriminant is
$$
(15x^2+116x+140)^2-280(21x+70)(x+1)^2
$$
$$
=3x(5x+6)(15x^2-178x-700)
=d^2(75x^2-890x-3500)=d^2S^2.
$$
Since $A=15x+9$, also $3S^2=A^2-196A-8817$. For $j=1$, $(x,d)=(15,27)$ and the quadratic is a nonzero multiple of $(y+7)(77y+512)$, giving $y=-7,e=11$. For $j=2$, $S^2=48483100$ lies between $6962^2$ and $6963^2$.

For $j\geq3$, $d\geq72981$ and $A^2=75d^2+81$, so $\sqrt{75}<A/d<26/3$. Therefore
$$
3(S^2-(5d-56)^2)=1680d-196A-18144<0,
$$
$$
3(S^2-(5d-57)^2)=1710d-196A-18483>\frac{34}{3}d-18483>0.
$$
Thus $S^2$ lies between consecutive squares. The only nonzero branch is $(15,27,-7,11,0)$ and its simultaneous label swap.

Step 7: Count the automorphisms
Balanced branches use
$$
(n_0,n_1,n_2)=(t+1,t,t)
$$
and $p$-distribution $(t,t+1,t)$ or $(t,t,t+1)$, hence contribution
$$
2\left(\frac{r!}{(t+1)!t!^2}\right)^2.
$$
Exceptional branch:
$$
(n_0,n_1,n_2)=(t+16,t+6,t-21),\quad(p_0,p_1,p_2)=(t-6,t+9,t-2),
$$
and its simultaneous $1,2$ swap give
$$
2\frac{r!}{(t+16)!(t+6)!(t-21)!}\frac{r!}{(t-6)!(t+9)!(t-2)!}.
$$
Fixing a sum on an index cycle of length $\ell$ leaves $3^{\ell-1}$ assignments; the $2$-cycle sum is forced globally. Thus the $2r+1$ cycles give $3^{n-(2r+1)}=3^{10r+1}$ phase vectors. There are
$$
\frac{n!}{2\cdot35^r(r!)^2}.
$$
Multiplication and cancellation of $(r!)^2$ give the result.
Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{3^{10r+1}n!}{35^r}(\frac{1}{(t+1)!^2t!^4}+\frac{1}{(t+16)!(t+6)!(t-21)!(t-6)!(t+9)!(t-2)!})}$

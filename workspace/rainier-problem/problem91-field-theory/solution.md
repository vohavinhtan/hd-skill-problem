## Steps

Step 1: Recover the two hidden cubic factors

Put
$$
Y=X^3-3X.
$$
Then
$$
F_p(X)
=
(t^2-8)Y^2
-2(t-2)(t^2-4)Y
+t(t^3-4t^2+16).
$$

Set
$$
r=\frac{t^3-4t^2+16}{t^2-8}.
$$
A direct expansion gives
$$
t+r=\frac{2(t-2)(t^2-4)}{t^2-8},
$$
and
$$
tr=\frac{t(t^3-4t^2+16)}{t^2-8}.
$$
Therefore
$$
F_p(X)
=
(t^2-8)(Y-t)(Y-r).
$$

Thus the splitting field of $F_p$ is the compositum
$$
L_p=M_1M_2,
$$
where $M_1$ and $M_2$ are the splitting fields over $K$ of
$$
f_1(X)=X^3-3X-t
$$
and
$$
f_2(X)=X^3-3X-r,
$$
respectively.

Step 2: Show that both cubic splitting fields have degree six

Consider first $f_1$. At the place at infinity of $K$, normalize the valuation by
$$
v_\infty(t)=-1.
$$
The Newton polygon of
$$
X^3-3X-t
$$
has one segment joining
$$
(0,-1)
\qquad\text{to}\qquad
(3,0).
$$
Its slope is $1/3$, because the point $(1,0)$ lies strictly above that segment. Hence every root has valuation
$$
-\frac13.
$$
A field containing a root therefore has ramification index divisible by $3$. Since $f_1$ has degree $3$, it is irreducible over $K$.

For $f_2$, choose a geometric place $P$ satisfying
$$
t^2=8.
$$
The numerator of $r$ reduces there to
$$
t^3-4t^2+16=8t-16=8(t-2),
$$
which is nonzero because $t^2=8$ cannot hold together with $t=2$ in characteristic $p\geq5$. Thus
$$
v_P(r)=-1.
$$
The same Newton-polygon argument applied to
$$
X^3-3X-r
$$
shows that $f_2$ is also irreducible.

For
$$
f_a(X)=X^3-3X-a,
$$
the discriminant is
$$
\operatorname{disc}(f_a)=27(4-a^2).
$$
Hence
$$
\Delta_1=27(4-t^2).
$$
This is not a square in $K$, since it has simple zeros at the geometric places $t=2$ and $t=-2$.

The identity
$$
4-r^2
=
\left(\frac{t(t-4)}{t^2-8}\right)^2(4-t^2)
$$
gives
$$
\Delta_2
=
\left(\frac{t(t-4)}{t^2-8}\right)^2\Delta_1.
$$
Thus $\Delta_2$ is also nonsquare.

An irreducible separable cubic has Galois group contained in $A_3$ exactly when its discriminant is a square. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(M_1/K)\cong S_3,
\qquad
\operatorname{Gal}(M_2/K)\cong S_3,
$$
and
$$
[M_1:K]=[M_2:K]=6.
$$

Step 3: Identify a common quadratic subfield

For an $S_3$ splitting field of a separable cubic, the fixed field of $A_3$ is generated by the square root of the discriminant. From Step 2,
$$
\Delta_2
=
\left(\frac{t(t-4)}{t^2-8}\right)^2\Delta_1.
$$
Hence
$$
K(\sqrt{\Delta_1})=K(\sqrt{\Delta_2}).
$$

Put
$$
Q=K\left(\sqrt{27(4-t^2)}\right).
$$
Then
$$
Q\subseteq M_1\cap M_2
$$
and
$$
[Q:K]=2.
$$

Step 4: Prove that the two splitting fields are not equal

Return to a geometric place $P$ with
$$
t^2=8.
$$
For $f_1$,
$$
\Delta_1(P)=27(4-8)=-108\ne0
$$
because $p\geq5$. Its coefficients are integral at $P$, and its discriminant is a unit. Therefore the splitting field $M_1/K$ is unramified at $P$.

For $f_2$, Step 2 gave
$$
v_P(r)=-1.
$$
Its Newton polygon has slope $1/3$, so adjoining any root produces ramification index divisible by $3$. Hence
$$
M_2/K
$$
is ramified at $P$.

Therefore
$$
M_1\ne M_2.
$$

Step 5: Determine the intersection and the compositum degree

Let
$$
I=M_1\cap M_2.
$$
Because both $M_1/K$ and $M_2/K$ are Galois, the extension $I/K$ is Galois.

By Step 3,
$$
Q\subseteq I.
$$
The normal subgroups of $S_3$ are
$$
S_3,\qquad A_3,\qquad\{1\}.
$$
Consequently the only Galois intermediate fields of an $S_3$ extension have degrees
$$
1,\qquad2,\qquad6
$$
over the base field.

Since $[Q:K]=2$, either
$$
I=Q
$$
or
$$
I=M_1.
$$
The second possibility would imply
$$
M_1\subseteq M_2.
$$
Both fields have degree $6$ over $K$, so this would force $M_1=M_2$, contradicting Step 4. Hence
$$
M_1\cap M_2=Q.
$$

Finally,
$$
[L_p:K]
=
[M_1M_2:K]
=
\frac{[M_1:K][M_2:K]}{[M_1\cap M_2:K]}.
$$
Therefore
$$
[L_p:K]
=
\frac{6\cdot6}{2}
=
18.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{18}$

## Answer

$18$

## Steps

Step 1: Determine the branch data of the degree-$n$ root field

Put
$$
F=\mathbb F_p(t).
$$
If $x$ is a root of $F_p(X)$, then
$$
t=\phi(x),
\qquad
\phi(X)=\frac{nX-X^n}{n-1}.
$$
At the pole of $X$, the function $\phi(X)$ has pole order $n$. Hence
$$
[\mathbb F_p(X):F]=n,
$$
so $F_p(X)$ is irreducible over $F$.

Differentiate:
$$
\phi'(X)=\frac{n(1-X^{n-1})}{n-1}.
$$
Since $n<p$, the finite critical points are the $n-1$ distinct roots $c$ of
$$
c^{n-1}=1.
$$
For such $c$,
$$
\phi(c)=c
$$
and
$$
\phi''(c)=-nc^{n-2}\ne0.
$$
Thus the finite branch divisor is
$$
t^{n-1}=1,
$$
and every finite inertia group has order $2$.

At infinity, $\phi$ has ramification index $n$. All ramification is tame because
$$
n<p.
$$

Step 2: Identify the Galois closure over the rational field

Let $M$ be the splitting field of $F_p$ over $F$, and let $G$ be its geometric Galois group on the $n$ roots.

Irreducibility from Step 1 makes $G$ transitive. The finite inertia generators are transpositions. Their product is the inverse of the inertia generator at infinity, which is an $n$-cycle. Hence the subgroup generated by the finite transpositions contains an $n$-cycle and is transitive.

Associate a graph to those transpositions, with the $n$ roots as vertices and one edge for every generating transposition. The subgroup generated by the edge transpositions preserves each connected component, and on each component it generates the full symmetric group. Transitivity therefore forces the graph to be connected, so
$$
G=S_n.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(M/F)\cong S_n.
$$

Step 3: Analyze the cubic base field and its intersection with $M$

Because
$$
p\equiv1\pmod3,
$$
the field $\mathbb F_p$ contains the cube roots of unity. The equation
$$
u^3=t^{n-1}-1
$$
therefore defines a cyclic Kummer extension
$$
K_p/F
$$
of degree $3$. It really has degree $3$, since every zero of $t^{n-1}-1$ has valuation $1$, whereas a cube has all valuations divisible by $3$.

Also
$$
p=6r+1
$$
for some $r$, so
$$
n=3r.
$$
Hence
$$
\gcd(3,n-1)=1.
$$

Over an algebraic closure of the constant field, the polynomial $t^{n-1}-1$ has $n-1$ distinct zeros. Each is totally ramified in $K_p/F$. Infinity is also totally ramified because
$$
v_\infty(t^{n-1}-1)=-(n-1)
$$
is prime to $3$.

Thus the degree-$3$ cover has exactly $n$ branch points, all of ramification index $3$. Riemann-Hurwitz gives
$$
2g(K_p)-2=3(-2)+n(3-1)=2n-6,
$$
so
$$
g(K_p)=n-2.
$$

Now
$$
M\cap K_p=F.
$$
Indeed, since $[K_p:F]=3$ is prime, a nontrivial intersection would force
$$
K_p\subseteq M.
$$
Because $K_p/F$ is Galois, this would give a quotient of $S_n$ of order $3$. No such quotient exists: the abelianization of $S_n$ has order $2$. Therefore $M$ and $K_p$ are linearly disjoint over $F$.

Consequently
$$
L_p=MK_p,
\qquad
\operatorname{Gal}(L_p/K_p)\cong S_n,
$$
and
$$
[L_p:K_p]=n!.
$$

Step 4: Determine how the base change alters the inertia groups

For tame extensions of a local field with ramification indices $a$ and $b$, their compositum has ramification index
$$
\operatorname{lcm}(a,b).
$$
Indeed, after an unramified extension both are obtained by adjoining appropriate roots of a uniformizer. Therefore, relative to the degree-$b$ extension, the remaining ramification index is
$$
\frac{a}{\gcd(a,b)}.
$$

At each of the $n-1$ finite branch points from Step 1, the extension $M/F$ has index $2$, while $K_p/F$ has index $3$. Hence the corresponding point of $K_p$ has ramification index
$$
\frac{2}{\gcd(2,3)}=2
$$
in $L_p/K_p$.

At infinity, the indices over $F$ are
$$
n
$$
for $M/F$ and
$$
3
$$
for $K_p/F$. Since $3\mid n$, the remaining index in $L_p/K_p$ is
$$
\frac n3.
$$

There are no other branch points.

Step 5: Apply Riemann-Hurwitz after the cubic base change

Set
$$
N=n!.
$$
By Step 3,
$$
2g(K_p)-2=2n-6.
$$
The $n-1$ finite branch points each have inertia order $2$ in the Galois extension $L_p/K_p$, so together they contribute
$$
N\frac{n-1}{2}
$$
to the different degree.

The point above infinity has inertia order $n/3$, so its contribution is
$$
N\left(1-\frac{3}{n}\right).
$$

Riemann-Hurwitz now gives
$$
2g(L_p)-2
=
N\left(
2n-6+\frac{n-1}{2}+1-\frac3n
\right).
$$
The expression in parentheses is
$$
\frac{5n-11}{2}-\frac3n.
$$
Hence
$$
2g(L_p)-2
=
\frac{(n-1)!(5n^2-11n-6)}{2}.
$$
Therefore
$$
g(L_p)
=
1+\frac{(n-1)!(5n^2-11n-6)}4.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{1+\frac{(n-1)!(5n^2-11n-6)}4}$

## Answer

## Steps

Step 1: Determine the ramification of the degree-$n$ root field

Work first over
$$
K=\mathbb F_p(t).
$$
Let $x$ be transcendental and impose
$$
t=\frac{nx-x^n}{n-1}.
$$
Then $x$ satisfies
$$
x^n-nx+(n-1)t=0.
$$

At the pole of $x$, the function $t$ has pole order $n$. Hence
$$
[\mathbb F_p(x):\mathbb F_p(t)]\geq n.
$$
The displayed polynomial has degree $n$, so equality holds. Therefore $F_p$ is irreducible over $K$.

Differentiate the rational map:
$$
\frac{dt}{dx}=\frac{n(1-x^{n-1})}{n-1}.
$$
Since $p>n$, its finite critical points are the $n-1$ distinct solutions of
$$
x^{n-1}=1.
$$
At such a point $c$,
$$
t(c)=\frac{nc-c^n}{n-1}=c,
$$
and
$$
\frac{d^2t}{dx^2}(c)=-nc^{n-2}\ne0.
$$
Thus the finite branch points are exactly
$$
t^{n-1}=1,
$$
and each has ramification index $2$.

At infinity, $t$ has pole order $n$, so the ramification index is $n$. All ramification is tame because
$$
p>n.
$$

Step 2: Identify the Galois group of the splitting field over $\mathbb F_p(t)$

Let $M$ be the splitting field of $F_p$ over $K$, and let $G$ be its geometric Galois group acting on the $n$ roots.

Irreducibility from Step 1 makes this action transitive. Each finite branch point has inertia generated by a transposition, while inertia at infinity is generated by an $n$-cycle.

The tame branch cycles satisfy that their product is $1$. Hence the finite inertia transpositions generate a subgroup containing the inverse of the $n$-cycle, so that subgroup is transitive.

A subgroup of $S_n$ generated by transpositions is obtained by taking the symmetric groups on the connected components of the graph whose edges are those transpositions. Since the subgroup here is transitive, that graph is connected. Therefore the finite inertia transpositions generate
$$
S_n.
$$
Hence
$$
G=S_n.
$$
The geometric group is already $S_n$, so the arithmetic Galois group over $\mathbb F_p(t)$ is also $S_n$.

Step 3: Show that the prescribed quadratic field is the alternating fixed field

For
$$
f(X)=X^n-nX+(n-1)t,
$$
the formula for the discriminant of $X^n+aX+b$ gives
$$
\operatorname{disc}_X(f)
=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}
\left(
n^n((n-1)t)^{n-1}
+(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)^{n-1}(-n)^n
\right).
$$
The second term inside the parentheses equals
$$
-n^n(n-1)^{n-1},
$$
so
$$
\operatorname{disc}_X(f)
=\delta(t^{n-1}-1).
$$

The polynomial $t^{n-1}-1$ has simple zeros, so this discriminant is not a square in $K$.

If $r_1,\ldots,r_n$ are the roots, then
$$
D=\prod_{i<j}(r_i-r_j)
$$
satisfies
$$
D^2=\operatorname{disc}_X(f).
$$
A permutation of the roots sends $D$ to its sign times $D$. Since the Galois group is $S_n$, the fixed field of $A_n$ is therefore
$$
K(D)
=
\mathbb F_p(t)\left(\sqrt{\delta(t^{n-1}-1)}\right)
=
K_p.
$$
Consequently
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(L_p/K_p)\cong A_n,
\qquad
[L_p:K_p]=\frac{n!}{2}.
$$

Step 4: Determine the genus of $K_p$ and the remaining ramification

Genus is unchanged after extending the constant field, so ramification may be counted geometrically.

The quadratic cover
$$
K_p/K
$$
is branched at the $n-1$ finite roots of
$$
t^{n-1}=1.
$$

If $n$ is odd, then $n-1$ is even and infinity is unramified. Riemann-Hurwitz gives
$$
2g(K_p)-2=-4+(n-1)=n-5.
$$
There are two points of $K_p$ above infinity.

Every finite inertia group in $M/K$ is generated by a transposition, which lies outside $A_n$. Hence its intersection with
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(L_p/K_p)=A_n
$$
is trivial. Thus all finite branch points become unramified in $L_p/K_p$.

The inertia at infinity is generated by an $n$-cycle. For odd $n$, this cycle is even, so each of the two points above infinity has ramification index
$$
n
$$
in $L_p/K_p$.

If $n$ is even, then infinity is also ramified in the quadratic extension $K_p/K$. The total number of branch points of that quadratic cover is $n$, so
$$
2g(K_p)-2=-4+n=n-4.
$$
There is one point above infinity.

An $n$-cycle is odd when $n$ is even. Its intersection with $A_n$ is the subgroup generated by its square, of order
$$
\frac n2.
$$
Hence the unique point above infinity has ramification index $n/2$ in $L_p/K_p$.

Step 5: Apply Riemann-Hurwitz in both parity cases

Put
$$
N=\frac{n!}{2}.
$$

Suppose first that $n$ is odd. By Step 4,
$$
2g(K_p)-2=n-5.
$$
There are two ramified points, each with inertia order $n$. Since the extension is Galois, each contributes
$$
N\left(1-\frac1n\right)
$$
to the different degree. Therefore
$$
2g(L_p)-2
=N(n-5)+2N\left(1-\frac1n\right).
$$
Substituting $N=n!/2$ gives
$$
2g(L_p)-2
=\frac{(n-1)!(n^2-3n-2)}{2}.
$$

Now suppose that $n$ is even. Then
$$
2g(K_p)-2=n-4,
$$
and the single ramified point has inertia order $n/2$. Hence
$$
2g(L_p)-2
=N(n-4)+N\left(1-\frac2n\right).
$$
This simplifies to the same expression,
$$
2g(L_p)-2
=\frac{(n-1)!(n^2-3n-2)}{2}.
$$

Therefore, in every case,
$$
g(L_p)=1+\frac{(n-1)!(n^2-3n-2)}4.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{1+\frac{(n-1)!(n^2-3n-2)}4}$

## Steps

Step 1: Recover the hidden constant field

Since
$$
n=p^2+p+1
$$
divides
$$
p^3-1=(p-1)(p^2+p+1),
$$
there exists an element $\zeta\in\mathbb F_{p^3}^\times$ of order $n$.

Also
$$
n>p-1,
$$
so $\zeta\notin\mathbb F_p$. Because the only subfields of $\mathbb F_{p^3}$ containing $\mathbb F_p$ have degrees dividing $3$,
$$
[\mathbb F_p(\zeta):\mathbb F_p]=3.
$$
Thus
$$
C=\mathbb F_{p^3}(t)
$$
satisfies
$$
[C:K]=3.
$$

We claim that
$$
C\subseteq L_p.
$$
Over $C$, choose a root $\rho$ of any one of the three displayed factors. If $\xi\in\mu_n$, then $\xi\rho$ is another root of the same factor. In particular both
$$
\rho
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\zeta\rho
$$
belong to the splitting field. Since $\rho\ne0$,
$$
\zeta=\frac{\zeta\rho}{\rho}\in L_p.
$$
Hence
$$
C=K(\zeta)\subseteq L_p.
$$

Step 2: Rewrite the problem over the constant field

Put
$$
x_i=t-\zeta_i
$$
for $i\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$, and define
$$
f_i=\frac{x_i^2}{x_{i-1}}.
$$
Over $C$ the $i$th factor becomes
$$
x_{i-1}(X^n-f_i).
$$

Because
$$
\mu_n\subseteq\mathbb F_{p^3}\subset C,
$$
the splitting field over $C$ is
$$
L_p=C(r_0,r_1,r_2),
\qquad
r_i^n=f_i.
$$

Also
$$
p\nmid n
$$
because
$$
n\equiv1\pmod p.
$$
Thus this is a separable Kummer extension.

For fields containing $\mu_n$, Kummer theory identifies
$$
[L_p:C]
$$
with the order of the subgroup generated by
$$
[f_0],[f_1],[f_2]
$$
in
$$
C^\times/C^{\times n}.
$$
It therefore remains to determine all relations among these three classes.

Step 3: Determine every possible Kummer relation

Suppose
$$
f_0^{e_0}f_1^{e_1}f_2^{e_2}\in C^{\times n},
$$
where the $e_i$ are taken modulo $n$.

At the place
$$
x_0=t-\zeta_0=0,
$$
the valuations of $f_0,f_1,f_2$ are respectively
$$
2,-1,0.
$$
Hence
$$
2e_0-e_1\equiv0\pmod n.
$$

Similarly, the places $x_1=0$ and $x_2=0$ give
$$
2e_1-e_2\equiv0\pmod n,
$$
and
$$
2e_2-e_0\equiv0\pmod n.
$$

Therefore
$$
e_1\equiv2e_0\pmod n,
$$
and
$$
e_2\equiv4e_0\pmod n.
$$
Substitution into the third congruence gives
$$
7e_0\equiv0\pmod n.
$$

Put
$$
g=\gcd(n,7).
$$
There are therefore at most $g$ relation vectors.

Since $7$ is prime,
$$
g\in\{1,7\}.
$$
If $g=1$, the only possible relation is the trivial one.

Step 4: Show that all exceptional relations really occur

There is an exact identity in $C^\times$:
$$
f_0f_1^2f_2^4
=
\frac{x_0^2}{x_2}
\left(\frac{x_1^2}{x_0}\right)^2
\left(\frac{x_2^2}{x_1}\right)^4.
$$
Cancelling powers gives
$$
f_0f_1^2f_2^4=x_2^7.
$$

Suppose now that
$$
7\mid n.
$$
Write
$$
n=7m.
$$
Raising the preceding identity to the $m$th power gives
$$
f_0^m f_1^{2m} f_2^{4m}
=
x_2^n.
$$
Thus
$$
(m,2m,4m)
$$
is a nontrivial Kummer relation modulo $n$.

Its multiples give exactly seven relation vectors. Step 3 showed that no others are possible. Hence in all cases the relation subgroup has order
$$
g=\gcd(n,7).
$$

The subgroup generated by the three Kummer classes therefore has order
$$
\frac{n^3}{g}.
$$
Consequently
$$
[L_p:C]
=
\frac{n^3}{\gcd(n,7)}.
$$

Step 5: Restore the constant-field degree

Step 1 gave
$$
[C:K]=3
$$
and
$$
C\subseteq L_p.
$$
Therefore
$$
[L_p:K]
=
[L_p:C][C:K].
$$
Using Step 4,
$$
[L_p:K]
=
\frac{3n^3}{\gcd(n,7)}.
$$
Finally,
$$
n=p^2+p+1,
$$
so
$$
[L_p:K]
=
\frac{3(p^2+p+1)^3}
{\gcd(p^2+p+1,7)}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{3(p^2+p+1)^3}{\gcd(p^2+p+1,7)}}$

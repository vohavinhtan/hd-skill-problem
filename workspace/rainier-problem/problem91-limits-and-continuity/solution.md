## Steps

Step 1: Reconstruct the group generated by the two hidden transformations

Introduce
$$
\sigma(x,y)=(\zeta x,\zeta^{-4}y)
$$
and
$$
\tau(x,y)=\left(\frac{y}{x},\frac1x\right).
$$
The projective form of $\tau$ is
$$
[X:Y:Z]\longmapsto[Y:Z:X].
$$
Since
$$
Y^n+\omega Z^n+\omega^2X^n
=
\omega^2\left(X^n+\omega Y^n+\omega^2Z^n\right),
$$
the map $\tau$ preserves the curve. The map $\sigma$ also preserves it because
$$
\zeta^n=1.
$$

Let $\alpha,\beta$ denote the two automorphisms stated in the problem. Their formulas give
$$
\alpha=\sigma\tau,
\qquad
\beta=\sigma\tau^2.
$$

A direct conjugation yields
$$
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{16}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\alpha\beta
=
\sigma\tau\sigma\tau^2
=
\sigma^{17}.
$$
Since
$$
17\cdot5\equiv1\pmod{21},
$$
we recover
$$
\sigma=(\alpha\beta)^5.
$$
Then
$$
\tau=\sigma^{-1}\alpha.
$$
Thus the common fixed field of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ is the fixed field of
$$
H=\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle.
$$

We have
$$
\sigma^{21}=\tau^3=1.
$$
Also
$$
16^3\equiv1\pmod{21},
\qquad
16\not\equiv1\pmod{21}.
$$
Hence $\tau$ acts nontrivially with order $3$ on the cyclic group generated by $\sigma$, so
$$
|H|=63
$$
and
$$
K_Q=L_Q^H.
$$

Step 2: Compute the genus of the covering curve

Write the smooth projective model as
$$
C_Q:\quad X^n+\omega Y^n+\omega^2Z^n=0.
$$
The characteristic of $\mathbb F_Q$ does not divide
$$
n=Q-1,
$$
so the three partial derivatives cannot vanish simultaneously at a projective point. The curve is smooth.

A smooth plane curve of degree $n$ has genus
$$
g(C_Q)=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}2.
$$
Therefore
$$
2g(C_Q)-2=n(n-3).
$$

Since $63\mid Q-1$, the characteristic does not divide $63$. The quotient by $H$ is tame.

Step 3: Show that the nonidentity diagonal elements have no fixed points

For
$$
1\leq j\leq20,
$$
the automorphism $\sigma^j$ acts projectively by
$$
[X:Y:Z]\longmapsto
[\zeta^jX:\zeta^{-4j}Y:Z].
$$
Its three eigenvalues are
$$
\zeta^j,\qquad
\zeta^{-4j},\qquad
1.
$$
They are pairwise distinct. Indeed, equality of the first and third or the second and third would force $21\mid j$, while equality of the first two would give
$$
21\mid5j,
$$
again forcing $21\mid j$.

A fixed projective point must therefore be one of the three coordinate points. None lies on $C_Q$. Hence
$$
\#\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^j)=0
$$
for every nonidentity element of $\langle\sigma\rangle$.

Step 4: Count the fixed points in the two nontrivial cosets

Consider
$$
g_j=\sigma^j\tau.
$$
Projectively,
$$
g_j[X:Y:Z]
=
[\zeta^jY:\zeta^{-4j}Z:X].
$$
At an eigenpoint with eigenvalue $\lambda$,
$$
\zeta^jY=\lambda X,
$$
$$
\zeta^{-4j}Z=\lambda Y,
$$
$$
X=\lambda Z.
$$
Multiplying gives
$$
\lambda^3=\zeta^{-3j}.
$$

Because $3\mid n$,
$$
\lambda^n
=
(\lambda^3)^{n/3}
=
\zeta^{-jn}
=
1.
$$
The eigenvector equations then give
$$
X^n=Y^n=Z^n.
$$
Every eigenline therefore lies on $C_Q$, since
$$
X^n+\omega Y^n+\omega^2Z^n
=
X^n(1+\omega+\omega^2)
=
0.
$$

The equation
$$
T^3-\zeta^{-3j}=0
$$
has three distinct roots because the characteristic is not $3$. Thus
$$
\#\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^j\tau)=3.
$$

The inverse of an element in the coset $\langle\sigma\rangle\tau^2$ lies in $\langle\sigma\rangle\tau$, and an automorphism and its inverse have the same fixed points. Hence every element of the second nontrivial coset also fixes exactly three points.

There are $21$ elements in each coset. The full fixed-point contribution from nonidentity elements of $H$ is therefore
$$
R=21\cdot3+21\cdot3=126.
$$

Step 5: Determine the exact genus correction and evaluate the limit

The tame group-action form of Riemann-Hurwitz gives
$$
n(n-3)
=
63(2g(K_Q)-2)+126.
$$
Rearranging,
$$
2g(K_Q)
=
\frac{n(n-3)}{63},
$$
so
$$
g(K_Q)=\frac{n(n-3)}{126}.
$$
Since
$$
n=Q-1,
$$
we obtain
$$
126g(K_Q)
=
(Q-1)(Q-4)
=
Q^2-5Q+4.
$$
Therefore, for every admissible $Q$,
$$
126g(K_Q)-Q^2+5Q=4.
$$
The requested limit is consequently equal to $4$.

Final Answer: $\boxed{4}$

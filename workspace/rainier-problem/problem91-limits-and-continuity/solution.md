## Steps

Step 1: Reconstruct the group hidden by the two given automorphisms

Write the smooth projective model as
$$
C_Q:\quad X^n+Y^n+Z^n=0.
$$
The two stated automorphisms are induced by
$$
\phi[X:Y:Z]=[-X:Z:Y],
$$
$$
\psi[X:Y:Z]=[-Z:-Y:X].
$$
They preserve $C_Q$ because $n=Q-1$ is even.

Their composition is
$$
a=\psi\phi,
\qquad
a[X:Y:Z]=[Y:Z:X].
$$
Thus $a$ cyclically permutes the coordinates.

Set
$$
b=a^{-1}\phi.
$$
Then
$$
b[X:Y:Z]=[Y:-X:Z].
$$
Hence
$$
b^2[X:Y:Z]=[-X:-Y:Z].
$$
Conjugating $b^2$ by $a$ gives the other two nontrivial projective sign changes. These three elements together with the identity form
$$
D\cong C_2\times C_2.
$$

Modulo $D$, the maps $a$ and $b$ induce respectively a $3$-cycle and a transposition of the coordinates, so their images generate $S_3$. Every element generated by $a,b$ is a signed coordinate permutation. The full projective signed-permutation group has
$$
4\cdot6=24
$$
elements. Since the generated group contains $D$ and maps onto $S_3$, it has at least $24$ elements and therefore equals that full group.

Thus, with
$$
G=\langle\phi,\psi\rangle,
$$
we have
$$
|G|=24
$$
and
$$
K_Q=L_Q^G.
$$

Step 2: Compute the genus of the covering curve

The characteristic of $\mathbb F_Q$ does not divide $n=Q-1$, so the three partial derivatives of
$$
X^n+Y^n+Z^n
$$
cannot vanish simultaneously at a projective point. Hence $C_Q$ is smooth.

A smooth plane curve of degree $n$ has
$$
g(C_Q)=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}2,
$$
so
$$
2g(C_Q)-2=n(n-3).
$$

Since $Q\equiv17\pmod{24}$, the characteristic is neither $2$ nor $3$. Therefore the order-$24$ action of $G$ is tame.

Step 3: Count fixed points of the nonidentity sign changes

The subgroup $D$ has three nonidentity elements. A representative is
$$
[X:Y:Z]\longmapsto[-X:-Y:Z].
$$
Its projective eigenspaces are the line
$$
Z=0
$$
and the isolated point
$$
[0:0:1].
$$
The isolated point is not on $C_Q$.

On $Z=0$, the curve equation is
$$
X^n+Y^n=0.
$$
Because the characteristic does not divide $n$, this has exactly $n$ distinct projective solutions over the algebraic closure.

All three nonidentity elements of $D$ are conjugate under coordinate permutations, so each fixes exactly $n$ points. Their total contribution is
$$
3n.
$$

Step 4: Separate the two types of elements above coordinate transpositions

Fix the transposition exchanging $X$ and $Y$. The four signed lifts can be written
$$
[X:Y:Z]\longmapsto
[\varepsilon Y:\delta X:Z],
\qquad
\varepsilon,\delta\in\{1,-1\}.
$$

If
$$
\varepsilon\delta=1,
$$
the $X,Y$ block has eigenvalues $1$ and $-1$. The $1$-eigenvector together with the $Z$-axis forms a projective line. On this line the Fermat equation becomes
$$
2X^n+Z^n=0,
$$
so there are exactly $n$ fixed points.

The isolated $-1$ eigenline lies in $Z=0$. Since $n$ is even, its two nonzero coordinates have equal $n$th powers, so it does not lie on $C_Q$.

If
$$
\varepsilon\delta=-1,
$$
the $X,Y$ eigenvalues are $i$ and $-i$, while the $Z$ eigenvalue is $1$. Since
$$
Q\equiv17\pmod{24},
$$
we have
$$
8\mid n.
$$
Thus
$$
i^n=(-i)^n=1.
$$
Neither of the two eigenlines in $Z=0$ lies on $C_Q$, and the $Z$-axis is also absent from the curve. These elements have no fixed points.

For each coordinate transposition, two signed lifts satisfy $\varepsilon\delta=1$ and two satisfy $\varepsilon\delta=-1$. There are three coordinate transpositions. Hence six elements fix $n$ points each and six fix none, contributing
$$
6n.
$$

Step 5: Count fixed points above the three-cycles and derive the exact genus

Consider a signed lift of a coordinate $3$-cycle:
$$
g[X:Y:Z]=[\varepsilon Y:\delta Z:X],
\qquad
\varepsilon,\delta\in\{1,-1\}.
$$
An eigenpoint with eigenvalue $\lambda$ satisfies
$$
\varepsilon Y=\lambda X,
$$
$$
\delta Z=\lambda Y,
$$
$$
X=\lambda Z.
$$
Therefore
$$
\lambda^3=\varepsilon\delta.
$$

Because $Q\equiv17\pmod{24}$,
$$
n\equiv1\pmod3
$$
and $n$ is even.

If $\lambda^3=1$, the three values of $\lambda^n$ are $1,\omega,\omega^2$, where $\omega$ is a primitive cube root of unity. If $\lambda^3=-1$, write $\lambda=-\mu$ with $\mu^3=1$. Since $n$ is even,
$$
\lambda^n=\mu^n,
$$
so the same three values occur.

For an eigenline,
$$
Y^n=\lambda^nX^n,
\qquad
Z^n=\lambda^{2n}X^n.
$$
Exactly the two primitive cube-root values satisfy
$$
1+\lambda^n+\lambda^{2n}=0.
$$
Hence every signed lift of a $3$-cycle fixes exactly two points.

There are two coordinate $3$-cycles and four signed lifts of each, giving eight elements and total contribution
$$
16.
$$

The total fixed-point contribution from all nonidentity elements is
$$
R=3n+6n+16=9n+16.
$$
Tame Riemann-Hurwitz gives
$$
n(n-3)
=
24(2g(K_Q)-2)+9n+16.
$$
Therefore
$$
48g(K_Q)
=
n^2-12n+32.
$$
Since $n=Q-1$,
$$
48g(K_Q)
=
Q^2-14Q+45
=
(Q-7)^2-4.
$$

Step 6: Evaluate the renormalized limit

The requested expression is
$$
Q\left(\sqrt{(Q-7)^2-4}-(Q-7)\right).
$$
Rationalizing gives
$$
Q\left(\sqrt{(Q-7)^2-4}-(Q-7)\right)
=
\frac{-4Q}
{\sqrt{(Q-7)^2-4}+Q-7}.
$$
Dividing numerator and denominator by $Q$,
$$
\frac{-4}
{\sqrt{\left(1-\frac7Q\right)^2-\frac4{Q^2}}+1-\frac7Q}
\longrightarrow
\frac{-4}{2}.
$$
Hence the limit is $-2$.

Final Answer: $\boxed{-2}$

## Steps

Step 1: Convert the polynomial iteration into powers of one auxiliary variable

For every nonzero $z$,
$$
P_0(z+z^{-1})=z+z^{-1}.
$$
If
$$
P_r(z+z^{-1})=z^{2^r}+z^{-2^r},
$$
then
$$
P_{r+1}(z+z^{-1})
=
\left(z^{2^r}+z^{-2^r}\right)^2-2
=
z^{2^{r+1}}+z^{-2^{r+1}}.
$$
Induction gives
$$
P_n(z+z^{-1})=z^{2^n}+z^{-2^n}.
$$

Put
$$
N=2^n,\qquad
\lambda=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}.
$$
Then
$$
\lambda+\lambda^{-1}=3,\qquad \lambda=\phi^2.
$$
Thus $P_n(x)=3$ with $x=z+z^{-1}$ is equivalent to
$$
z^N=\lambda
\quad\text{or}\quad
z^N=\lambda^{-1}.
$$

Choose $t$ so that
$$
t^{N/2}=\phi.
$$
Then $t^N=\lambda$. If $\zeta$ is a primitive $N$th root of unity, all roots of $P_n(x)-3$ are
$$
x_j=\zeta^j t+\zeta^{-j}t^{-1},
\qquad 0\leq j<N.
$$

These roots are distinct. If $x_j=x_k$, then $\zeta^jt$ and $\zeta^kt$ are roots of the same quadratic
$$
Z^2-x_jZ+1.
$$
Hence either $\zeta^jt=\zeta^kt$, giving $j=k$, or
$$
\zeta^jt=\zeta^{-k}t^{-1}.
$$
The latter would make $t^2$ a root of unity, which is impossible because $t^N=\lambda>1$ under the chosen real embedding. Since $P_n$ has degree $N$, the displayed list is the complete root set.

Step 2: Place the splitting field inside a radical-cyclotomic field

Let
$$
M_n=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta,t).
$$
Since
$$
\sqrt5=2t^{N/2}-1,
$$
the field $M_n$ also contains $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$. Step 1 shows that every root $x_j$ belongs to $M_n$, so
$$
L_n\subseteq M_n.
$$

The element $t$ satisfies
$$
t^{2N}-3t^N+1=0.
$$
The roots of this polynomial are
$$
\zeta^jt,\qquad \zeta^jt^{-1}
\qquad(0\leq j<N).
$$
They all lie in $M_n$. Hence $M_n$ is a splitting field over $\mathbb{Q}$ and is Galois.

Any $\mathbb{Q}$-automorphism of $M_n$ sends
$$
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^a
$$
for some odd $a$ modulo $N$, and sends $t$ to either
$$
\zeta^bt
\quad\text{or}\quad
\zeta^bt^{-1}
$$
for some $b$ modulo $N$. Its action on the roots is therefore respectively
$$
x_j\mapsto x_{aj+b}
$$
or
$$
x_j\mapsto x_{-aj-b}.
$$

Suppose the automorphism fixes every $x_j$. Since the $x_j$ are distinct, the first case forces
$$
a\equiv1\pmod N,\qquad b\equiv0\pmod N,
$$
so the automorphism is the identity.

In the second case, pointwise fixation would force
$$
a\equiv-1\pmod N,\qquad b\equiv0\pmod N.
$$
It would then send $t$ to $t^{-1}$. But
$$
t^{N/2}=\phi,
$$
so it would send $\phi$ to $\phi^{-1}$. Every $\mathbb{Q}$-automorphism sends $\sqrt5$ to $\pm\sqrt5$, and hence sends
$$
\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}
$$
to either $\phi$ or
$$
\frac{1-\sqrt5}{2}=-\phi^{-1},
$$
never to $\phi^{-1}$. The second case is impossible.

Thus the subgroup of $\operatorname{Gal}(M_n/\mathbb{Q})$ fixing all roots is trivial. Since $L_n$ is generated by those roots,
$$
L_n=M_n.
$$

Step 3: Compute the cyclotomic-quadratic base degree

Set
$$
E_n=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta,\sqrt5).
$$
The field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ has degree
$$
[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}]=\varphi(2^n)=2^{n-1}.
$$
The quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$ is not contained in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$: the latter is ramified only at $2$, while $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$ is ramified at $5$. Since the second field has prime degree $2$, their intersection is $\mathbb{Q}$. Therefore
$$
[E_n:\mathbb{Q}]=2^n.
$$

Also
$$
M_n=E_n(t),
\qquad
t^{2^{n-1}}=\phi.
$$
It remains to find the degree of this radical extension.

Step 4: Show that $\phi$ cannot become a square in the abelian base

Suppose first that $\phi=y^2$ for some $y\in E_n$. Then $y$ satisfies
$$
y^4-y^2-1=0.
$$
This polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. Indeed, if
$$
X^4-X^2-1=(X^2+aX+b)(X^2-aX+d)
$$
with rational coefficients, comparison of the linear coefficient gives
$$
a(d-b)=0.
$$
If $a=0$, then $b+d=-1$ and $bd=-1$, so $b,d$ would be rational roots of
$$
T^2+T-1,
$$
which is impossible. If $b=d$, then $b^2=-1$, also impossible over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Thus $[\mathbb{Q}(y):\mathbb{Q}]=4$. Taking $y$ to be the real square root of $\phi$, the field $\mathbb{Q}(y)$ is contained in $\mathbb{R}$. The other two roots of its minimal polynomial satisfy
$$
Y^2=\frac{1-\sqrt5}{2}<0,
$$
so they are nonreal. Hence $\mathbb{Q}(y)/\mathbb{Q}$ is not normal.

On the other hand, $E_n/\mathbb{Q}$ is abelian because it is the compositum of two abelian extensions. Every intermediate field of a finite abelian Galois extension is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$. Therefore $\mathbb{Q}(y)$ cannot be contained in $E_n$. This contradiction proves
$$
\phi\notin E_n^{\times2}.
$$

Step 5: Determine the radical degree and finish

Put
$$
s=2^{n-1}.
$$
For $n=1$, $s=1$ and $M_1=E_1$, so
$$
[M_1:E_1]=1=s.
$$

Now let $n\geq2$. Since $E_n$ contains all $s$th roots of unity, the field
$$
E_n(t),\qquad t^s=\phi,
$$
is the splitting field over $E_n$ of $X^s-\phi$. Let
$$
d=[E_n(t):E_n].
$$
Its Galois group sends $t$ to $\xi t$, where $\xi$ lies in the cyclic group of $s$th roots of unity. Hence the Galois group is a subgroup of that cyclic group and has order $d$.

If $d<s$, then, because $s$ is a power of $2$, the unique subgroup of order $d$ consists of the $d$th roots of unity. Therefore every automorphism fixes $t^d$, so
$$
t^d\in E_n.
$$
But
$$
\phi=t^s=(t^d)^{s/d}.
$$
Since $d<s$, the integer $s/d$ is even. This would make $\phi$ a square in $E_n$, contradicting Step 4. Thus
$$
[M_n:E_n]=s=2^{n-1}.
$$

Combining this with Step 3 and $L_n=M_n$ from Step 2 gives
$$
[L_n:\mathbb{Q}]
=
2^n\cdot2^{n-1}
=
2^{2n-1}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{2^{2n-1}}$

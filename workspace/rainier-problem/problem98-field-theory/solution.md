## Steps

Step 1: Recover the cyclic cubic field hidden in the coefficients

Put
$$
q(y)=y^3-31y^2+290y-841,
$$
so
$$
g_n(x)=q(x^{2^n}).
$$

Consider
$$
h(t)=t^3+t^2-2t-1.
$$
Modulo $2$ this is $t^3+t^2+1$, which has no root in $\mathbb{F}_2$, so $h$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. Its discriminant is
$$
1^2(-2)^2-4(-2)^3-4(1)^3(-1)-27(-1)^2+18(1)(-2)(-1)=49.
$$
An irreducible cubic with square discriminant has Galois group $A_3$. Hence, if $\theta$ is a root,
$$
K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
$$
is cyclic Galois of degree $3$.

From
$$
\theta^3=-\theta^2+2\theta+1
$$
one obtains
$$
(\theta^2-2)^2-2=-\theta^2-\theta+1
$$
and
$$
(-\theta^2-\theta+1)^2-2=\theta.
$$
Also substituting $\theta^2-2$ into $h$ and reducing by the displayed cubic relation gives $0$. Therefore an automorphism $\sigma$ of $K$ is given by
$$
\sigma(\theta)=\theta^2-2,
$$
and $\sigma$ has order $3$.

Set
$$
u_i=\sigma^i(\theta)-3,\qquad c_i=u_i u_{i+1},
$$
with indices modulo $3$. For $i=0$,
$$
c_0=(\theta-3)(\theta^2-5)=16-3\theta-4\theta^2.
$$
Reduction with $\theta^3=-\theta^2+2\theta+1$ gives
$$
c_0^2=264-64\theta-95\theta^2
$$
and
$$
c_0^3=4385-1114\theta-1785\theta^2.
$$
Hence
$$
c_0^3-31c_0^2+290c_0-841=0.
$$
Applying $\sigma$ shows that $c_0,c_1,c_2$ are roots of $q$. Since
$$
q(y)\equiv y^3+y^2+1\pmod2
$$
has no root in $\mathbb{F}_2$, $q$ is irreducible. Thus its three roots generate exactly $K$.

Step 2: Describe the splitting field over a cyclotomic base

Let $\zeta_{2^n}$ be a primitive $2^n$th root of unity and put
$$
E_n=K(\zeta_{2^n}).
$$
The field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^n})$ has degree $2^{n-1}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $[K:\mathbb{Q}]=3$, the intersection
$$
K\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^n})
$$
has degree dividing both $3$ and $2^{n-1}$, so it is $\mathbb{Q}$. Therefore
$$
[E_n:\mathbb{Q}]=3\cdot2^{n-1}.
$$

The roots of $g_n$ are all the $2^n$th roots of $c_0,c_1,c_2$. Since $E_n$ contains every $2^n$th root of unity,
$$
L_n=E_n\left(c_0^{1/2^n},c_1^{1/2^n},c_2^{1/2^n}\right).
$$
It remains to determine the subgroup generated by $c_0,c_1,c_2$ in
$$
E_n^\times/E_n^{\times2^n}.
$$

Step 3: Extract the prime-valuation incidence pattern

Since
$$
h(3)=29,
$$
we have
$$
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(u_0)=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\theta-3)=-29.
$$
Thus the principal ideal $(u_0)$ is a prime ideal of norm $29$. Its conjugates
$$
P_i=(u_i)
$$
are the three distinct primes of $K$ above $29$.

Indeed, modulo $29$,
$$
h(t)=(t-3)(t-7)(t-18),
$$
so $29$ splits completely in $K$. Consequently
$$
v_{P_j}(u_i)=
\begin{cases}
1,&i=j,\\
0,&i\neq j.
\end{cases}
$$

Only the prime $2$ ramifies in the cyclotomic extension $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^n})/\mathbb{Q}$. Hence $E_n/K$ is unramified at every $P_i$. Choose a prime $Q_i$ of $E_n$ above $P_i$. Then
$$
v_{Q_j}(u_i)=v_{P_j}(u_i).
$$
Because $c_i=u_i u_{i+1}$, for integers $r_0,r_1,r_2$ we have
$$
v_{Q_0}(c_0^{r_0}c_1^{r_1}c_2^{r_2})=r_0+r_2,
$$
$$
v_{Q_1}(c_0^{r_0}c_1^{r_1}c_2^{r_2})=r_0+r_1,
$$
and
$$
v_{Q_2}(c_0^{r_0}c_1^{r_1}c_2^{r_2})=r_1+r_2.
$$

Step 4: Determine the exact Kummer relation kernel

Suppose
$$
c_0^{r_0}c_1^{r_1}c_2^{r_2}\in E_n^{\times2^n}.
$$
Every valuation of this element must be divisible by $2^n$. Step 3 gives
$$
r_0+r_2\equiv0\pmod{2^n},
$$
$$
r_0+r_1\equiv0\pmod{2^n},
$$
$$
r_1+r_2\equiv0\pmod{2^n}.
$$
Subtracting the first two congruences gives $r_2\equiv r_1$, while the second gives $r_1\equiv-r_0$. Hence
$$
2r_0\equiv0\pmod{2^n}.
$$
Modulo $2^n$, the only possibilities are
$$
(r_0,r_1,r_2)=(0,0,0)
$$
and
$$
(r_0,r_1,r_2)=(2^{n-1},2^{n-1},2^{n-1}).
$$

The second vector really is a relation. Since
$$
u_0u_1u_2=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\theta-3)=-29,
$$
we have
$$
c_0c_1c_2=(u_0u_1u_2)^2=29^2.
$$
Therefore
$$
(c_0c_1c_2)^{2^{n-1}}=29^{2^n},
$$
which is a $2^n$th power in $E_n$.

Thus the kernel of the map
$$
(\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^3\longrightarrow E_n^\times/E_n^{\times2^n},
\qquad
(r_0,r_1,r_2)\longmapsto[c_0^{r_0}c_1^{r_1}c_2^{r_2}]
$$
has exactly $2$ elements. The subgroup generated by the three classes therefore has order
$$
\frac{2^{3n}}{2}=2^{3n-1}.
$$

Step 5: Compute the splitting-field degree

Because $E_n$ contains all $2^n$th roots of unity, Kummer theory identifies the degree obtained by adjoining the $2^n$th roots of $c_0,c_1,c_2$ with the order of the subgroup generated by their classes in $E_n^\times/E_n^{\times2^n}$. Step 4 gives
$$
[L_n:E_n]=2^{3n-1}.
$$
Combining this with Step 2,
$$
[L_n:\mathbb{Q}]
=
2^{3n-1}\cdot3\cdot2^{n-1}
=
3\cdot2^{4n-2}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{3\cdot2^{4n-2}}$

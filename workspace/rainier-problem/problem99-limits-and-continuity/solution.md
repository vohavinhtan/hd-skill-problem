## Steps

Step 1: Convert the nonlinear equations into two second-order dilation recurrences

Put
$$
g(t)=f(e^t),\qquad t>0,
$$
and define
$$
A(t)=g(t)+\frac{t}{2}g(t)^2.
$$
Then $g$ and $A$ are continuous. The first given identity is
$$
A(4t)-6A(2t)+8A(t)=24t^3,
$$
while the second is
$$
A(9t)-12A(3t)+27A(t)=432t^3.
$$

Since
$$
(4t)^3-6(2t)^3+8t^3=24t^3
$$
and
$$
(9t)^3-12(3t)^3+27t^3=432t^3,
$$
the function
$$
H(t)=A(t)-t^3
$$
satisfies
$$
H(4t)-6H(2t)+8H(t)=0,
$$
$$
H(9t)-12H(3t)+27H(t)=0.
$$

Step 2: Extract the quadratic component of the homogeneous solution

Define
$$
U(t)=H(2t)-2H(t).
$$
The first homogeneous relation from Step 1 gives
$$
U(2t)=4U(t).
$$
Applying the operation $H(t)\mapsto H(2t)-2H(t)$ to the second homogeneous relation gives
$$
U(9t)-12U(3t)+27U(t)=0.
$$

Set
$$
u(t)=\frac{U(t)}{t^2}.
$$
Then
$$
u(2t)=u(t)
$$
and
$$
3u(9t)-4u(3t)+u(t)=0.
$$
Define
$$
v(t)=u(3t)-u(t).
$$
The last relation is equivalent to
$$
v(3t)=\frac{1}{3}v(t).
$$

Because $u(2t)=u(t)$, every value of $u$ equals a value on $[1,2]$. Continuity makes $u$ bounded there, so $u$ and $v$ are bounded on $(0,\infty)$. Iterating the relation for $v$ backwards gives
$$
v\left(\frac{t}{3^k}\right)=3^kv(t).
$$
Boundedness forces $v(t)=0$. Therefore
$$
u(3t)=u(t).
$$

The ratio $\log2/\log3$ is irrational, since otherwise $2^r=3^s$ for some positive integers $r,s$. The multiplicative subgroup generated by $2$ and $3$ is consequently dense in $(0,\infty)$. Continuity together with
$$
u(2t)=u(3t)=u(t)
$$
forces $u$ to be constant. Write
$$
u(t)=2b.
$$
Then
$$
H(2t)-2H(t)=2bt^2.
$$

Step 3: Extract the remaining linear component

Define
$$
V(t)=H(t)-bt^2.
$$
The last relation from Step 2 gives
$$
V(2t)=2V(t).
$$
Since $t^2$ satisfies the second homogeneous relation from Step 1, $V$ also satisfies
$$
V(9t)-12V(3t)+27V(t)=0.
$$

Put
$$
w(t)=\frac{V(t)}{t}.
$$
Then
$$
w(2t)=w(t)
$$
and
$$
w(9t)-4w(3t)+3w(t)=0.
$$
For
$$
z(t)=w(3t)-w(t),
$$
this becomes
$$
z(3t)=3z(t).
$$
The relation $w(2t)=w(t)$ and continuity make $w$, and therefore $z$, bounded on $(0,\infty)$. Iterating forward gives
$$
z(3^kt)=3^kz(t),
$$
so boundedness forces $z(t)=0$.

Therefore $w(3t)=w(t)$. The same density argument used in Step 2 shows that $w$ is constant. Write
$$
w(t)=a.
$$
Consequently,
$$
H(t)=at+bt^2,
$$
and
$$
A(t)=t^3+at+bt^2.
$$

Step 4: Determine the constants and recover the correct algebraic branch

From $f(e)=0$,
$$
g(1)=0,
$$
so
$$
A(1)=0.
$$
Step 3 gives
$$
a+b=-1.
$$

From $f(e^2)=1$,
$$
g(2)=1,
$$
and therefore
$$
A(2)=1+\frac{2}{2}=2.
$$
Hence
$$
8+2a+4b=2.
$$
Solving the two equations gives
$$
a=1,\qquad b=-2.
$$
Thus
$$
A(t)=t^3-2t^2+t.
$$

By the definition of $A$,
$$
\frac{t}{2}g(t)^2+g(t)=t^3-2t^2+t.
$$
The two algebraic roots are
$$
g(t)=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{1+2t^2(t-1)^2}}{t}.
$$
Their discriminant satisfies
$$
1+2t^2(t-1)^2>0
$$
for every $t>0$, so the branches never meet. At $t=1$, their values are $0$ and $-2$. Continuity and $g(1)=0$ select
$$
g(t)=\frac{\sqrt{1+2t^2(t-1)^2}-1}{t}.
$$

Step 5: Compute the sixth-order endpoint expansion

Set
$$
z=2t^2(t-1)^2=2t^2-4t^3+2t^4.
$$
Then
$$
z^2=4t^4-16t^5+24t^6-16t^7+O(t^8),
$$
$$
z^3=8t^6-48t^7+O(t^8).
$$
Using
$$
\sqrt{1+z}=1+\frac{z}{2}-\frac{z^2}{8}+\frac{z^3}{16}+O(t^8),
$$
Step 4 gives
$$
g(t)
=
t-2t^2+\frac{1}{2}t^3+2t^4-\frac{5}{2}t^5-t^6+O(t^7).
$$

Put
$$
s=x-1.
$$
Since
$$
t=\log(1+s)
=
s-\frac{s^2}{2}+\frac{s^3}{3}-\frac{s^4}{4}
+\frac{s^5}{5}-\frac{s^6}{6}+O(s^7),
$$
the required powers are
$$
t^2=s^2-s^3+\frac{11}{12}s^4-\frac{5}{6}s^5+\frac{137}{180}s^6+O(s^7),
$$
$$
t^3=s^3-\frac{3}{2}s^4+\frac{7}{4}s^5-\frac{15}{8}s^6+O(s^7),
$$
$$
t^4=s^4-2s^5+\frac{17}{6}s^6+O(s^7),
$$
$$
t^5=s^5-\frac{5}{2}s^6+O(s^7),
$$
$$
t^6=s^6+O(s^7).
$$
Substitution yields
$$
f(1+s)
=
s-\frac{5}{2}s^2+\frac{17}{6}s^3-\frac{5}{6}s^4
-\frac{451}{120}s^5+\frac{5969}{720}s^6+O(s^7).
$$
The terms of degrees one through five cancel with the prescribed numerator.

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{5969}{720}}$

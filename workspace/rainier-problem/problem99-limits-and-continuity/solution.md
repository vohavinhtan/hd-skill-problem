## Steps

Step 1: Recover the moving rational coordinate hidden in the two identities

Write
$$
g(t)=f(e^t),\qquad t>0.
$$
Because $g(t)>-1$,
$$
1+t^4+tg(t)>1+t^4-t>0.
$$
For $0<t<1$, the right side is $t^4+(1-t)>0$, while for $t\geq1$ it is at least $1$. The corresponding denominators with $2t$ and $3t$ are positive by the same argument.

Define
$$
u(t)=\frac{(1+t^4)(g(t)-t^2)}{1+t^4+tg(t)}.
$$
Dividing the first given identity by
$$
(1+t^4+tg(t))(1+16t^4+2tg(2t))
$$
gives
$$
u(2t)=2u(t)+t^3.
$$
Likewise, division of the second identity gives
$$
u(3t)=3u(t)+4t^3.
$$
Since $g(1)=f(e)=0$,
$$
u(1)=\frac{2(0-1)}{2}=-1.
$$

Step 2: Remove the inhomogeneous scaling term

Set
$$
v(t)=u(t)-\frac{t^3}{6}.
$$
The two relations from Step 1 give
$$
v(2t)
=
2u(t)+t^3-\frac{8t^3}{6}
=
2v(t),
$$
and
$$
v(3t)
=
3u(t)+4t^3-\frac{27t^3}{6}
=
3v(t).
$$
Therefore
$$
w(t)=\frac{v(t)}{t}
$$
satisfies
$$
w(2t)=w(t),\qquad w(3t)=w(t).
$$

Step 3: Use the two incommensurable scalings to force rigidity

Define
$$
W(s)=w(e^s),\qquad s\in\mathbb R.
$$
Then $W$ is continuous and
$$
W(s+\log2)=W(s),\qquad W(s+\log3)=W(s).
$$
The ratio $\log2/\log3$ is irrational, since a relation
$$
r\log2=s\log3
$$
with positive integers $r,s$ would imply $2^r=3^s$.

For any positive integer $M$, two of the $M+1$ fractional parts
$$
0,\frac{\log2}{\log3},\ldots,\frac{M\log2}{\log3}
$$
differ by less than $1/M$. Hence the additive subgroup generated by $\log2$ and $\log3$ contains nonzero periods of arbitrarily small absolute value.

If $d\in\mathbb R$ and $\eta>0$ is such a period, choose an integer $k$ with
$$
|d-k\eta|\leq\frac{\eta}{2}.
$$
Taking periods $\eta\to0$ shows that the period subgroup is dense in $\mathbb R$. Continuity then forces $W$ to be constant.

From Step 1,
$$
v(1)=u(1)-\frac16=-\frac76.
$$
Thus
$$
w(t)=-\frac76
$$
for all $t>0$, and
$$
u(t)=\frac{t^3-7t}{6}.
$$

Step 4: Recover the function explicitly

The definition of $u$ gives
$$
u(t)(1+t^4+tg(t))=(1+t^4)(g(t)-t^2).
$$
Collecting the terms containing $g(t)$ yields
$$
g(t)\left(1+t^4-tu(t)\right)=(1+t^4)(t^2+u(t)).
$$
Using the formula from Step 3,
$$
1+t^4-tu(t)
=
1+t^4-\frac{t^4-7t^2}{6}
=
\frac{6+7t^2+5t^4}{6},
$$
and
$$
t^2+u(t)=\frac{t^3+6t^2-7t}{6}.
$$
Therefore
$$
g(t)=
\frac{(1+t^4)(t^3+6t^2-7t)}
{6+7t^2+5t^4}.
$$
Since $t=\log x$,
$$
f(x)=
\frac{(1+(\log x)^4)((\log x)^3+6(\log x)^2-7\log x)}
{6+7(\log x)^2+5(\log x)^4}.
$$

Step 5: Compute the required fifth-order coefficient

Expanding the rational expression from Step 4 at $t=0$ gives
$$
g(t)
=
-\frac76t+t^2+\frac{55}{36}t^3-\frac76t^4-\frac{427}{216}t^5+O(t^6).
$$
Put
$$
s=x-1.
$$
Then
$$
t=\log(1+s)
=
s-\frac{s^2}{2}+\frac{s^3}{3}-\frac{s^4}{4}+\frac{s^5}{5}+O(s^6),
$$
and the needed powers are
$$
t^2=s^2-s^3+\frac{11}{12}s^4-\frac56s^5+O(s^6),
$$
$$
t^3=s^3-\frac32s^4+\frac74s^5+O(s^6),
$$
$$
t^4=s^4-2s^5+O(s^6),
$$
$$
t^5=s^5+O(s^6).
$$
Substitution gives
$$
f(1+s)
=
-\frac76s+\frac{19}{12}s^2+\frac{5}{36}s^3-\frac94s^4+\frac{4241}{2160}s^5+O(s^6).
$$
The first four displayed terms cancel with the prescribed numerator.

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{4241}{2160}}$

## Steps

Step 1: Convert the dilation identities into two shift equations

Put
$$
g(t)=f(e^t),\qquad t>0,
$$
and define
$$
A(t)=g(t)+2g(t)^2+3g(t)^3.
$$
Let
$$
H(s)=e^{-2s}A(e^s),\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}.
$$
Write
$$
a=\log2,\qquad b=\log3.
$$

The first functional identity becomes
$$
H(s+3a)-4H(s+2a)+5H(s+a)-2H(s)=0.
$$
Its shift polynomial factors as
$$
z^3-4z^2+5z-2=(z-1)^2(z-2).
$$
The second identity becomes
$$
H(s+3b)-5H(s+2b)+7H(s+b)-3H(s)=0,
$$
whose shift polynomial is
$$
z^3-5z^2+7z-3=(z-1)^2(z-3).
$$

Step 2: Isolate the common exponential mode

Let
$$
F(s)=H(s+2a)-2H(s+a)+H(s).
$$
The first equation from Step 1 gives
$$
F(s+a)=2F(s).
$$
Set
$$
G(s)=e^{-s}F(s).
$$
Then
$$
G(s+a)=G(s),
$$
so $G$ is bounded on $\mathbb{R}$.

The second shift operator commutes with the second $a$-difference, so $F$ also satisfies
$$
F(s+3b)-5F(s+2b)+7F(s+b)-3F(s)=0.
$$
Since $F(s)=e^sG(s)$ and $e^b=3$, division by $e^s$ gives
$$
27G(s+3b)-45G(s+2b)+21G(s+b)-3G(s)=0.
$$
For fixed $s$, the sequence
$$
q_k=G(s+kb)
$$
has characteristic polynomial
$$
27z^3-45z^2+21z-3=27(z-1)\left(z-\frac13\right)^2.
$$
Therefore
$$
q_k=c+(d+ek)3^{-k}.
$$
The sequence is bounded for all integers $k$, because $G$ is bounded. Letting $k\to-\infty$ forces
$$
d=e=0.
$$
So
$$
G(s+b)=G(s).
$$

The ratio $a/b$ is irrational, since a rational relation would give $2^r=3^q$ for positive integers $r,q$. The additive subgroup generated by $a$ and $b$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$. Continuity and the two periods force $G$ to be constant. Write
$$
G(s)=\gamma.
$$
Then
$$
F(s)=\gamma e^s.
$$

Step 3: Recover the affine part after removing the exponential mode

Since
$$
e^{s+2a}-2e^{s+a}+e^s=e^s,
$$
the function
$$
J(s)=H(s)-\gamma e^s
$$
satisfies
$$
J(s+2a)-2J(s+a)+J(s)=0.
$$
It also satisfies the second shift equation from Step 1 because $e^s$ is annihilated by its factor $z-3$.

Put
$$
R(s)=J(s+a)-J(s).
$$
Then $R$ is $a$-periodic and bounded. It also satisfies
$$
R(s+3b)-5R(s+2b)+7R(s+b)-3R(s)=0.
$$
For fixed $s$, the sequence $R(s+kb)$ has characteristic roots $1,1,3$. Its general form is
$$
c+dk+e3^k.
$$
Boundedness for all integers $k$ forces
$$
d=e=0.
$$
Therefore $R$ is also $b$-periodic. Density and continuity make it constant. Write
$$
R(s)=\beta a.
$$

Now set
$$
Q(s)=J(s)-\beta s.
$$
Then $Q$ is $a$-periodic. A linear function is annihilated by the factor $(z-1)^2$, so $Q$ still satisfies the second shift equation. The same bounded-sequence argument shows that $Q$ is $b$-periodic. Density gives
$$
Q(s)=\alpha.
$$
Consequently,
$$
H(s)=\alpha+\beta s+\gamma e^s.
$$

Step 4: Use the three normalization conditions

At $t=1$, the condition $f(e)=0$ gives
$$
A(1)=0.
$$
Therefore
$$
H(0)=0,
$$
so
$$
\alpha+\gamma=0.
$$

At $t=2$,
$$
A(2)=4\log2,
$$
hence
$$
H(a)=a.
$$
This gives
$$
\alpha+\beta a+2\gamma=a.
$$

At $t=3$,
$$
A(3)=9\log3,
$$
so
$$
H(b)=b,
$$
and
$$
\alpha+\beta b+3\gamma=b.
$$

Using $\alpha=-\gamma$, the last two equations reduce to
$$
(\beta-1)a+\gamma=0,
$$
$$
(\beta-1)b+2\gamma=0.
$$
Eliminating $\gamma$ gives
$$
(\beta-1)(b-2a)=0.
$$
Since $3\neq4$, we have $b\neq2a$. Therefore
$$
\beta=1,\qquad \gamma=0,\qquad \alpha=0.
$$
So
$$
H(s)=s
$$
and
$$
A(t)=t^2\log t.
$$

Step 5: Invert the nonlinear coordinate and evaluate the limit

Put
$$
R=t^2\log t.
$$
The identity from Step 4 is
$$
g+2g^2+3g^3=R.
$$
The polynomial
$$
P(y)=y+2y^2+3y^3
$$
has
$$
P'(y)=1+4y+9y^2>0,
$$
so the real solution $g$ is unique.

As $R\to0$, write
$$
g=R+c_2R^2+c_3R^3+c_4R^4+O(R^5).
$$
Then
$$
g^2=R^2+2c_2R^3+(c_2^2+2c_3)R^4+O(R^5),
$$
and
$$
g^3=R^3+3c_2R^4+O(R^5).
$$
Substitution into $g+2g^2+3g^3=R$ gives successively
$$
c_2=-2,
$$
$$
c_3+4c_2+3=0,
$$
$$
c_4+2(c_2^2+2c_3)+9c_2=0.
$$
Therefore
$$
c_3=5,\qquad c_4=-10,
$$
and
$$
f(e^t)=R-2R^2+5R^3-10R^4+O(R^5).
$$

Let
$$
s=x-1.
$$
Then
$$
\frac{t}{s}=\frac{\log(1+s)}{s}\to1
$$
and
$$
\log t-\log s=\log\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)\to0.
$$
Since $\log s\to-\infty$,
$$
\frac{\log t}{\log s}\to1.
$$
It follows that
$$
\frac{R^4}{s^8(\log s)^4}
=
\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^8
\left(\frac{\log t}{\log s}\right)^4
\to1.
$$
Also,
$$
\frac{R^5}{s^8(\log s)^4}
=
\frac{R^4}{s^8(\log s)^4}R\to0.
$$
The numerator in the required limit is
$$
-10R^4+O(R^5).
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{-10}$

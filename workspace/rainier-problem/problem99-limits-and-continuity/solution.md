## Steps

Step 1: Convert the two equations into repeated dilation differences

Put
$$
g(t)=f(e^t),\qquad t>0,
$$
and define
$$
A(t)=g(t)+2g(t)^2+3g(t)^3.
$$
Then $A$ is continuous. The two given identities become
$$
A(4t)-8A(2t)+16A(t)=32(\log2)^2t^2
$$
and
$$
A(9t)-18A(3t)+81A(t)=162(\log3)^2t^2.
$$

Since $t>0$, define
$$
B(t)=\frac{A(t)}{t^2}.
$$
Dividing the first relation by $16t^2$ and the second by $81t^2$ gives
$$
B(4t)-2B(2t)+B(t)=2(\log2)^2,
$$
$$
B(9t)-2B(3t)+B(t)=2(\log3)^2.
$$

Now put
$$
C(s)=B(e^s)-s^2,\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}.
$$
With
$$
a=\log2,\qquad b=\log3,
$$
the identity
$$
(s+2a)^2-2(s+a)^2+s^2=2a^2
$$
and its analogue for $b$ reduce the two equations to
$$
C(s+2a)-2C(s+a)+C(s)=0,
$$
$$
C(s+2b)-2C(s+b)+C(s)=0.
$$

Step 2: Show that the two repeated differences force an affine function

For any $h>0$, write
$$
\Delta_hC(s)=C(s+h)-C(s).
$$
The relations from Step 1 say
$$
\Delta_aC(s+a)=\Delta_aC(s),
$$
$$
\Delta_bC(s+b)=\Delta_bC(s).
$$
Thus $\Delta_aC$ is $a$-periodic and $\Delta_bC$ is $b$-periodic.

Consider
$$
P(s)=\Delta_b\Delta_aC(s).
$$
Because the difference operators commute,
$$
P(s)=\Delta_a\Delta_bC(s).
$$
The first form shows that $P$ is $a$-periodic, while the second shows that it is $b$-periodic.

The ratio $a/b=\log2/\log3$ is irrational. Otherwise positive integers $r,q$ would satisfy
$$
r\log2=q\log3,
$$
which would imply $2^r=3^q$. The additive subgroup generated by $a$ and $b$ is therefore dense in $\mathbb{R}$, so continuity forces $P$ to be constant.

Write $P(s)=c$. Since
$$
P(s)=\Delta_b(\Delta_aC)(s),
$$
iteration gives
$$
\Delta_aC(s+mb)=\Delta_aC(s)+mc.
$$
The function $\Delta_aC$ is continuous and $a$-periodic, hence bounded on $\mathbb{R}$. Therefore $c=0$.

It follows that $\Delta_aC$ has both periods $a$ and $b$, so it is constant. Write
$$
\Delta_aC(s)=\alpha.
$$
Likewise,
$$
\Delta_bC(s)=\beta
$$
for some constant $\beta$.

Step 3: Determine the remaining affine freedom

Set
$$
L(s)=C(s)-\frac{\alpha}{a}s.
$$
Then
$$
L(s+a)=L(s).
$$
Also,
$$
L(s+b)-L(s)=\beta-\frac{\alpha b}{a}.
$$
If the right-hand side were nonzero, iteration in the $b$ direction would make $L$ unbounded. This is impossible because the continuous $a$-periodic function $L$ is bounded. Hence
$$
\beta=\frac{\alpha b}{a},
$$
so $L$ has both periods $a$ and $b$. Density now forces $L$ to be constant.

Thus
$$
C(s)=cs+d
$$
for constants $c,d$, and Step 1 gives
$$
B(t)=(\log t)^2+c\log t+d.
$$

Since $f(e)=0$, we have $g(1)=0$, hence $A(1)=0$ and
$$
B(1)=0.
$$
Therefore $d=0$.

Since $f(e^e)=0$, we have $g(e)=0$, hence $A(e)=0$ and
$$
B(e)=0.
$$
Because $\log e=1$,
$$
1+c=0.
$$
Thus
$$
B(t)=(\log t)^2-\log t
$$
and
$$
A(t)=t^2\left((\log t)^2-\log t\right).
$$

Step 4: Invert the nonlinear coordinate near the endpoint

Put
$$
R(t)=t^2\left((\log t)^2-\log t\right).
$$
From Step 3,
$$
g(t)+2g(t)^2+3g(t)^3=R(t).
$$
The polynomial
$$
\phi(y)=y+2y^2+3y^3
$$
is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$ because
$$
\phi'(y)=1+4y+9y^2>0.
$$
Hence the equation determines $g(t)$ uniquely.

As $t\to0^+$,
$$
R(t)\to0.
$$
The defining equation first gives
$$
g(t)=R(t)+O(R(t)^2).
$$
Substituting this estimate back gives
$$
g(t)=R(t)-2R(t)^2+O(R(t)^3).
$$
Therefore
$$
g(t)^2=R(t)^2-4R(t)^3+O(R(t)^4),
$$
$$
g(t)^3=R(t)^3+O(R(t)^4).
$$
Using
$$
g=R-2g^2-3g^3
$$
now yields
$$
g(t)=R(t)-2R(t)^2+5R(t)^3+O(R(t)^4).
$$

Step 5: Evaluate the normalized endpoint limit

Let
$$
s=x-1,
$$
so
$$
t=\log(1+s).
$$
From Step 4, the numerator in the required limit is
$$
5R(t)^3+O(R(t)^4).
$$
Since
$$
R(t)^3=t^6\left((\log t)^2-\log t\right)^3,
$$
we compare this with $s^6(\log s)^6$.

As $s\to0^+$,
$$
\frac{t}{s}=\frac{\log(1+s)}{s}\to1.
$$
Also,
$$
\log t-\log s=\log\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)\to0,
$$
while $\log s\to-\infty$. Hence
$$
\frac{\log t}{\log s}\to1.
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{R(t)^3}{s^6(\log s)^6}
=
\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^6
\left(\frac{\log t}{\log s}\right)^6
\left(1-\frac{1}{\log t}\right)^3
\to1.
$$

Finally,
$$
\frac{R(t)^4}{s^6(\log s)^6}
=
\frac{R(t)^3}{s^6(\log s)^6}R(t)\to0.
$$
Thus the error term from Step 4 disappears after normalization.

Final Answer: $\boxed{5}$

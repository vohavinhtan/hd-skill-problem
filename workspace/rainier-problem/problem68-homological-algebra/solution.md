## Steps

Step 1: Compute the Poincaré series of the residue field

For
$$
A_r=\Bbbk[z_1,\ldots,z_r]/(z_1^2,\ldots,z_r^2),
$$
the tensor product of the rank-one minimal resolutions of the factors $\Bbbk[z_i]/(z_i^2)$ gives
$$
P_{\Bbbk}^{A_r}(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^r}.
$$

Before identifying the three top monomials, form
$$
T=A_a\times_{\Bbbk}A_b\times_{\Bbbk}A_c.
$$
A minimal resolution over $T$ is indexed by reduced words whose nonempty blocks come from different branches. If $P_a,P_b,P_c$ are the branch Poincaré series, the reduced-word decomposition gives
$$
\frac{1}{P_{\Bbbk}^{T}(t)}=\frac{1}{P_a(t)}+\frac{1}{P_b(t)}+\frac{1}{P_c(t)}-2=\Delta(t)-2.
$$

The kernel of $T\to R$ is generated by the two independent socle classes
$$
X-U,\qquad U-V,
$$
where
$$
X=\prod_{i=1}^{a}x_i,\qquad U=\prod_{j=1}^{b}u_j,\qquad V=\prod_{k=1}^{c}v_k.
$$
Each lies in the square of the maximal ideal. If $\widetilde S=S/(z)$ with $z$ such a socle element, the minimal resolution of $\Bbbk$ over $\widetilde S$ is obtained by inserting a degree-$2$ separator between copies of the old $S$-resolution. Therefore
$$
P_{\Bbbk}^{\widetilde S}(t)=P_{\Bbbk}^{S}(t)+t^2P_{\Bbbk}^{S}(t)P_{\Bbbk}^{\widetilde S}(t),
$$
which is equivalent to
$$
\frac{1}{P_{\Bbbk}^{\widetilde S}(t)}=\frac{1}{P_{\Bbbk}^{S}(t)}-t^2.
$$
Applying this to the two independent socle differences gives
$$
P_{\Bbbk}^{R}(t)=\frac{1}{D(t)},\qquad D(t)=\Delta(t)-2-2t^2.
$$

Step 2: Identify the cyclic relation module

Let
$$
\omega=X=U=V
$$
in $R$, and put $N=Rg$. The four coordinates of $g$ satisfy
$$
x_1g=(\omega,0,0,0)^T,\qquad x_2g=(0,\omega,0,0)^T,
$$
$$
x_3g=(0,0,\omega,0)^T,\qquad u_1g=(0,0,0,\omega)^T.
$$
Every other degree-one variable annihilates $g$, and every element of $\mathfrak m^2$ annihilates $g$. Since the four displayed vectors are linearly independent,
$$
\operatorname{Ann}_R(g)=\mathfrak m^2+(x_4,\ldots,x_a,u_2,\ldots,u_b,v_1,\ldots,v_c).
$$
This gives
$$
N\cong R/\operatorname{Ann}_R(g)
$$
with basis
$$
1,\ x_1,\ x_2,\ x_3,\ u_1.
$$
Its radical is four-dimensional and is annihilated by $\mathfrak m$, so there is an exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow\Bbbk^4\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow\Bbbk\longrightarrow0.
$$

Step 3: Compute the Yoneda quotient selected by the extension

Let
$$
E=\operatorname{Ext}_R^*(\Bbbk,\Bbbk),\qquad H_E(t)=\frac{1}{D(t)}.
$$
The extension in Step 2 determines
$$
W=\operatorname{span}_{\Bbbk}\{\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3,\eta_1\}\subseteq E_1,
$$
where $\xi_i$ corresponds to $x_i$ and $\eta_j$ corresponds to $u_j$.

For the $x$-branch, a basis in homological degree $n$ is indexed by tuples
$$
(n_1,\ldots,n_a)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{a},\qquad n_1+\cdots+n_a=n.
$$
Right multiplication by $\xi_i$ increments $n_i$. Quotienting by $\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3$ leaves the tuples with
$$
n_1=n_2=n_3=0,
$$
so the branch Hilbert-series ratio is $(1-t)^3$. The same argument for the $u$-branch shows that quotienting by $\eta_1$ contributes the ratio $1-t$.

Set
$$
Q=E/WE.
$$
In the reduced-word model for $E$, only the first branch block is changed by quotienting on the right by $W$. If $H_i$ is the Hilbert series of a branch algebra and $R_i$ is the series of nonempty reduced words beginning in that branch, then
$$
R_i=(H_i-1)(H_E-R_i).
$$
Solving gives
$$
H_E-R_i=\frac{H_E}{H_i}.
$$
Replacing the initial $x$-block by its quotient multiplies that contribution by $(1-t)^3$, while replacing the initial $u$-block multiplies its contribution by $1-t$. Since the unchanged contribution is counted once in $H_E$, this gives
$$
H_Q(t)=H_E(t)\bigl((1-t)^3+(1-t)-1\bigr)=\frac{1-4t+3t^2-t^3}{D(t)}.
$$

Step 4: Recover the Poincaré series of the cyclic module

Write
$$
e_n=\dim_{\Bbbk}E_n,\qquad q_n=\dim_{\Bbbk}Q_n.
$$
Applying $\operatorname{Ext}_R^*(-,\Bbbk)$ to the exact sequence from Step 2 gives connecting maps
$$
\delta_n:E_n^4\longrightarrow E_{n+1}.
$$
Yoneda multiplication identifies
$$
\operatorname{im}\delta_n=(WE)_{n+1},
$$
so
$$
\operatorname{rank}\delta_n=e_{n+1}-q_{n+1}.
$$
For $n\geq1$, exactness gives
$$
\beta_n^R(N)=e_n-\operatorname{rank}\delta_{n-1}+4e_n-\operatorname{rank}\delta_n.
$$
Substitution yields
$$
\beta_n^R(N)=q_n+4e_n-e_{n+1}+q_{n+1}.
$$
Because $\beta_0^R(N)=1$, summing over $n$ gives
$$
P_N^R(t)=4H_E(t)+H_Q(t)+\frac{H_Q(t)-H_E(t)}{t}.
$$
Using the formulas from Steps 1 and 3,
$$
P_N^R(t)=\frac{1-t+2t^2-t^3}{D(t)}.
$$

Step 5: Pass from the relation module to the presented module

The defining exact sequence is
$$
0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow R^4\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow0.
$$
Every coordinate of $g$ lies in $\mathfrak m$, so the map $N\to R^4$ is minimal. Therefore
$$
\beta_0^R(M)=4,\qquad \beta_n^R(M)=\beta_{n-1}^R(N)\quad(n\geq1).
$$
It follows that
$$
P_M^R(t)=4+tP_N^R(t).
$$
Substituting the expression from Step 4 and $D(t)=\Delta(t)-2-2t^2$ gives
$$
P_M^R(t)=4+\frac{t(1-t+2t^2-t^3)}{\Delta(t)-2-2t^2}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{4+\frac{t(1-t+2t^2-t^3)}{\Delta(t)-2-2t^2}}$

## Answer

## Steps

Step 1: Identify the Kummer space generated by the four defining elements
Let
$$
W=\left\langle[\pi],[\varepsilon_a],[\varepsilon_b],[\varepsilon_c]\right\rangle_{\mathbb F_p}
\subset K^\times/K^{\times p}.
$$
These four classes are independent. Indeed, suppose
$$
\pi^{x_0}\varepsilon_a^{x_a}\varepsilon_b^{x_b}\varepsilon_c^{x_c}=y^p
$$
with $0\leq x_0,x_a,x_b,x_c<p$. Taking valuations modulo $p$ gives $x_0=0$. If $y$ is a unit and $y^p\equiv1\pmod\pi$, then the residue of $y$ is $1$, so $y=1+z$ with $v_K(z)\geq1$. Since $v_K(p)=p-1$,
$$
(1+z)^p-1=pz+\sum_{k=2}^{p-1}\binom pkz^k+z^p\in\pi^p\mathcal O_K.
$$
Thus a $p$-th power unit cannot have first nonzero unit level below $p$. Because $a<b<c<p$, comparison of the first nonzero levels successively gives $x_a=x_b=x_c=0$.

The exponent matrix of $u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4$ relative to this basis is
$$
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&1\\
1&2&3&4\\
1&3&6&10\\
1&4&10&20
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Elementary row operations give
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&1\\
0&1&2&3\\
0&1&3&6\\
0&1&4&10
\end{pmatrix}
\longrightarrow
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&1\\
0&1&2&3\\
0&0&1&3\\
0&0&2&7
\end{pmatrix}
\longrightarrow
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&1\\
0&1&2&3\\
0&0&1&3\\
0&0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},
$$
so $\det M=1$. Therefore the $u_i$ span $W$, and the Kummer pairing gives
$$
L=K\left(\sqrt[p]{\pi},\sqrt[p]{\varepsilon_a},
\sqrt[p]{\varepsilon_b},\sqrt[p]{\varepsilon_c}\right),
\qquad
G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\cong(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^4.
$$

Step 2: Compute the ramification break attached to each Kummer level
First consider a Kummer line represented by $w$ with $v_K(w)$ not divisible by $p$. Choose integers $d,k$ with $d\,v_K(w)=1+kp$ and replace $w$ by $w^d/(\pi^k)^p$; this does not change the Kummer line and gives $v_K(w)=1$. If $\alpha^p=w$, then $\alpha$ is a uniformizer of the degree-$p$ extension $E=K(\alpha)$. For a generator $\sigma$ with $\sigma(\alpha)=\zeta_p\alpha$,
$$
v_E(\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha)
=v_E(\pi\alpha)=p+1,
$$
because $v_E(\pi)=p$. Thus this cyclic extension has break $p$.

Now let a Kummer line be represented by $w=1+t$ with $v_K(t)=n$, where $1\leq n<p$, and put $\alpha^p=w$, $\beta=\alpha-1$. The residue of $\alpha$ is $1$, so $s=v_E(\beta)>0$. Normalize $v_E$ by $v_E(\pi)=p$. From
$$
t=p\beta+\sum_{k=2}^{p-1}\binom pk\beta^k+\beta^p
$$
and
$$
v_E\left(\binom pk\right)=p(p-1)\qquad(1\leq k<p),
$$
we have $v_E(t)=np<p^2$. If $s\geq p$, every term on the right has valuation at least $p^2$, a contradiction. Thus $s<p$, and for $1\leq k<p$,
$$
p(p-1)+ks-ps\geq(p-1)(p-s)>0.
$$
Hence $\beta^p$ is the unique term of least valuation, so $ps=np$ and $s=n$.

Choose integers $A,B$ with $An+Bp=1$. Then $p\nmid A$ and
$$
\varpi=\beta^A\pi^B
$$
has valuation $1$, hence is a uniformizer of $E$. Since
$$
\frac{\sigma(\beta)}{\beta}
=1+\frac{\pi\alpha}{\beta}
$$
and $v_E(\pi\alpha/\beta)=p-n$, the first term of the binomial expansion of
$$
\left(\frac{\sigma(\beta)}{\beta}\right)^A-1
$$
has valuation $p-n$ and unit coefficient because $p\nmid A$. Therefore
$$
v_E(\sigma(\varpi)-\varpi)=p-n+1,
$$
so the break is $p-n$. Consequently the four possible upper breaks in $L/K$ are
$$
p-c,\qquad p-b,\qquad p-a,\qquad p.
$$

Step 3: Determine the upper ramification filtration
The Kummer pairing identifies the character group of $G$ with $W$. A nonzero character is represented by
$$
\pi^{x_0}\varepsilon_a^{x_a}\varepsilon_b^{x_b}\varepsilon_c^{x_c}.
$$
If $x_0\neq0$, its break is $p$, giving $p^3(p-1)$ characters. If $x_0=0$ and $x_a\neq0$, its first unit level is $a$, giving $p^2(p-1)$ characters of break $p-a$. Similarly, there are $p(p-1)$ characters of break $p-b$ and $p-1$ characters of break $p-c$.

For an abelian extension, the image of $G^u$ in a cyclic quotient is that quotient's upper ramification group. Thus a character is nontrivial on $G^u$ exactly while $u$ does not exceed its quotient break. Hence
$$
|G^u|=
\begin{cases}
p^4,&0\leq u\leq p-c,\\
p^3,&p-c<u\leq p-b,\\
p^2,&p-b<u\leq p-a,\\
p,&p-a<u\leq p,\\
1,&u>p.
\end{cases}
$$

Step 4: Convert to lower numbering and compute the relative different
For the Herbrand function
$$
\psi(u)=\int_0^u[G^0:G^t]\,dt,
$$
the successive indices are $1,p,p^2,p^3$. Thus the lower breaks are
$$
\ell_1=p-c,
$$
$$
\ell_2=(p-c)+p(c-b),
$$
$$
\ell_3=(p-c)+p(c-b)+p^2(b-a),
$$
and
$$
\ell_4=(p-c)+p(c-b)+p^2(b-a)+p^3a.
$$
Therefore
$$
b_{L/K}=ap^3+(b-a)p^2+(c-b)p+p-c.
$$

The lower ramification groups have orders $p^4,p^3,p^2,p$ on intervals of respective lengths
$$
\ell_1+1,\qquad p(c-b),\qquad p^2(b-a),\qquad p^3a.
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
v_L(\mathfrak D_{L/K})
={}&(p-c+1)(p^4-1)+p(c-b)(p^3-1)\\
&+p^2(b-a)(p^2-1)+p^3a(p-1)\\
={}&(p+1)p^4-b_{L/K}-1.
\end{aligned}
$$

Step 5: Pass to the absolute discriminant and verify the result independently
The extension $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is totally ramified of degree $p-1$. Since $p\nmid(p-1)$, it is tame, and the different exponent of a tame totally ramified extension is its ramification index minus $1$. Therefore
$$
v_K(\mathfrak D_{K/\mathbb Q_p})=p-2.
$$
The tower formula for the different gives
$$
v_L(\mathfrak D_{L/\mathbb Q_p})
=v_L(\mathfrak D_{L/K})+p^4(p-2)
=(2p-1)p^4-b_{L/K}-1.
$$
Every nontrivial character of $G$ has positive break, so $L/K$ is totally ramified. Hence $L/\mathbb Q_p$ is totally ramified and the last quantity equals $v_p(\operatorname{Disc}(L/\mathbb Q_p))$.

As an independent check, the conductor-discriminant formula and the character counts from Step 3 give
$$
\begin{aligned}
v_K(\operatorname{Disc}(L/K))
={}&(p-1)\big(p^3(p+1)+p^2(p-a+1)\\
&\qquad+p(p-b+1)+(p-c+1)\big)\\
={}&(p+1)p^4-\big(ap^3+(b-a)p^2+(c-b)p+p-c\big)-1.
\end{aligned}
$$
This matches the lower-group calculation. For the smallest boundary example $p=7$, $(a,b,c)=(1,2,3)$, both formulas give $b_{L/K}=403$ and absolute discriminant exponent $30809$.

Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(ap^3+(b-a)p^2+(c-b)p+p-c,\ 2p^5-p^4-ap^3-(b-a)p^2-(c-b+1)p+c-1\right)}$

---

## Steps

Step 1: Parametrize the recurrence and compute the local orders
Since
$$
3t^2-10t+3=(3t-1)(t-3),
$$
the relation $t+t^{-1}=10/3$ is solved by $t=3$. Therefore
$$
x_k=3^{2^k}+3^{-2^k}
$$
in every $R_n$, because this formula gives $x_0=10/3$ and squaring it subtracts $2$ to produce the next term.

For the two prime powers,
$$
\operatorname{ord}_{7}(3)=6,\qquad v_7(3^6-1)=1,
$$
and
$$
3^8\equiv-1\pmod{17},\qquad v_{17}(3^{16}-1)=1.
$$
The second valuation follows from $3^{16}-1=(3^8-1)(3^8+1)$ and $3^8+1=6562=17\cdot386$, with $17\nmid386$. LTE gives
$$
v_7(3^{6m}-1)=1+v_7(m),\qquad v_{17}(3^{16m}-1)=1+v_{17}(m).
$$
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{ord}_{7^n}(3)=6\cdot7^{n-1},\qquad \operatorname{ord}_{17^n}(3)=16\cdot17^{n-1}.
$$

Step 2: Determine the preperiod and period
For powers $a,b$ of $2$,
$$
3^a+3^{-a}-3^b-3^{-b}=(3^a-3^b)(1-3^{-a-b}).
$$
Modulo $7^n$, the two factors cannot both be divisible by $7$: that would force $6\mid a-b$ and $6\mid a+b$, so $3\mid2b$, impossible for a power of $2$. Equality modulo $7^n$ is therefore equivalent to
$$
a\equiv b\pmod{6\cdot7^{n-1}}\quad\text{or}\quad a\equiv-b\pmod{6\cdot7^{n-1}}.
$$
The odd exponent $1$ cannot be congruent to any later even exponent modulo $2$, so the local preperiod at $7$ is $1$. For $k\geq1$, a return after $d$ steps is equivalent to
$$
2^d\equiv\pm1\pmod{3\cdot7^{n-1}}.
$$
Since $v_7(2^3-1)=1$, LTE gives
$$
\operatorname{ord}_{7^{n-1}}(2)=3\cdot7^{n-2}.
$$
Also $\operatorname{ord}_{3}(2)=2$, so the order modulo $3\cdot7^{n-1}$ is $6\cdot7^{n-2}$. A negative return is impossible modulo $7^{n-1}$ because the subgroup generated by $2$ there has odd order $3\cdot7^{n-2}$. Therefore
$$
L_7=6\cdot7^{n-2}.
$$

For the $17$-part, all terms with $k\geq4$ satisfy $x_k\equiv2\pmod{17}$, while $x_3\equiv-2\pmod{17}$ because $3^8\equiv-1\pmod{17}$. The local preperiod is exactly $4$. Put $z=3^{16}$. Since $v_{17}(z-1)=1$, LTE gives, for $j\geq0$ and $d\geq1$,
$$
v_{17}(x_{4+j+d}-x_{4+j})=2+v_{17}(4^d-1).
$$
Indeed the two factors in the difference have valuations $1+v_{17}(2^d-1)$ and $1+v_{17}(2^d+1)$. For $n=2$ every $d\geq1$ gives equality modulo $17^2$, so $L_{17}=1$. For $n\geq3$, the least return is the order of $4$ modulo $17^{n-2}$. Since $4^2\equiv-1\pmod{17}$ and $v_{17}(4^4-1)=1$,
$$
L_{17}=4\cdot17^{n-3}\qquad(n\geq3).
$$
The Chinese remainder theorem gives
$$
\mu_n=4
$$
and
$$
\lambda_n=6\cdot2^{\min(1,n-2)}7^{n-2}17^{\max(n-3,0)}.
$$

Step 3: Compute the cycle sum modulo $7^n$
Let $z=3^{16}$ modulo $7^n$. Its order is
$$
N=3\cdot7^{n-1}.
$$
The powers $2^j$ have order $L_7=6\cdot7^{n-2}$ modulo $N$. The element $-1$ is not in the subgroup they generate, and
$$
\varphi(N)=12\cdot7^{n-2}=2L_7.
$$
Therefore the exponents $\pm2^j$, for $0\leq j<L_7$, run through all units modulo $N$. The local $7$-cycle sum is
$$
C_7=\sum_{\substack{1\leq a\leq N\\(a,N)=1}}z^a.
$$
With $S_d=\sum_{b=1}^{N/d}z^{db}$, Möbius inclusion-exclusion gives
$$
C_7=S_1-S_3-S_7+S_{21}.
$$
Because $z^N\equiv1\pmod{7^n}$ and both $z-1$ and $z^7-1$ are units modulo $7$, the geometric sums give
$$
S_1\equiv S_7\equiv0\pmod{7^n}.
$$
Put $w=z^3$. Then $v_7(w-1)=1$ and $v_7(w^7-1)=2$. If $v_p(q-1)=c\geq1$ and $M=p^m$, then
$$
\sum_{b=1}^{M}q^b\equiv M\pmod{p^{m+c}}.
$$
For completeness, expand $(q^M-1)/(q-1)$ by the binomial theorem. After the leading term $M$, a term indexed by $r\geq2$ has valuation at least
$$
m-v_p(r)+(r-1)c\geq m+c,
$$
and multiplication by $q$ changes the result by another multiple of $p^{m+c}$. Applying this with $(q,M)=(w,7^{n-1})$ and $(w^7,7^{n-2})$ gives
$$
S_3\equiv7^{n-1},\qquad S_{21}\equiv7^{n-2}\pmod{7^n}.
$$
Therefore
$$
C_7\equiv-6\cdot7^{n-2}\pmod{7^n}.
$$
For $n=2$ the global period contains one such cycle, while for $n\geq3$ it contains $2\cdot17^{n-3}$ such cycles. The full cycle sum $S_n$ satisfies
$$
S_n\equiv-6\pmod{49}\qquad(n=2),
$$
and
$$
S_n\equiv-84\cdot119^{n-3}\pmod{7^n}\qquad(n\geq3).
$$

Step 4: Compute the cycle sum modulo $17^n$
Put $z=3^{16}=1+h$, so $v_{17}(h)=1$. For $n=2$,
$$
z+z^{-1}-2=\frac{(z-1)^2}{z}
$$
is divisible by $17^2$. Every term of the tail is therefore $2$ modulo $17^2$. Since the global period is $6$,
$$
S_n\equiv12\pmod{289}\qquad(n=2).
$$

Assume $n\geq3$ and let $L=4\cdot17^{n-3}$. For an integer $a$,
$$
(1+h)^a+(1+h)^{-a}=2+\sum_{m\geq2}P_m(a)h^m,
$$
where
$$
P_m(a)=\binom{a}{m}+\binom{-a}{m}.
$$
The polynomial $P_m$ is even, has no constant term, and is a linear combination of $a^{2r}$ with $1\leq r\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor$. Modulo $17^n$ only $m<n$ can contribute because each binomial coefficient is an integer and $17^m\mid h^m$.

Summing over $a=2^j$, $0\leq j<L$, reduces every correction term to geometric sums
$$
A_r=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}4^{rj}.
$$
If $4\nmid r$, then $4^r-1$ is a unit modulo $17$. Since $4^L\equiv1\pmod{17^{n-2}}$,
$$
v_{17}(A_r)\geq n-2.
$$
If $4\mid r$, LTE gives
$$
v_{17}(A_r)=v_{17}(4^{rL}-1)-v_{17}(4^r-1)=n-3.
$$
In this second case $r\geq4$, so the monomial $a^{2r}$ can occur only when $m\geq8$. The rational coefficients of $P_m$ have denominators dividing $m!$, while $h^m$ contributes $m$ powers of $17$. Thus the net contribution is at least $m-v_{17}(m!)$ powers of $17$, which is at least $2$ in the first case and at least $8$ when $m\geq8$ in the second case. Every correction term is divisible by $17^n$. Therefore the local $17$-cycle sum is
$$
C_{17}\equiv2L=8\cdot17^{n-3}\pmod{17^n}.
$$
The global cycle contains $3\cdot7^{n-2}$ such local cycles, so
$$
S_n\equiv168\cdot119^{n-3}\pmod{17^n}\qquad(n\geq3).
$$

Step 5: Reconstruct the cycle sum and state the closed form
For $n=2$, Step 3 and Step 4 give
$$
S_n\equiv-6\pmod{49},\qquad S_n\equiv12\pmod{289}.
$$
Writing $S_n=12+289a$ gives $-5a\equiv31\pmod{49}$, so $a\equiv33\pmod{49}$ and
$$
s_2=9549.
$$

For $n\geq3$, the congruences from Step 3 and Step 4 permit
$$
S_n=119^{n-3}t.
$$
After dividing by the appropriate prime powers,
$$
t\equiv-84\pmod{343},\qquad t\equiv168\pmod{4913}.
$$
Set $t=168+4913a$. Since $4913\equiv111\pmod{343}$,
$$
111a\equiv91\pmod{343}.
$$
The inverse of $111$ modulo $343$ is $34$, so $a\equiv7\pmod{343}$ and
$$
t=34559.
$$
Therefore $s_n=34559\cdot119^{n-3}$ for $n\geq3$. The two cases can be written without a cases environment as
$$
s_n=(9549+25010\min(1,n-2))119^{\max(n-3,0)}.
$$
Combining this with Step 2 gives the requested tuple.
Final Answer: $\boxed{(4,6\cdot2^{\min(1,n-2)}7^{n-2}17^{\max(n-3,0)},(9549+25010\min(1,n-2))119^{\max(n-3,0)})}$

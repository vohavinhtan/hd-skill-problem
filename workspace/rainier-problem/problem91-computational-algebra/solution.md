## Steps

Step 1: Determine the group generated by the two automorphisms

Write the curve projectively as
$$
C:\quad X^n+\omega Y^n+\omega^2Z^n=0.
$$
The automorphism $\sigma$ is
$$
[X:Y:Z]\mapsto[\zeta X:\zeta^{-4}Y:Z].
$$
It has order $21$.

The map $\tau$ is induced by
$$
[X:Y:Z]\mapsto[Y:Z:X].
$$
Indeed,
$$
Y^n+\omega Z^n+\omega^2X^n
=
\omega^2\left(X^n+\omega Y^n+\omega^2Z^n\right),
$$
so $\tau$ preserves $C$, and $\tau^3=1$.

A direct conjugation gives
$$
\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{16}.
$$
Since
$$
16^3\equiv1\pmod{21}
$$
and
$$
16\not\equiv1\pmod{21},
$$
conjugation by $\tau$ induces an automorphism of order $3$ on $\langle\sigma\rangle$. Therefore
$$
H\cong C_{21}\rtimes C_3
$$
and
$$
|H|=63.
$$

Step 2: Compute the genus of the covering curve

The curve $C$ is a smooth diagonal plane curve of degree $n$. Its partial derivatives are nonzero simultaneously only at
$$
X=Y=Z=0,
$$
which is not a projective point, because $p\nmid n$.

Hence
$$
g(C)=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}2
$$
and
$$
2g(C)-2=n(n-3).
$$

Since $p\nmid63$, the extension
$$
L/K_p
$$
is tame.

Step 3: Show that every nonidentity element of the normal subgroup is fixed-point free

Consider
$$
\sigma^j,
\qquad
1\leq j\leq20.
$$
Its three projective eigenvalues are
$$
\zeta^j,\qquad
\zeta^{-4j},\qquad
1.
$$
They are pairwise distinct.

Indeed,
$$
\zeta^j=1
$$
or
$$
\zeta^{-4j}=1
$$
would force $21\mid j$, while
$$
\zeta^j=\zeta^{-4j}
$$
would give
$$
21\mid5j,
$$
again forcing $21\mid j$.

A projective fixed point of $\sigma^j$ must therefore be one of the three coordinate points. None lies on $C$, since each leaves exactly one nonzero term in the defining equation.

Thus
$$
\#\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^j)=0
$$
for every nonidentity element of $\langle\sigma\rangle$.

Step 4: Count the fixed points in a nontrivial coset

Consider
$$
g_j=\sigma^j\tau.
$$
Projectively,
$$
g_j[X:Y:Z]=[\zeta^jY:\zeta^{-4j}Z:X].
$$
If $[X:Y:Z]$ is an eigenpoint with eigenvalue $\lambda$, then
$$
\zeta^jY=\lambda X,
$$
$$
\zeta^{-4j}Z=\lambda Y,
$$
$$
X=\lambda Z.
$$
All coordinates are nonzero, and multiplication of these equations gives
$$
\lambda^3=\zeta^{-3j}.
$$

Because $3\mid n$,
$$
\lambda^n
=
(\lambda^3)^{n/3}
=
\zeta^{-jn}
=
1.
$$
Also $\zeta^n=1$, so the eigenvector equations give
$$
Y^n=X^n,
\qquad
Z^n=X^n.
$$
Every eigenpoint therefore satisfies
$$
X^n+\omega Y^n+\omega^2Z^n
=
X^n(1+\omega+\omega^2)
=
0.
$$

The polynomial
$$
T^3-\zeta^{-3j}
$$
has three distinct roots because $p\neq3$. Hence $g_j$ has exactly three projective eigenlines, and all three lie on $C$:
$$
\#\operatorname{Fix}(g_j)=3.
$$

The same argument applies to every element
$$
\sigma^j\tau^2.
$$
There are $21$ elements in each of the two nontrivial cosets, so the total fixed-point contribution is
$$
R=21\cdot3+21\cdot3=126.
$$

Step 5: Apply Riemann-Hurwitz

The tame group-action form of Riemann-Hurwitz gives
$$
n(n-3)
=
63(2g(K_p)-2)+126.
$$
Hence
$$
2g(K_p)-2
=
\frac{n(n-3)}{63}-2.
$$

Write
$$
n=21M.
$$
Since $p\equiv1\pmod{42}$, $M$ is even. We have
$$
\frac{n(n-3)}{63}
=
M(7M-1).
$$
Therefore
$$
2g(K_p)=M(7M-1)
$$
and
$$
g(K_p)=\frac{M(7M-1)}2.
$$
Substituting
$$
M=\frac{p-1}{21}
$$
gives
$$
7M-1=\frac{p-4}{3}.
$$
It follows that
$$
g(K_p)=\frac{(p-1)(p-4)}{126}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{(p-1)(p-4)}{126}}$

## Answer

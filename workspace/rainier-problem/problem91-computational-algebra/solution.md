## Steps

Step 1: Rewrite the quotient in coordinates adapted to the multiplication map

Put
$$
u=x+y,
\qquad
v=y.
$$
Then
$$
x=u-v,
\qquad
x+2y=u+v,
$$
and multiplication by $\ell$ becomes multiplication by $u$. Therefore
$$
A_p\cong
\frac{\mathbb F_p[u,v]}
{\left((u-v)^{p+2},v^{p+2},u^{p+1},(u+v)^{p+1}\right)}.
$$

In characteristic $p$,
$$
(u+v)^{p+1}
=u^{p+1}+u^pv+uv^p+v^{p+1}.
$$
After removing the generator $u^{p+1}$, define
$$
r=u^pv+uv^p+v^{p+1}.
$$

Also
$$
(u-v)^{p+2}
=(u^p-v^p)(u-v)^2.
$$
Modulo $u^{p+1}$ and $v^{p+2}$, this generator becomes
$$
s=u^pv^2-u^2v^p+2uv^{p+1}.
$$
Hence the transformed defining ideal is
$$
J=(u^{p+1},v^{p+2},r,s).
$$

Step 2: Extract the monomial boundary relations from the two remaining generators

Multiplication of $r$ by $u$ and $v$ gives
$$
ur=u^{p+1}v+u^2v^p+uv^{p+1},
$$
$$
vr=u^pv^2+uv^{p+1}+v^{p+2}.
$$
Using the generators $u^{p+1}$ and $v^{p+2}$,
$$
s-vr+ur\equiv2uv^{p+1}\pmod{J}.
$$
Since $p\geq5$, the element $2$ is invertible in $\mathbb F_p$, so
$$
uv^{p+1}\in J.
$$
The identities for $ur$ and $vr$ now give
$$
u^2v^p\in J,
\qquad
u^pv^2\in J.
$$

Conversely, $s$ belongs to the ideal generated by these three monomials. Therefore
$$
J=
\left(
u^{p+1},
v^{p+2},
r,
uv^{p+1},
u^2v^p,
u^pv^2
\right).
$$
This form records every relation needed for the action of multiplication by $u$.

Step 3: Convert the quotient into a direct sum of cyclic modules over $\mathbb F_p[u]$

As an $\mathbb F_p[u]$-module, $A_p$ is generated by
$$
e_j=v^j,
\qquad
0\leq j\leq p+1.
$$
The monomial generators from Step 2 impose
$$
u^{p+1}e_0=u^{p+1}e_1=0,
$$
$$
u^pe_j=0
\qquad
(2\leq j\leq p-1),
$$
$$
u^2e_p=0,
\qquad
ue_{p+1}=0.
$$
The only relation mixing different $v$-degrees is
$$
u^pe_1+ue_p+e_{p+1}=0.
$$

No further mixed relation is produced by multiplying this relation by $v$. For one multiplication,
$$
vr=u^pv^2+uv^{p+1}+v^{p+2}=0
$$
by the monomial relations from Step 2. Every higher positive power of $v$ also gives zero term by term. Multiplication by powers of $u$ is already part of the $\mathbb F_p[u]$-submodule generated by the displayed relation.

Eliminate $e_{p+1}$:
$$
e_{p+1}=-u^pe_1-ue_p.
$$
Its relation $ue_{p+1}=0$ becomes
$$
u^{p+1}e_1+u^2e_p=0,
$$
which is already implied by the annihilator relations above. The remaining generators are independent as cyclic $\mathbb F_p[u]$-summands. Therefore
$$
A_p\cong
\left(\frac{\mathbb F_p[u]}{(u^{p+1})}\right)^{\!2}
\oplus
\left(\frac{\mathbb F_p[u]}{(u^p)}\right)^{\!p-2}
\oplus
\frac{\mathbb F_p[u]}{(u^2)}.
$$

Step 4: Read the Jordan blocks from the cyclic decomposition

On
$$
\frac{\mathbb F_p[u]}{(u^m)},
$$
multiplication by $u$ has the basis
$$
1,u,\ldots,u^{m-1}
$$
and consists of one nilpotent Jordan block of size $m$.

Step 3 therefore gives two blocks of size $p+1$, exactly $p-2$ blocks of size $p$, and one block of size $2$. Since $u$ is the residue class of $x+y$, these are precisely the Jordan blocks of $M_\ell$. Hence
$$
\mathcal J_p(t)=2t^{p+1}+(p-2)t^p+t^2.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{2t^{p+1}+(p-2)t^p+t^2}$

---

## Answer

$2t^{p+1}+(p-2)t^p+t^2$

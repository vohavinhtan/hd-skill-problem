## Steps

Step 1: Convert the four requested counts into four character sums

For $k=0,1,2,3$, define
$$
S_k=\sum_{\substack{x_1+\cdots+x_5=1\\ x_i\in\mathbb F_p^\times\\ x_r\neq \pm x_s\ (r<s)}}\chi^k(x_1x_2x_3x_4x_5).
$$
The condition $\chi(x_1x_2x_3x_4x_5)=i^j$ is isolated by the order-$4$ character projector
$$
\mathbf 1_{\chi(\prod x_i)=i^j}
=\frac14\sum_{k=0}^{3}i^{-jk}\chi^k(x_1x_2x_3x_4x_5).
$$
Therefore
$$
N_j=\frac14\sum_{k=0}^3 i^{-jk}S_k.
$$
Since $p\equiv1\pmod 8$, we have $\chi(-1)=1$. Hence the condition $x_r\neq \pm x_s$ is equivalent to saying that the five squares $x_1^2,\ldots,x_5^2$ are pairwise distinct. Also $S_3=\overline{S_1}$, because $\chi^3=\overline{\chi}$.

Step 2: Evaluate the unrestricted Jacobi sums before enforcing signed distinctness

For a nontrivial multiplicative character $\psi$ extended by $\psi(0)=0$, the five-variable Jacobi sum is
$$
\sum_{\substack{x_1+\cdots+x_5=1\\ x_i\in\mathbb F_p^\times}}\psi(x_1x_2x_3x_4x_5)
=\frac{g(\psi)^5}{g(\psi^5)}
$$
when $\psi^5$ is nontrivial, where $g(\psi)$ is the Gauss sum of $\psi$. The nontrivial characters used here are $\chi,\eta,\overline\chi$. Each satisfies $\psi^4=1$, so $\psi^5=\psi$ and the quotient is $g(\psi)^4$. For the trivial character, inclusion-exclusion over the five zero coordinates gives
$$
p^4-5p^3+10p^2-10p+5.
$$
The Gauss sums satisfy
$$
g(\chi)^2=Jg(\eta),\qquad g(\overline\chi)^2=\overline J\,g(\eta),\qquad g(\eta)^2=p.
$$
Thus the unrestricted sums are
$$
T_0=p^4-5p^3+10p^2-10p+5,\qquad
T_1=pJ^2,\qquad
T_2=p^2,\qquad
T_3=p\overline J^{\,2}.
$$

Step 3:
Impose the condition $x_a\neq \pm x_b$ by inclusion-exclusion over signed collision edges. A signed edge $(a,b,+)$ means $x_a=x_b$, and a signed edge $(a,b,-)$ means $x_a=-x_b$. It is cleaner to sum over the signed partition lattice generated by these edges. For a signed block $B$, choose the smallest index in $B$ to have sign $+1$, write $b=|B|$, and write
$$
c(B)=\left|\sum_{i\in B}\varepsilon_i\right|.
$$
Only consistent signed collision graphs need to be retained. Along a signed cycle, composing the edge relations gives
$$
x_v=\left(\prod_{\text{edges of the cycle}}\varepsilon_e\right)x_v.
$$
If the sign product is $-1$, then $x_v=-x_v$, so $2x_v=0$. Since $p$ is odd, this forces $x_v=0$, contradicting $x_v\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. The intersection represented by such a graph is therefore empty and contributes $0$. If every signed cycle has product $+1$, choose one root variable in each connected component; propagation along paths assigns consistent signs to all other variables, uniquely up to reversing every sign in that component. These consistent components are exactly the signed blocks used below.

For a fixed signed partition $\Pi$, its Mobius coefficient is
$$
\mu(\Pi)=\prod_{B\in\Pi}(-1)^{|B|-1}(|B|-1)!.
$$
If $\Pi$ has block data $(b_1,c_1),\ldots,(b_t,c_t)$, then its contribution to $S_k$ is
$$
\mu(\Pi)\left(\prod_{c_\nu=0}Z_{k b_\nu}\right)
\left(\prod_{c_\nu\neq0}\chi^{-k b_\nu}(c_\nu)\right)
\mathcal J_k(b_\nu:c_\nu\neq0),
$$
where
$$
Z_a=\begin{cases}p-1,&a\equiv0\pmod4,\\0,&a\not\equiv0\pmod4,\end{cases}
$$
and
$$
\mathcal J_k(b_1,\ldots,b_u)=
\sum_{z_1+\cdots+z_u=1}\chi^{k b_1}(z_1)\cdots\chi^{k b_u}(z_u),
$$
with every $z_\nu$ nonzero. Fix a nontrivial additive character $e$ of $\mathbb F_p$. The required Jacobi evaluations are obtained from the identity
$$
\mathcal J_k(b_1,\ldots,b_u)
=\frac1p\sum_{v\in\mathbb F_p}e(-v)
\prod_{\nu=1}^{u}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb F_p^\times}\chi^{k b_\nu}(z)e(vz)\right).
$$
For $v\neq0$, the inner sum is $\chi^{-k b_\nu}(v)g(\chi^{k b_\nu})$ when $\chi^{k b_\nu}$ is nontrivial, and it is $-1$ when $\chi^{k b_\nu}$ is trivial. The $v=0$ term is present only when every involved character is trivial. In that all-trivial case, the same identity gives
$$
\mathcal J_k(b_1,\ldots,b_u)=\frac{(p-1)^u-(-1)^u}{p}.
$$
The identities
$$
g(\chi)^2=Jg(\chi^2),\qquad g(\chi^2)^2=p,
$$
and conjugation reduce every term to $p,J,\overline J,\chi(2),\chi(3),\chi(5)$.

The following ledger is the signed-partition computation. A row $((b_1,c_1),\ldots,(b_t,c_t))$ means that the signed blocks have those sizes and absolute signed sums. The number in the second column is the aggregate of $\mu(\Pi)$ over all labelled signed partitions with that block data.
For a single block, put
$$
a(b,c)=\#\{(\varepsilon_2,\ldots,\varepsilon_b)\in\{1,-1\}^{b-1}: |1+\varepsilon_2+\cdots+\varepsilon_b|=c\}.
$$
If a type has $n_{b,c}$ blocks of kind $(b,c)$, then the aggregate coefficient in the table is
$$
\frac{5!}{\prod_{b,c}(b!)^{n_{b,c}}n_{b,c}!}
\prod_{b,c}a(b,c)^{n_{b,c}}
\prod_{b,c}\left((-1)^{b-1}(b-1)!\right)^{n_{b,c}}.
$$

## Verification

- Sanity check: summing the four displayed values gives $A$, so the four character classes partition the full admissible set.
- Conjugation check: $S_3=\overline{S_1}$, so the formulas for $N_1$ and $N_3$ are conjugate-compatible and real.
- Counterexample attack: the tempting replacement of signed distinctness by ordinary distinctness fails, because $x_r=-x_s$ is also forbidden. The inclusion-exclusion is therefore performed on equal squares $x_i^2$, not on equal entries $x_i$.
- Small-prime check: for $p=17$, direct enumeration gives $(3240,3000,3120,3360)$, and the displayed formula gives the same quadruple. For $p=41$, direct enumeration gives $(365760,359880,363120,363720)$, again matching the formula.

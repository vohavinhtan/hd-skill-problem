## Steps

Step 1: Reduce every automorphism to its linear part and count its higher-order lifts

Let $x,y$ denote the residue classes of $X,Y$, and let
$$
\mathfrak m=(x,y).
$$
Since
$$
A/\mathfrak m\cong K,
$$
the ideal $\mathfrak m$ is the unique maximal ideal, so every $K$-algebra automorphism preserves the filtration
$$
\mathfrak m\supset\mathfrak m^2\supset\cdots\supset\mathfrak m^5\supset0.
$$

Put
$$
f(X,Y)=5X^4Y+10X^2Y^3+Y^5.
$$
There are no relations in degrees below $5$, while the only degree-$5$ relation is the line $Kf$. Hence the graded dimensions are
$$
1,2,3,4,5,5.
$$
In particular,
$$
\dim_K\mathfrak m^2=3+4+5+5=17.
$$

Let $L\in\operatorname{GL}_2(K)$ be the induced map on $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$. Necessarily
$$
f(L(X,Y))=\kappa f(X,Y)
$$
for some $\kappa\in K^\times$.

Conversely, suppose $L$ has this property. Choose arbitrary
$$
h_1,h_2\in\mathfrak m^2
$$
and send
$$
\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}
\longmapsto
L\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}
+
\begin{pmatrix}h_1\\h_2\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Substituting a term of degree at least $2$ into the homogeneous quintic $f$ changes it only in degrees at least $6$, which vanish in $A$. Thus the defining relation is preserved. The induced map on $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ is invertible, so the resulting endomorphism is an automorphism.

Therefore every admissible $L$ has exactly
$$
q^{17}q^{17}=q^{34}
$$
lifts, and distinct choices give distinct automorphisms.

Step 2: Reveal the hidden five-point configuration

The congruence assumption implies that the characteristic is neither $2$ nor $5$. Make the invertible change of variables
$$
U=X+Y,\qquad V=X-Y.
$$
The binomial theorem gives
$$
U^5-V^5=2\left(5X^4Y+10X^2Y^3+Y^5\right)=2f(X,Y).
$$

Thus the projective linear parts preserving $Kf$ are conjugate to the projective transformations preserving the line generated by
$$
U^5-V^5.
$$
Since
$$
q\equiv1\pmod5,
$$
the field $K$ contains all five fifth roots of unity.

Step 3: Determine the projective stabilizer without invoking a classification theorem

Let
$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_2(K)
$$
preserve the line $K(U^5-V^5)$. Then for some $\kappa\neq0$,
$$
(aU+bV)^5-(cU+dV)^5=\kappa(U^5-V^5).
$$

The four mixed coefficients give
$$
a^kb^{5-k}=c^kd^{5-k},
\qquad
1\leq k\leq4,
$$
because the relevant binomial coefficients are nonzero in $K$.

If $a,b,c,d$ were all nonzero, the equations for $k=1,2$ would give
$$
\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d},
$$
contradicting $ad-bc\neq0$.

If $b=0$, the equation for $k=1$ and invertibility force $c=0$; similarly $c=0$ forces $b=0$. If $a=0$, the equation for $k=4$ forces $d=0$, and conversely. Hence $H$ is either diagonal or anti-diagonal.

For
$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&d\end{pmatrix},
$$
we need $a^5=d^5$, so projectively there are five maps
$$
H_j=\begin{pmatrix}\zeta^j&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
0\leq j<5,
$$
where $\zeta$ is any primitive fifth root of unity.

For the anti-diagonal case there are five projective maps represented by
$$
S_j=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\zeta^j\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
0\leq j<5.
$$
Thus there are exactly
$$
10
$$
projective classes of possible linear parts.

Step 4: Compute the determinant of an automorphism from its highest-degree multiplier

Let
$$
\delta=\det L
$$
and let $\kappa$ be defined by
$$
f(L(X,Y))=\kappa f(X,Y).
$$

Because every automorphism preserves the maximal-ideal filtration, its determinant equals the product of the determinants on the graded pieces. On the degree-$d$ binary forms,
$$
\det(\operatorname{Sym}^dL)=\delta^{d(d+1)/2}.
$$
Indeed, over an algebraic closure, if the eigenvalues of $L$ are $\lambda,\mu$, the eigenvalues on degree $d$ are
$$
\lambda^d,\lambda^{d-1}\mu,\ldots,\mu^d,
$$
whose product is
$$
(\lambda\mu)^{d(d+1)/2}.
$$

Degrees $1,2,3,4$ therefore contribute
$$
\delta^{1+3+6+10}=\delta^{20}.
$$
In degree $5$, $\operatorname{Sym}^5(K^2)$ has determinant $\delta^{15}$, but the relation line $Kf$ has eigenvalue $\kappa$. Hence the five-dimensional quotient contributes
$$
\frac{\delta^{15}}{\kappa}.
$$
Consequently every lift with linear part $L$ has
$$
\det_K(\varphi)=\frac{\delta^{35}}{\kappa}.
$$
The arbitrary higher-order terms from Step 1 do not change this value because they act trivially on the associated graded pieces.

Step 5: Impose determinant one inside each projective class

Work first in the $U,V$ coordinates. For $H_j$,
$$
\det H_j=\zeta^j,\qquad \kappa=1,
$$
and
$$
(\det H_j)^{35}=\zeta^{35j}=1=\kappa.
$$

For $S_j$,
$$
\det S_j=-\zeta^j,\qquad \kappa=-1,
$$
so
$$
(\det S_j)^{35}=-1=\kappa.
$$
Conjugating back to the $X,Y$ coordinates preserves both the determinant and the multiplier $\kappa$. Thus every one of the ten projective classes has a representative $L_0$ satisfying
$$
\kappa_0=(\det L_0)^{35}.
$$

Every other representative of that projective class is
$$
L=cL_0,
\qquad
c\in K^\times.
$$
Since $f$ has degree $5$,
$$
\det L=c^2\det L_0,
\qquad
\kappa=c^5\kappa_0.
$$
Step 4 therefore gives
$$
\det_K(\varphi)
=
\frac{c^{70}(\det L_0)^{35}}{c^5\kappa_0}
=
c^{65}.
$$

Hence determinant one is equivalent to
$$
c^{65}=1.
$$

Step 6: Count the surviving linear parts and all their lifts

From
$$
q\equiv71\pmod{130}
$$
we have
$$
q-1\equiv70\pmod{130}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\gcd(65,q-1)=5.
$$
Since $K^\times$ is cyclic, the equation
$$
c^{65}=1
$$
has exactly five solutions.

Each of the ten projective classes consequently contributes five determinant-one linear parts, for a total of
$$
50
$$
possible linear parts.

Step 1 gives exactly $q^{34}$ automorphisms above each one. Thus the required number is
$$
50q^{34}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{50q^{34}}$

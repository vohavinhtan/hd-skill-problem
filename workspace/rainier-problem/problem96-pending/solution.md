## Steps

Step 1: Reduce the count to quotient maps preserving two forms and one subgroup

Write
$$
A(v,w)=ad'+bc'-cb'-da'
$$
and
$$
B(v,w)=ac'-ca'+t(bd'-db'),
$$
so that
$$
\beta(v,w)=(A(v,w),tB(v,w)).
$$
The form $A$ is nondegenerate over $R$. If $A(v,w)=0$ for every $w$, testing the four coordinate directions gives $a=b=c=d=0$. Therefore
$$
Z(G)=\{0\}\times Z.
$$

A center-fixing automorphism has the form
$$
\alpha(v,z)=(Lv,z+f(v)),
$$
where $L$ is an additive automorphism of $V$. The homomorphism condition gives
$$
f(v+w)-f(v)-f(w)=\frac12\left(\beta(Lv,Lw)-\beta(v,w)\right).
$$
The left side is symmetric in $v,w$, while the right side is alternating. Since $p$ is odd, both vanish. Hence $f$ is additive and $L$ preserves $\beta$. Conversely, every such pair gives an automorphism.

The subgroup $P$ contains the whole center, so an arbitrary additive $f$ does not affect the condition $\alpha(P)=P$. Since
$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_p}V=4n,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb F_p}Z=2n,
$$
there are
$$
p^{8n^2}
$$
choices for $f$.

Step 2: Recover $R$-linearity from the first commutator coordinate

Let $T$ be multiplication by $t$ on $V$. Since $L$ preserves the $R$-valued form $A$,
$$
A(LTv,Lw)=A(Tv,w)=tA(v,w),
$$
while
$$
A(TLv,Lw)=tA(Lv,Lw)=tA(v,w).
$$
As $Lw$ ranges over $V$, nondegeneracy of $A$ gives
$$
LTv=TLv.
$$
Thus $L$ commutes with $T$, so it is $R$-linear. The first component condition is
$$
L\in\operatorname{Sp}_4(R,A).
$$

Step 3: Convert the second form into a hidden ramified module structure

Define
$$
H(a,b,c,d)=(tb,a,td,c).
$$
A direct substitution gives
$$
B(v,w)=A(Hv,w).
$$
Since $L$ preserves $A$, preservation of the second coordinate of $\beta$ is equivalent to
$$
tA((L^{-1}HL-H)v,w)=0
$$
for every $v,w$.

Put
$$
m=n-1,\qquad R_m=\mathbb F_p[t]/(t^m).
$$
Since the annihilator of $t$ in $R$ is $t^{n-1}\mathbb F_p$, the reduction $\overline L$ modulo $t^m$ must commute with the reduction of $H$.

Now set
$$
S=\mathbb F_p[x]/(x^{2m})
$$
and identify $R_m$ with the even subring generated by $x^2$, so $t=x^2$. The map
$$
(a,b,c,d)\longmapsto(a+bx,c+dx)
$$
identifies $R_m^4$ with $S^2$. Multiplication by $x$ becomes
$$
(a+bx,c+dx)\longmapsto(tb+ax,td+cx),
$$
which is exactly $H$. Therefore $\overline L$ commutes with $H$ exactly when it is $S$-linear.

Every $q\in S$ has a unique form $q=r+sx$ with $r,s\in R_m$. Let
$$
\mu(r+sx)=s.
$$
For $u=(u_1,u_2)$ and $v=(v_1,v_2)$ in $S^2$,
$$
A(u,v)=\mu(u_1v_2-u_2v_1).
$$
If $M\in\operatorname{GL}_2(S)$, then
$$
A(Mu,Mv)=\mu\left(\det(M)(u_1v_2-u_2v_1)\right).
$$
If $\mu(hq)=0$ for every $q$, writing $h=r+sx$ and taking $q=1,x$ gives $s=r=0$. Hence preservation of $A$ forces $\det(M)=1$. Therefore
$$
\overline L\in\operatorname{SL}_2(S).
$$

Step 4: Translate the subgroup condition into a twisted fixed graph

Define the involution
$$
\sigma(r+sx)=r-sx
$$
on $S$. Modulo $t^m$, the defining conditions for $P$ are
$$
c=a,\qquad d=-b.
$$
Under the identification in Step 3, the corresponding submodule is
$$
W=\{(q,\sigma(q)):q\in S\}.
$$
Thus $\alpha(P)=P$ is equivalent to
$$
\overline L(W)=W.
$$

Write
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\ u&v\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_2(S).
$$
For every $q\in S$, preservation of $W$ gives
$$
uq+v\sigma(q)=\sigma(r)\sigma(q)+\sigma(s)q.
$$
Set
$$
a=u-\sigma(s),\qquad b=v-\sigma(r).
$$
Taking $q=1$ gives $b=-a$, and the remaining condition is
$$
a(q-\sigma(q))=0\qquad\text{for every }q\in S.
$$
Since the values $q-\sigma(q)$ contain $2x$ and $2$ is invertible,
$$
ax=0.
$$
Hence
$$
a\in\mathbb F_p x^{2m-1}.
$$

The determinant equation is
$$
1=r\sigma(r)-s\sigma(s)-a(r+s).
$$
The first two terms contain only even powers of $x$. If $r_0,s_0$ are the constant terms of $r,s$, the odd term is
$$
-a(r_0+s_0).
$$
The constant term of the determinant equation gives
$$
r_0^2-s_0^2=1,
$$
so $(r_0-s_0)(r_0+s_0)=1$ and $r_0+s_0\neq0$. Therefore $a=0$.

Consequently every allowed reduction has the unique form
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}
r&s\\
\sigma(s)&\sigma(r)
\end{pmatrix}
$$
with
$$
r\sigma(r)-s\sigma(s)=1.
$$

Step 5: Count the solutions of the hidden norm equation

For
$$
S_m=\mathbb F_p[x]/(x^{2m}),
$$
let $C_m$ be the number of pairs $(r,s)$ satisfying
$$
r\sigma(r)-s\sigma(s)=1.
$$

For $m=1$, write
$$
r=a+bx,\qquad s=c+dx.
$$
Since $x^2=0$,
$$
r\sigma(r)-s\sigma(s)=a^2-c^2.
$$
The equation is
$$
(a-c)(a+c)=1.
$$
Choosing $a+c\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ determines $a-c$, hence determines $a,c$ because $2$ is invertible. The coefficients $b,d$ are free. Therefore
$$
C_1=(p-1)p^2.
$$

Suppose a solution is fixed modulo $x^{2m}$. Its lifts modulo $x^{2m+2}$ have the form
$$
r'=r+\alpha x^{2m}+\beta x^{2m+1},
$$
$$
s'=s+\gamma x^{2m}+\delta x^{2m+1}.
$$
Terms quadratic in the four new coefficients vanish modulo $x^{2m+2}$. The new coefficient of $x^{2m}$ in the norm equation changes by
$$
2(\alpha r_0-\gamma s_0).
$$
Because $r_0^2-s_0^2=1$, the pair $(r_0,s_0)$ is not $(0,0)$. Hence this is one nonzero linear equation in $\alpha,\gamma$, giving $p$ choices. The coefficients $\beta,\delta$ are free, giving another $p^2$ choices. Every solution has exactly $p^3$ lifts.

It follows that
$$
C_m=(p-1)p^2(p^3)^{m-1}=(p-1)p^{3m-1}.
$$
With $m=n-1$, the number of admissible reductions $\overline L$ is
$$
(p-1)p^{3n-4}.
$$

Step 6: Lift the reductions and combine all automorphisms

The reduction map
$$
\operatorname{Sp}_4(R,A)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_4(R_m,A)
$$
is onto. Let $\Omega$ be the matrix of $A$. If an arbitrary lift $M$ of a symplectic matrix has
$$
M^T\Omega M=\Omega+t^mE,
$$
then $E$ is alternating. Replacing $M$ by $M(I+t^mX)$ changes $E$ by
$$
X^T\Omega+\Omega X.
$$
Writing $Y=\Omega X$, this change is $Y-Y^T$. Taking $Y=-E/2$ removes the defect.

The kernel consists of $I+t^mX$ satisfying
$$
X^T\Omega+\Omega X=0.
$$
Equivalently, $\Omega X$ is symmetric. A symmetric $4\times4$ matrix over $\mathbb F_p$ has $10$ free entries, so the kernel has size
$$
p^{10}.
$$
Both the second-form condition and the subgroup condition depend only on reduction modulo $t^m$, so every one of these lifts is allowed. The number of quotient maps is therefore
$$
p^{10}(p-1)p^{3n-4}=(p-1)p^{3n+6}.
$$

Multiplying by the $p^{8n^2}$ central additive twists from Step 1 gives
$$
A_n(p)=(p-1)p^{8n^2+3n+6}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{(p-1)p^{8n^{2}+3n+6}}$

## Steps

Step 1: Reduce center-fixing automorphisms to form-preserving maps on $V$

Write
$$
A(v,w)=ad'+bc'-cb'-da'
$$
and
$$
B(v,w)=ac'-ca'+t(bd'-db'),
$$
so $\beta(v,w)=(A(v,w),tB(v,w))$. The form $A$ is nondegenerate over $R$: if $A(v,w)=0$ for every $w$, choosing $w$ successively in the fourth, third, second, and first coordinate directions gives $a=b=c=d=0$. Therefore
$$
Z(G)=\{0\}\times Z.
$$

If $\alpha$ fixes this center pointwise, its quotient map is an $\mathbb F_p$-linear automorphism $L$ of the additive group $V$, and
$$
\alpha(v,z)=(Lv,z+f(v))
$$
for some $f:V\to Z$. Comparing $\alpha((v,0)(w,0))$ with $\alpha(v,0)\alpha(w,0)$ gives
$$
f(v+w)-f(v)-f(w)=\frac12\left(\beta(Lv,Lw)-\beta(v,w)\right).
$$
The left side is symmetric in $v,w$ and the right side is alternating. Since $p$ is odd, both vanish. Hence $f$ is additive and $L$ preserves $\beta$. Conversely, every such pair gives an automorphism. There are
$$
|\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb F_p}(V,Z)|=p^{(4n)(2n)}=p^{8n^2}
$$
choices for $f$.

Step 2: Recover the hidden $R$-module structure from the first form

Let $T$ denote multiplication by $t$ on $V$, and let $\lambda:R\to\mathbb F_p$ take the coefficient of $t^{n-1}$. Define
$$
\langle v,w\rangle=\lambda(A(v,w)).
$$
This scalar alternating form is nondegenerate: a nonzero coefficient of any coordinate of $v$ can be paired with the complementary coordinate of $w$, multiplied by the needed power of $t$, to reach degree $n-1$.

Since $L$ preserves the first component $A$, it preserves $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$. For all $v,w$,
$$
\langle TLv,Lw\rangle=\lambda(tA(Lv,Lw))=\lambda(tA(v,w)),
$$
while
$$
\langle LTv,Lw\rangle=\langle Tv,w\rangle=\lambda(tA(v,w)).
$$
As $Lw$ ranges over $V$, nondegeneracy gives $TLv=LTv$. Thus $L$ commutes with $T$. Since $R=\mathbb F_p[T]$, the map $L$ is $R$-linear. The first coordinate condition is therefore
$$
L\in\operatorname{Sp}_4(R,A).
$$

Step 3: Reconstruct the operator encoded by the second form

The second form hides the $R$-linear map
$$
H(a,b,c,d)=(tb,a,td,c).
$$
Indeed,
$$
A(Hv,w)=tbd'+ac'-tdb'-ca'=B(v,w),
$$
and direct composition gives
$$
H^2=tI.
$$

Because $L$ preserves $A$,
$$
B(Lv,Lw)=A(HLv,Lw)=A(L^{-1}HLv,w).
$$
The second coordinate of $\beta$ is preserved exactly when
$$
tA((L^{-1}HL-H)v,w)=0
$$
for every $v,w$. In $R$, the annihilator of $t$ is $t^{n-1}\mathbb F_p$. Nondegeneracy of $A$ therefore gives
$$
L^{-1}HL\equiv H\pmod{t^{n-1}}.
$$
If $R_m=\mathbb F_p[t]/(t^m)$ with $m=n-1$, the reduction of $L$ to $R_m^4$ must lie in the centralizer of the reduction of $H$ inside $\operatorname{Sp}_4(R_m,A)$.

Step 4: Identify that centralizer through a ramified coefficient ring

Set
$$
S=\mathbb F_p[x]/(x^{2m})
$$
and identify $R_m$ with the subring generated by $x^2$, so $t=x^2$. The map
$$
(a,b,c,d)\longmapsto(a+bx,\ c+dx)
$$
identifies $R_m^4$ with $S^2$. Under this identification, multiplication by $x$ sends
$$
(a+bx,\ c+dx)\longmapsto(tb+ax,\ td+cx),
$$
which is exactly the operator $H$ from Step 3. Hence an $R_m$-linear map commutes with $H$ if and only if it is $S$-linear.

Let $\mu:S\to R_m$ take the coefficient of $x$ in the unique expression $r+sx$ with $r,s\in R_m$. For $u=(u_1,u_2)$ and $v=(v_1,v_2)$ in $S^2$, direct multiplication gives
$$
A(u,v)=\mu(u_1v_2-u_2v_1).
$$
If $M\in\operatorname{GL}_2(S)$, then
$$
A(Mu,Mv)=\mu\left(\det(M)(u_1v_2-u_2v_1)\right).
$$
Every element of $S$ occurs as $u_1v_2-u_2v_1$, for example by taking $u=(s,0)$ and $v=(0,1)$. The pairing $(q,s)\mapsto\mu(qs)$ is nondegenerate: for $q=r+ux$, the choices $s=1$ and $s=x$ recover $u$ and $r$. Therefore $M$ preserves $A$ exactly when $\det(M)=1$. We obtain
$$
C_{\operatorname{Sp}_4(R_m,A)}(H)\cong\operatorname{SL}_2(S).
$$

Step 5: Count the centralizer and all lifts to $R$

For $S_k=\mathbb F_p[x]/(x^k)$, reduction
$$
\operatorname{SL}_2(S_{k+1})\to\operatorname{SL}_2(S_k)
$$
is onto: if an arbitrary lift has determinant $1+cx^k$, right multiplication by $\operatorname{diag}(1-cx^k,1)$ corrects its determinant. Its kernel consists of
$$
I+x^kY,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(Y)=0,
$$
so each layer has $p^3$ elements. Since $|\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_p)|=p(p^2-1)$,
$$
|\operatorname{SL}_2(S)|=p^{3(2m-1)}p(p^2-1)=p^{6m-2}(p^2-1).
$$
With $m=n-1$, the centralizer in Step 4 has size
$$
p^{6n-8}(p^2-1).
$$

It remains to lift from $R_{n-1}$ to $R$. Let $\Omega$ be the matrix of $A$. The reduction
$$
\rho:\operatorname{Sp}_4(R,A)\to\operatorname{Sp}_4(R_{n-1},A)
$$
is onto. To see this, lift a symplectic matrix arbitrarily to $M$; then
$$
M^T\Omega M=\Omega+t^{n-1}E
$$
with $E$ alternating over $\mathbb F_p$. Multiplying $M$ on the right by $I+t^{n-1}X$ changes the error by $X^T\Omega+\Omega X$. If $Y=\Omega X$, this change is $Y-Y^T$, so taking $Y=-E/2$ removes the error.

The kernel consists of $I+t^{n-1}X$ with $X^T\Omega+\Omega X=0$. With $Y=\Omega X$, this says $Y=Y^T$. A symmetric $4\times4$ matrix has $10$ free entries, so
$$
|\ker\rho|=p^{10}.
$$
Consequently the number $N$ of possible quotient maps $L$ is
$$
N=p^{10}p^{6n-8}(p^2-1)=p^{6n+2}(p^2-1).
$$

Step 6: Combine quotient maps with central shears

Step 1 gives $p^{8n^2}$ additive central shears, while Step 5 gives $p^{6n+2}(p^2-1)$ admissible quotient maps. Therefore
$$
A_n(p)=p^{8n^2+6n+2}(p^2-1).
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{p^{8n^{2}+6n+2}(p^{2}-1)}$

# Solution

Step 1:
Convert the four residue classes into character power sums. For $k=0,1,2,3$, define
$$
S_k=\sum_{\substack{x_1+\cdots+x_5=1\\ x_a\neq \pm x_b\ (a<b)}}\chi^k(x_1x_2x_3x_4x_5),
$$
where all $x_i$ are required to lie in $\mathbb F_p^\times$. If
$$
\chi(x_1x_2x_3x_4x_5)=\mathrm{i}^{\,j},
$$
then the same quintuple contributes $\mathrm{i}^{jk}$ to $S_k$. Thus the discrete Fourier inversion on the cyclic group of order $4$ gives
$$
N_j=\frac14\sum_{k=0}^{3}\mathrm{i}^{-jk}S_k.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
(N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)
=\frac14\left(
S_0+S_1+S_2+S_3,\ 
S_0-\mathrm{i}S_1-S_2+\mathrm{i}S_3,\ 
S_0-S_1+S_2-S_3,\ 
S_0+\mathrm{i}S_1-S_2-\mathrm{i}S_3
\right).
$$
Since $p\equiv1\pmod8$, we have $\chi(-1)=1$, so conjugation of the character gives
$$
S_3=\overline{S_1}.
$$

Step 2:
Evaluate the unrestricted nonzero sums before imposing the signed-distinct condition. Let
$$
U_k=\sum_{\substack{x_1+\cdots+x_5=1\\ x_i\in\mathbb F_p^\times}}\chi^k(x_1x_2x_3x_4x_5).
$$
For $k=0$, inclusion-exclusion over the zero coordinates gives
$$
U_0=p^4-5p^3+10p^2-10p+5.
$$
For $k=1$, the five-fold Jacobi sum with five copies of $\chi$ has total character $\chi^5=\chi$. We use the following precise Jacobi-sum identity: if $\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_t$ are nontrivial multiplicative characters and $\psi_1\cdots\psi_t$ is nontrivial, then
$$
\sum_{\substack{y_1+\cdots+y_t=1\\ y_i\in\mathbb F_p^\times}}
\psi_1(y_1)\cdots\psi_t(y_t)
=\frac{g(\psi_1)\cdots g(\psi_t)}{g(\psi_1\cdots\psi_t)}.
$$
Here $g(\psi)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb F_p^\times}\psi(u)e(u)$ for a fixed nontrivial additive character $e$. To justify the identity, multiply the left side by $g(\psi_1\cdots\psi_t)$ and expand:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&g(\psi_1\cdots\psi_t)
\sum_{\substack{y_1+\cdots+y_t=1\\ y_i\neq0}}
\psi_1(y_1)\cdots\psi_t(y_t)\\
&=\sum_{a\neq0}\sum_{\substack{y_1+\cdots+y_t=1\\ y_i\neq0}}
(\psi_1\cdots\psi_t)(a)\psi_1(y_1)\cdots\psi_t(y_t)e(a)\\
&=\sum_{\substack{x_1+\cdots+x_t=a\\ a\neq0,\ x_i\neq0}}
\psi_1(x_1)\cdots\psi_t(x_t)e(a)\\
&=\sum_{x_1,\ldots,x_t\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
\psi_1(x_1)\cdots\psi_t(x_t)e(x_1+\cdots+x_t)
=\prod_{\nu=1}^{t}g(\psi_\nu),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $x_\nu=ay_\nu$. Dividing by $g(\psi_1\cdots\psi_t)$ proves the identity.
It applies here with $t=5$ and all $\psi_i=\chi$, so
$$
U_1=\frac{g(\chi)^5}{g(\chi)}=g(\chi)^4.
$$
The same identity with $t=2$, $\psi_1=\psi_2=\chi$, gives the quartic Jacobi sum relation
$$
J=\frac{g(\chi)^2}{g(\chi^2)}.
$$
Also $g(\chi^2)^2=p$, because $\chi^2$ is the quadratic character and $p\equiv1\pmod4$. Therefore
$$
U_1=g(\chi)^4=J^2g(\chi^2)^2=pJ^2.
$$
Similarly,
$$
U_2=g(\chi^2)^4=p^2,
$$
and
$$
U_3=p\overline J^{\,2}.
$$

Step 3:
Impose the condition $x_a\neq \pm x_b$ by inclusion-exclusion over signed collision edges. A signed edge $(a,b,+)$ means $x_a=x_b$, and a signed edge $(a,b,-)$ means $x_a=-x_b$. It is cleaner to sum over the signed partition lattice generated by these edges. For a signed block $B$, choose the smallest index in $B$ to have sign $+1$, write $b=|B|$, and write
$$
c(B)=\left|\sum_{i\in B}\varepsilon_i\right|.
$$
For a fixed signed partition $\Pi$, its Mobius coefficient is
$$
\mu(\Pi)=\prod_{B\in\Pi}(-1)^{|B|-1}(|B|-1)!.
$$
If $\Pi$ has block data $(b_1,c_1),\ldots,(b_t,c_t)$, then its contribution to $S_k$ is
$$
\mu(\Pi)\left(\prod_{c_\nu=0}Z_{k b_\nu}\right)
\left(\prod_{c_\nu\neq0}\chi^{-k b_\nu}(c_\nu)\right)
\mathcal J_k(b_\nu:c_\nu\neq0),
$$
where
$$
Z_a=\begin{cases}p-1,&a\equiv0\pmod4,\\0,&a\not\equiv0\pmod4,\end{cases}
$$
and
$$
\mathcal J_k(b_1,\ldots,b_u)=
\sum_{z_1+\cdots+z_u=1}\chi^{k b_1}(z_1)\cdots\chi^{k b_u}(z_u),
$$
with every $z_\nu$ nonzero. Fix a nontrivial additive character $e$ of $\mathbb F_p$. The required Jacobi evaluations are obtained from the identity
$$
\mathcal J_k(b_1,\ldots,b_u)
=\frac1p\sum_{v\in\mathbb F_p}e(-v)
\prod_{\nu=1}^{u}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb F_p^\times}\chi^{k b_\nu}(z)e(vz)\right).
$$
For $v\neq0$, the inner sum is $\chi^{-k b_\nu}(v)g(\chi^{k b_\nu})$ when $\chi^{k b_\nu}$ is nontrivial, and it is $-1$ when $\chi^{k b_\nu}$ is trivial. The $v=0$ term is present only when every involved character is trivial. In that all-trivial case, the same identity gives
$$
\mathcal J_k(b_1,\ldots,b_u)=\frac{(p-1)^u-(-1)^u}{p}.
$$
The identities
$$
g(\chi)^2=Jg(\chi^2),\qquad g(\chi^2)^2=p,
$$
and conjugation reduce every term to $p,J,\overline J,\chi(2),\chi(3),\chi(5)$.

The following ledger is the signed-partition computation. A row $((b_1,c_1),\ldots,(b_t,c_t))$ means that the signed blocks have those sizes and absolute signed sums. The number in the second column is the aggregate of $\mu(\Pi)$ over all labelled signed partitions with that block data.
For a single block, put
$$
a(b,c)=\#\{(\varepsilon_2,\ldots,\varepsilon_b)\in\{1,-1\}^{b-1}: |1+\varepsilon_2+\cdots+\varepsilon_b|=c\}.
$$
The only values needed for five variables are
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
b&1&2&3&4&5\\ \hline
a(b,0)&0&1&0&3&0\\
a(b,1)&1&0&3&0&10\\
a(b,2)&0&1&0&4&0\\
a(b,3)&0&0&1&0&5\\
a(b,4)&0&0&0&1&0\\
a(b,5)&0&0&0&0&1.
\end{array}
$$
For example, when $b=5$ the number of plus signs among $\varepsilon_2,\ldots,\varepsilon_5$ may be $0,1,2,3,4$, giving signed sums $-3,-1,1,3,5$ with multiplicities $1,4,6,4,1$. This gives $a(5,1)=10$, $a(5,3)=5$, and $a(5,5)=1$.

If a type has $n_{b,c}$ blocks of kind $(b,c)$, then the aggregate coefficient in the table is
$$
\frac{5!}{\prod_{b,c}(b!)^{n_{b,c}}n_{b,c}!}
\prod_{b,c}a(b,c)^{n_{b,c}}
\prod_{b,c}\left((-1)^{b-1}(b-1)!\right)^{n_{b,c}}.
$$
The first factor chooses the labelled blocks, the second factor chooses the allowed sign patterns inside those blocks, and the third factor is the ordinary partition-lattice Mobius value on each block. This gives, for instance,
$$
W((2,2),(3,1))
=\frac{5!}{2!\,3!}\,a(2,2)a(3,1)\,(-1)(2)
=10\cdot1\cdot3\cdot(-1)\cdot2=-60.
$$

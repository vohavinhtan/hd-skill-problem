# Solution

## Steps

Step 1: Convert the family conditions into a quadratic-space problem

Identify $A\subseteq X_m$ with $v_A=(x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_m,y_m)\in V:=\mathbb F_2^{2m}$, and set
$$
Q(v)=\sum_{i=1}^m x_i y_i,
\qquad
B(v,w)=Q(v+w)+Q(v)+Q(w).
$$
Then $Q(v_A)=p(A)$ and $B(v_A,v_B)=c(A,B)$. Moreover, $Q$ is the orthogonal sum of $m$ hyperbolic planes. For $e=(1,\ldots,1)$,
$$
B(e,v)=\sum_i(x_i+y_i),\qquad
U:=e^\perp,\qquad
\operatorname{rad}(B|_U)=\langle e\rangle,\qquad Q(e)=m\pmod2.
$$

Let $n_t=|\kappa^{-1}(t)|$. On $E=\mathbb F_2^{n_0}\oplus\mathbb F_2^{n_1}$ define
$$
q=q_{n_0}\perp q_{n_1},\qquad
q_n(z)=\sum_{i<j}z_i z_j.
$$
Sending the coordinate vectors to the corresponding $v_A$ gives a linear map $\phi:E\to U$ with $Q(\phi z)=q(z)$. The polar matrix of $q_n$ is $G_n=J_n+I_n$.

Step 2: Obtain the upper bounds and exclude the exceptional rank case

If $s=\sum_i z_i$, then $(G_nz)_i=z_i+s$. Hence
$$
\operatorname{rank}G_n=
\begin{cases}n,&n\text{ even},\\ n-1,&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}
$$
and for odd $n$ its radical is generated by $j_n=(1,\ldots,1)$, with
$$
q_n(j_n)=\frac{n-1}{2}\pmod2.
$$
Since $\operatorname{rank}(B|_U)=2m-2$,
$$
n_0+n_1\leq
\begin{cases}
2m,&n_0,n_1\text{ odd},\\
2m-1,&\text{exactly one is odd},\\
2m-2,&n_0,n_1\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
$$

If equality $n_0+n_1=2m$ holds, the polar radical of $q$ is
$$
R=\langle j_{n_0},j_{n_1}\rangle,\qquad \ker\phi\subseteq R.
$$
For even $m$, $q|_R$ cannot vanish, since that would give $n_0\equiv n_1\equiv1\pmod4$ but $n_0+n_1=2m\equiv0\pmod4$. Dimension forces $\ker\phi\neq0$, while $Q(\phi z)=q(z)$ forces $q$ to vanish on the kernel. Thus $\ker\phi$ is the unique singular line in $R$. Hence
$$
\dim\phi(E)=2m-1=\dim U,
$$
so $\phi(E)=U$. The remaining radical class has value $1$ and must map to the generator $e$ of $\operatorname{rad}(B|_U)$, contradicting $Q(e)=0$. Therefore size $2m$ requires odd $m$.

It remains to exclude $2m-1$ when $m\equiv0\pmod4$. Write the odd block as $2r+1$ and the even block as $2s$, so $r+s=m-1$. Since $\ker\phi$ is either $0$ or $\langle j_{2r+1}\rangle$, preservation of $Q$ gives $q(j_{2r+1})=r=0$ in both cases: if the kernel is zero, $\phi$ maps this radical generator to $e$. Thus $r$ is even.

Define $\epsilon(a)=0$ for $a\equiv0,1\pmod4$ and $\epsilon(a)=1$ for $a\equiv2,3\pmod4$. For a nondegenerate $2a$-space with symplectic basis $(x_i,y_i)$, put
$$
\operatorname{Arf}(Q)=\sum_iQ(x_i)Q(y_i)\pmod2.
$$
On each symplectic plane,
$$
\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb F_2}(-1)^{Q(\alpha x_i+\beta y_i)}
=2(-1)^{Q(x_i)Q(y_i)}.
$$
Multiplying over the planes proves
$$
\sum_z(-1)^{Q(z)}=2^a(-1)^{\operatorname{Arf}(Q)},
$$
which also makes the definition basis independent and shows that Arf invariants add under orthogonal sums.

For $q_N$, a vector of weight $k$ has value $\binom{k}{2}$, whose signs by $k\pmod4$ are $+,+,-,-$. Therefore
$$
\sum_z(-1)^{q_N(z)}=\operatorname{Re}(1+i)^N+\operatorname{Im}(1+i)^N.
$$
For $N=2a$ this is $2^a(-1)^{\epsilon(a)}$, so $\operatorname{Arf}(q_{2a})=\epsilon(a)$. For $N=2r+1$ with even $r$, the sum is $2^{r+1}(-1)^{\epsilon(r)}$; since $q_{2r+1}$ is constant on the two-element cosets of $\langle j_{2r+1}\rangle$, its quotient has Arf invariant $\epsilon(r)$. Hence the relevant source has invariant
$$
\epsilon(r)+\epsilon(s)=1,
$$
because $r+s\equiv3\pmod4$ and $r$ is even.

There are two kernel cases. If $\ker\phi=\langle j_{2r+1}\rangle$, then $W=\phi(E)$ is nondegenerate of dimension $2m-2$ and Arf invariant $1$. Yet $W^\perp$ is a nondegenerate plane containing the singular vector $e$. Choose $f\in W^\perp$ with $B(e,f)=1$ and replace $f$ by $f+e$ when necessary; then $Q(f)=0$, so $W^\perp$ is hyperbolic. Since $V$ is plus type, $\operatorname{Arf}(W)=0$, a contradiction.

If $\ker\phi=0$, then $\phi(E)=U$ and the induced source quotient has Arf invariant $1$. Let $a_1$ be the singleton incidence vector. The plane $H=\langle e,a_1\rangle$ is hyperbolic because $Q(e)=Q(a_1)=0$ and $B(e,a_1)=1$. The map
$$
H^\perp\longrightarrow U/\langle e\rangle,\qquad x\longmapsto x+\langle e\rangle,
$$
is an isometry: it is injective since $e\notin H^\perp$, bijective by dimension, and $Q$ is constant on the cosets. Thus the target quotient has Arf invariant $0$, again a contradiction. Consequently
$$
|\mathcal F|\leq2m-2\qquad(m\equiv0\pmod4).
$$

Step 3: Construct families meeting the upper bounds

We need one elementary classification fact. Let a quadratic space $S$ have polar radical $R$ and $Q|_R\neq0$. Write $R=R_0\oplus\langle t\rangle$ with $Q(t)=1$, and lift a symplectic basis $(x_i,y_i)$ of $S/R$. Their span intersects $R$ trivially and is nondegenerate, because a vector orthogonal to all the lifts has zero class in the nondegenerate quotient. Replacing
$$
x_i\mapsto x_i+Q(x_i)t,\qquad y_i\mapsto y_i+Q(y_i)t
$$
makes all lifted basis vectors singular without changing their pairings. Hence dimension and the quadratic restriction on $R$ determine $S$ up to isometry.

If $Q|_R=0$, factor out $R$. A symplectic pair of values $(0,1)$ becomes hyperbolic after replacing the nonsingular vector by the sum of the pair. Two anisotropic pairs become two hyperbolic pairs through
$$
x_1+x_2,\quad y_1+x_2,\quad x_1+y_1+y_2,\quad x_1+y_1+x_2+y_2.
$$
Substitution shows that these four vectors are singular and have only the first-second and third-fourth pairings nonzero. Thus a nondegenerate space is determined by its dimension and Arf invariant.

For odd $m$, take $(n_0,n_1)=(3,2m-3)$. Both radical generators have value $1$, while their sum is singular. After quotienting by that sum, the source and $U$ both have dimension $2m-1$ and a one-dimensional radical on which the quadratic value is $1$; the preceding basis construction gives an isometry. The coordinate classes stay distinct because the factored vector has weight $2m$. This attains $2m$.

For $m\equiv2\pmod4$, take $(n_0,n_1)=(5,2m-6)$ and quotient by $j_5$. The source is nondegenerate of dimension $2m-2$ and has Arf invariant
$$
\epsilon(2)+\epsilon(m-3)=1+1=0.
$$
The target $U/\langle e\rangle$ is isometric to $H^\perp$ for the hyperbolic plane $H=\langle e,a_1\rangle$, so it has the same dimension and Arf invariant. An isometry followed by any linear section into $U$ preserves $Q$ and $B$, which are constant on $e$-cosets. This attains $2m-1$.

For $m\equiv0\pmod4$, take $(n_0,n_1)=(3,2m-5)$ and set $h=a_1+b_1$, $R=\langle e,h\rangle$. Here $B(e,h)=0$ and
$$
Q(e)=0,\qquad Q(h)=Q(e+h)=1.
$$
Since $R\subseteq R^\perp$ and $B$ is nondegenerate on $V$,
$$
\operatorname{rad}(B|_{R^\perp})
=R^\perp\cap(R^\perp)^\perp=R.
$$
The source radical $\langle j_3,j_{2m-5}\rangle$ has values $1,1,0$ on its two generators and their sum. Map these three vectors to $h,e+h,e$, respectively. The source $E$ and target $R^\perp$ have the same dimension and identical radical restrictions, so the proved basis construction gives an isometry. This attains $2m-2$.

Step 4: Combine the bounds and constructions

The three residue classes of $m$ give
$$
\max|\mathcal F|=
\begin{cases}
2m,&m\text{ odd},\\
2m-1,&m\equiv2\pmod4,\\
2m-2,&m\equiv0\pmod4.
\end{cases}
$$
Using the indicator notation from the problem converts these cases into one expression.

Final Answer: $\boxed{2m-2+2\mathbf 1_{2\nmid m}+\mathbf 1_{m\equiv2\pmod4}}$
